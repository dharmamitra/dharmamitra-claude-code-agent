# Translation Brief — Pātañjalayogaśāstra 4.22–4.34 (Kaivalyapāda, close)

**Work**: Pātañjalayogaśāstra = Yogasūtra + Vyāsabhāṣya (treated as one work, per Maas).
**Edition**: GRETIL text input/proofread by Philipp A. Maas, based on Kāśinātha Śāstrī
Āgāśe, *Pātañjalayogasūtrāṇi…* (Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series 47), Pune 1904.
**Segment**: 4.22–4.34, the closing thirteen sūtras of the Kaivalyapāda (4.34 ends the work).
**Target language**: English.
**Register**: fluent scholarly English prose — readable, accurate, compounds resolved,
key technical terms glossed in parentheses on first occurrence (not on every recurrence),
no hyper-literal compound preservation. Suitable for an academic reader who knows the
tradition but wants the argument to flow.

## Style instruction (verbatim for cat-translate)
> Fluent scholarly English prose. Resolve Sanskrit compounds into clear English; on the
> first occurrence of a core technical term give the Sanskrit in parentheses (e.g.
> "the power of consciousness (citiśakti)"), then use the English thereafter. Keep
> sūtras terse and slightly elevated; render the bhāṣya as flowing expository prose.
> Mark sūtra vs. commentary. Do not omit the embedded verse quotations. Preserve the
> dialectical structure (objection / reply). No padding, no devotional flourish.

## Core terminology (fix these renderings)
- `puruṣa` → "the Self (puruṣa)" / "conscious self"; never "soul" loosely.
- `citiśakti` → "the power of consciousness (citiśakti)".
- `citta` → "mind (citta)"; `manas` → "mind-organ (manas)" where contrasted.
- `buddhi` / `buddhivṛtti` → "intellect (buddhi)" / "operation of the intellect".
- `jñāna` → "cognition" / "knowledge" by context; `jñānavṛtti` → "cognitive operation".
- `guṇa` → "constituent (guṇa)" (the three: sattva, rajas, tamas), not "quality" loosely.
- `pariṇāma` → "transformation"; `pariṇāmakrama` → "sequence of transformation".
- `kṣaṇa` → "moment"; `krama` → "sequence/succession".
- `vāsanā` → "latent trace (vāsanā)"; `saṃskāra` → "latent impression (saṃskāra)".
- `kleśa` → "affliction (kleśa)"; `karmāśaya` → "karmic residue".
- `vivekakhyāti` → "discriminative discernment"; `vivekaja-jñāna` → "discrimination-born knowledge".
- `dharmamegha samādhi` → "the cloud-of-dharma concentration (dharmamegha-samādhi)" — keep the name.
- `prasaṃkhyāna` → "meditative enumeration / higher reflection (prasaṃkhyāna)".
- `kaivalya` → "isolation (kaivalya)" — the standard rendering for final liberation here.
- `svarūpapratiṣṭhā` → "abiding in its own nature".
- `apavarga` → "release"; `bhoga` → "experience/enjoyment".
- `kūṭasthanityatā` → "immutable eternality"; `pariṇāminityatā` → "eternality-in-transformation".
- `sarvārtha` (4.23) → "(the mind that) takes all objects / is all-purposed" — note ambiguity, flag in apparatus.
- `pratibimba` → "reflection".
- `aparānta` → "the far end / terminus".
- `samūlaghāta` / `samūlakāṣa` → "uprooted, root and all".

## Named entities / schools
- `vaināśika` → "the Annihilationists (vaināśika)" = the Buddhists (esp. their no-self,
  momentariness, and cittamātra positions). This is polemical; keep the bite.
- `sāṃkhyayogādayaḥ` → "the Sāṃkhyas, Yogins, and the like".
- `brāhmaṇa` (4.29), `vidvān` (4.30) → "the brahmin", "the wise one" (the adept).
- The momentariness/vijñānavāda opponent in 4.21–4.24 is Buddhist; 4.23's *cittamātra*
  ("mind-only") view is Yogācāra.

## Embedded verse quotations (do NOT prose-flatten; render as verse, flag in apparatus)
1. **4.22**: "na pātālaṃ na ca vivaraṃ girīṇāṃ…" — brahman hidden in the cave of
   undifferentiated intellect-operation. Source to be traced in apparatus.
2. **4.31**: "andho maṇim avidhyat…" — the blind/fingerless/neckless/tongueless man
   stringing and wearing a jewel (a riddle-verse on cognition without the ordinary
   instruments). Trace source.
3. (4.33 has prose dialogue with the "oṃ bhoḥ" exclamation — keep as direct speech.)

## Doctrinal cruxes to render carefully
- 4.22: the intellect's operation, though unconscious, *appears* conscious by borrowing
  the reflected light of puruṣa → "self-cognition of one's own intellect (svabuddhisaṃvedana)".
- 4.23: the crystal (sphaṭikamaṇi) simile — mind coloured by both seer and seen.
- 4.24: the "house" (gṛha) analogy — aggregate acts for another, not itself; refutation
  of the vaināśika "mere generality (sāmānyamātra)".
- 4.33: the catuṣkoṭi-style taxonomy of questions (ekānta- / vibhajya- / avacanīya-
  vacanīya) — render the logical typology precisely; this echoes Buddhist
  question-classification (cf. the avyākṛta questions).
