### Paṭala 1 [Maṇḍalāvatārapaṭala — Chapter on Entering the Maṇḍala] — vv. 1.1–1.9

**Source:**
```
1.1ab: athāto rahasyaṃ vakṣye samāsan na tu vistarāt
1.1cd: śrīherukasaṃyogaṃ sarvakāmārthasādhakam
1.2ab: uttarād api cottaraṃ ḍākinījālasaṃvaram
1.2cd: rahasye parame ramye sarvātmani sadā sthitaḥ
1.3ab: sarvaḍākinīmayaḥ sattvo vajrasattvaḥ paraṃ sukham
1.3cd: asau hi svayambhūr bhagavān vīro ḍākinījālasaṃvaram
1.4ab: saṃbhavān nādarūpād viniṣkrāntāḥ samayācāragocarāḥ
1.4cd: durlabhaṃ triṣu lokeṣu ādimadhyāntasaṃsthitam
1.5ab: manthamanthānasaṃyogaṃ yathā tathā mantrajāpadhyānādibhir yuktam
1.5cd: yogaṃ caiva vidhijñānaṃ tantre nigāditaṃ śṛṇu
1.6ab: madhyamottamaśvāsena gandhodakasahitena tu
1.6cd: kulikāṃ pūjayen nityaṃ kālaviśeṣeṇa tu dūtikāḥ
1.7ab: dūtayaḥ sahajāḥ siddhā adhamottamamadhyamāḥ
1.7cd: antargatena manasā kāmasiddhiṃ tu bhāvayet
1.8ab: svaretobidubhir buddhān bodhisattvāṃc ca pūjayet
1.8cd: darśanasparśanābhyāṃ ca śravaṇasmareṇa ca
1.9ab: mucyate sarvapāpais tu evam eva na saṃśayaḥ
```

**Translation:**

Now, I shall declare the secret, briefly and not at length, the Śrīherukasaṃyoga [Union with Glorious Heruka], which accomplishes the purpose of all desires. It is the most supreme of all that is supreme, the Ḍākinījālasaṃvara [Binding of the Web of Ḍākinīs]. He who is eternally established in the supreme, delightful secret, the one who is the nature of all — Vajrasattva, the being who is the essence of all ḍākinīs — is supreme bliss. He is the self-originated, blessed, heroic Lord, the Ḍākinījālasaṃvara. Emerging from the state of the nāda [primordial sound], the range of the samayācāra [observance of the pledge] appears.

It is difficult to obtain in the three worlds, existing in the beginning, middle, and end. Just as the union of the churner and the churned is described, so too is it endowed with the recitation of mantra [sacred formula], meditation, and the rest. Listen also to the yoga and the knowledge of the rituals as declared in the tantra. With middling and excellent breath, together with scented water, one should daily worship the kulikā [lineage deity], and according to the specific time, the dūtikāḥ [female messengers/consorts]. The dūtayaḥ [messengers] are of the innate (sahaja), the attained (siddhā), and are of low, excellent, or middling status. With an inward-turned mind, one should contemplate the kāmasiddhi [attainment of desire]. Through the drops of one's own semen (svaretobindu), one should worship the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. Through seeing, touching, hearing, and remembering, one is truly liberated from all sins; of this, there is no doubt.

### Paṭala 1 (cont.) — vv. 1.10–1.15cd (Sarnath ed.) + 1.15ab–cd (Sanderson ed.)

**Source:**
```
1.9cd: yogitvaṃ paramaṃ puṇyaṃ pavitraṃ pāpanāśanam
1.10ab: siddhyate japamantreṇa jāpena dhyāyena ca
1.10cd: samayān pālayen nityaṃ sādhakaḥ susamāhitaḥ
1.11ab: bhedena samayānāṃ tu na dīkṣāmaṇḍale siddhir avāpyate
1.11cd: madhu raktaṃ sakarpūraṃ raktacandanayojitam
1.12ab: gaṇamadhye pratiṣṭhaṃ tu sarvavajrāṅkacihnadhṛk
1.12cd: anāmāṅguṣṭhavakrābhyāṃ lehayed yogavit sadā
1.13ab: somapānavad āsvādya siddhim āpnoti śāśvatīm
1.13cd: pañcāmṛtaṃ bhaved atra sarvasiddhipravartakam
1.14ab: catuṣpūjā tathā vīrā manasopsitayoginyaḥ
1.14cd: vīrādvayasamāpatti samāpannaṃ ca tattvadhṛk
1.15ab: divyamānuṣyatāsaukhyaṃ piṇḍīkṛtya tad eva ca
1.15cd: tad eva vajrakaṇikayā kalāṃ nārghanti ṣoḍaśīm
[Sanderson ed., Baroda MS:]
1.15ab: girigahvarakuñjeṣu mahodadhitateṣu vā
1.15cd: ādisiddhaśmaśāne ca tatra maṇḍalam ālikhet
```

**Translation:**

Yogic practice is supreme, meritorious, sacred, and the destroyer of all sins; it is accomplished through the recitation of mantras (japamantra), meditative recitation (jāpa), and contemplation (dhyāna). A focused sādhaka (ritual practitioner) must always observe the samayas (pledges). Indeed, if there is a breach in the samayas, the siddhi (attainment) cannot be realized within the dīkṣāmaṇḍala (initiation ritual diagram).

One should always take the nectar — consisting of honey, blood, and camphor, mixed with red sandalwood — while established in the midst of the gaṇa (assembly) and bearing the marks of all the vajras (adamantine symbols). With the ring finger and thumb, the one who knows yoga should always lick this [substance]. Having tasted it as if it were ambrosia (somapāna), he attains everlasting siddhi. Here, the pañcāmṛta (five nectars) becomes the catalyst for all attainments.

Likewise, there is the fourfold worship (catuṣpūjā), the vīras (male adepts), and the yoginīs (female adepts) desired in the mind. The one who holds the essence (tattvadhṛk) becomes fully endowed with the union of the vīra and the consort (vīrādvayasamāpatti). Having gathered together the bliss of the divine and the human, even that attainment is not worth a sixteenth part of a single vajra-atom (vajrakaṇikā). In mountain caverns, groves, or on the shores of great oceans, or in an ādisiddha-śmaśāna (primordial charnel ground), one should there draw the maṇḍala (ritual diagram).

[End of Chapter 1: Maṇḍalāvatārapaṭala — Chapter on Entering the Maṇḍala]

### Paṭala 2 [Cakrapūjāvidhi — Procedure for Worship of the Wheel/Maṇḍala] — vv. 2.1–2.11

**Source:**
```
2.1ab: tatra pānagomayena maṇḍalabhūmi pralepayet
2.1cd: śmaśānabhasmanā yuktaṃ pañcāmṛtasamanvitam
2.2ab: upalipya tato bhūmiṃ tatra maṇḍalam ārabhet
2.2c: śmaśānan tu samācaret
2.3ab: cityaṅgāracūrṇena śmaśāneṣṭakasaṃyuktam
2.3cd: ālikhen maṇḍalaṃ divyaṃ ācāryaḥ susalakṣaṇaḥ
2.4ab: samyagjñānatantrajñaḥ śrīherukama[ntrajñaḥ]
2.4cd: akrodhaś ca śucir dakṣo yogajño jñānapāragaḥ
2.5ab: kapālakṛtamūrdhajaḥ
2.5cd: bhasmānuliptāṅgaḥ sambhavān mātrair vibhūṣitagātraḥ
2.6ab: asthimālāsaṃsthitaś ca ekakhaṇḍīkṛtamūrdhajaḥ
2.6cd: asthimālāvalambī khaṭvāṅgakarasaṃsthitaḥ
2.7ab: ātmānaṃ śrīherukaṃ kṛtvā śrīherukatvaṃ tataḥ
2.7cd: smarec cakraṃ asya hṛdi nyaset
2.8ab: evaṃ saṃnahya-m-ātmānaṃ prākāraṃ ca diśo nyaset
2.8cd: evaṃ saṃnahya-m-ātmānaṃ adho hy astraṃ viniveśayet
2.9ab: cakrasya ātmānam ūrdhvaṃ tu śarajālakam
2.9cd: ātmanaḥ khecaraṃ pañjaraṃ kṛtvātmānaṃ susamāhitaḥ
2.10ab: evaṃ saṃnahya sakavaco 'bhedyas tridaśair api
2.10cd: evaṃ surakṣya-m-ātmānaṃ mudrāmantrair alaṅkṛtam
2.11ab: ālikhen maṇḍalaṃ ghoraṃ mahāsiddhipradāyakam
```

**Translation:**

There, one should smear the ground of the maṇḍala with cow dung. Having mixed it with cemetery ash and the pañcāmṛta (five nectars), one should smear the ground and then begin the maṇḍala. One should conduct it in a cemetery. Using the powder of charcoal from a funeral pyre combined with cemetery bricks, a qualified ācārya (ritual master) who is well-versed in the tantras of true knowledge and knows the mantra of Śrī Heruka should draw the divine maṇḍala. He should be devoid of anger, pure, capable, a master of yoga, and thoroughly accomplished in wisdom.

With his hair fashioned into a skull-cup (kapāla-kṛtamūrdhajaḥ), his limbs anointed with ash and adorned with the elements, he should be established with a garland of bones, his hair tied in a single knot, wearing a hanging garland of bones, and holding a khaṭvāṅga (skull-topped staff) in his hand. Having transformed himself into Śrī Heruka, he should then contemplate Śrī Herukahood. He should place the wheel (cakra) in his heart. Having thus armored himself, he should place the protective enclosure (prākāra) in the directions. Having armored himself in this way, he should place the weapon-mantra (astra) below. He should place a net of arrows above the wheel and his own self. Having made his own self into a sky-going (khecara) cage while remaining fully composed, he is armored in this way, rendered impenetrable even by the gods (tridaśas). Having thus protected himself and adorned with mudrās (ritual gestures) and mantras, he should draw the formidable maṇḍala, which bestows the great siddhis.

### Paṭala 2 (cont.) — vv. 2.11cd–2.19

**Source:**
```
2.11cd: tato mṛtakasūtreṇa mahārudhirarañjitena ca
2.12ab: sūtrayen maṇḍalaṃ ghoraṃ herukasya paraṃ puram
2.12cd: ekahastaṃ catur aṣṭaṃ caturasraṃ tu samantataḥ
2.13ab: caturdvārasamākīrṇaṃ catustoraṇabhūṣitam
2.13cd: vicared dviguṇaṃ mantrī japed ḍākinījālaśamvaram
2.14ab: tasya madhye pratiṣṭhāpya sapatraṃ karṇikojvalam
2.14cd: puṣkaraiś ca keśarānvitam
2.15ab: karṇikāyāṃ nyased vīraṃ mahābhairavabhīṣaṇam
2.15cd: tejaskaṃ tu sudīptāṅgaṃ aṭṭāṭṭahāsamahāravam
2.16ab: kapālamālābharaṇaṃ divyaṃ trinetraṃ caturmukham
2.16cd: hasticarmāvaruddhaṃ ca vajrasaṃbhinnasabhruvam
2.17ab: khaṭvāṅgakṛtahastaṃ tu śatamālārdhabhūṣitam
2.17cd: tasyāgrataḥ sthitāṃ devīṃ vajravārāhīṃ sughorām
2.18ab: śrīherukābhimukāṃ kṛtvā tu trimukhīṃ raudrarūpiṇīm
2.18cd: kapālamantrasaṃpūrṇāṃ sravantīṃ rudhiraṃ mukhāt
2.19ab: tarjayantīṃ diśāḥ sarvān sadevāsuramānuṣān
```

**Translation:**

Then, using a thread made from a shroud stained with great blood, the mantrī (officiant) should outline the terrifying maṇḍala, the supreme city of Heruka. It should be one cubit in size, four-sided, eight-petaled, and square on all sides, replete with four gates and adorned with four archways. The mantrī should circumambulate it twice and recite the Ḍākinījāla-śaṃvara.

In its center, he should establish [the lotus], complete with petals, a radiant pericarp, and stamens. Upon the pericarp, he should place the Vīra (Hero), who is terrifying and fierce, radiant with brilliance, with a lustrous body, making a great, roaring, mocking laughter. He is adorned with a garland of skulls, divine, three-eyed, and four-faced. Clad in an elephant hide, his eyebrows are joined by a vajra. He holds a khaṭvāṅga in his hand and is decorated with a half-garland of fifty skulls.

Before him, the goddess Vajravārāhī should be established, terrifying and wrathful, facing Śrī Heruka, three-faced and ferocious in form. She is filled with the mantra of the skull, with blood streaming from her mouth, threatening all the directions, including all gods, asuras, and humans.

### Paṭala 2 (cont.) — vv. 2.19cd–2.31

**Source:**
```
2.19cd: ḍākinyaś ca caturviṃśā vārāhyākulasaṃvarāḥ
2.20ab: cakragarbhe tu pūjayet diśāsu vidiśāsu ca
2.20cd: vīrāṃś caiva tathaiveha cakrasaṃsthā pūjayet
2.21ab: vīram advayaṃ yad icchet siddhi sādhakaḥ
2.21cd: kalaśāṃś ca tataḥ kuryān mūlakālādivarjitān
2.22ab: mauktikair hemaratnaiś ca pravāḍarajatais tāmraiḥ
2.22cd: sarvabhakṣyais tu saṃpūrṇāṃ kapāloparisaṃsthitaḥ
2.23ab: sūtreṇa veṣṭayet tatkaṇṭhaṃ pallavāgrasamanvitān
2.23cd: aṣṭau dvāreṣu viniveśya vastrayugmaiḥ suveṣṭitān
2.24ab: navamam madhyataḥ kalaśaṃ vastrayugmena veṣṭitam
2.24cd: kṛtvā rajataṃ hiraṇyaṃ vā ratnamaukutiśobhitam
2.25ab: vikiren maṇḍale sarvān ratnasauvarṇaśobhanān
2.25cd: pūjayet puravaraṃ ramyaṃ siddhir eva na saṃśayaḥ
2.26ab: gandhokena saṃsicyātmānaṃ sarvatomukham
2.26cd: prāg dīpaṃ śataṃ dadyād yad icchet siddhim uttamām
2.27ab: ākāśe ḍākinyaḥ sarvā manasā ūrdhvato nyaset
2.27cd: bhūrloke ḍākinyo yāś ca maṇḍale sarvato nyaset
2.28ab: pātāle ḍākinyā yā kācit pātāle tajjño vinyaset
2.28cd: diśāsu mātarāḥ sarvā vidiśāsu ca niyojayet
2.29ab: puṣpair gandhais tathā dhūpai dīpānāṃ tu śataṃ dadyād yad icchet siddhi sādhakaḥ
2.29cd: pūjayed vīrayoginyaḥ śrīherukasya maṇḍale sthitān
2.30ab: dhvajai raktais tathā pītai śvetaiḥ kṛṣṇapiṅgalaiḥ
2.30cd: sragdāmair vividhaiś caiva vastrair nānāvidhias tathā
2.31ab: vitānaiś ca kāṇḍapaṭṭakais suśobhanais tathā
2.31cd: bhakṣyabhojyakhādyapānai ca kuryāt susamāhitaḥ
```

**Translation:**

The twenty-four Ḍākinīs are the Ākulasaṃvara of Vārāhī; one should worship them within the maṇḍala's center and in the cardinal and intermediate directions. In this same way, one should worship the Vīras residing in the circle. The sādhaka who desires the non-dual Vīra should attain the siddhi.

Then, one should prepare the kalaśas (ritual water-vessels), excluding those [associated with] the base, time, and so forth. They should be adorned with pearls, gold, jewels, coral, silver, and copper, and filled with all types of offerings. Placed upon skulls, one should bind their necks with thread and adorn them with the tips of sprouts. Having placed eight of them at the gates and wrapped them in pairs of cloth, one should place the ninth kalaśa in the center, wrapped in a pair of cloth, and embellished with silver, gold, and jeweled pearls. One should scatter [offerings] throughout the maṇḍala, resplendent with gems and gold; by worshipping this beautiful, supreme city, there is no doubt that one will attain siddhi.

Having sprinkled oneself with scented water on all sides — if one desires the supreme siddhi, one should offer a hundred lamps at the outset. In the sky, one should mentally place all the Ḍākinīs above; in the earthly realm (bhūrloka), one should place the Ḍākinīs throughout the maṇḍala; whatever Ḍākinīs there are in the underworld (pātāla), the adept should place them there. One should appoint all the Mātaraḥ (Mothers) in the cardinal and intermediate directions. The sādhaka desiring siddhi should offer flowers, scents, incense, and a hundred lamps. One should worship the Vīrayoginīs situated in the maṇḍala of Śrī Heruka with red, yellow, white, and black-tawny banners, with various garlands, with garments of many types, and with beautiful canopies (vitāna) and decorative friezes. Being perfectly composed, one should offer consumable and edible foods and beverages.

[End of Chapter 2: Cakrapūjāvidhipaṭala — Procedure for Worship of the Cakra]
