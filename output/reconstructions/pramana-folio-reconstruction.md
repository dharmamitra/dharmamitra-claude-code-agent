# Philological Reconstruction — Pramāṇa Folio (8 lacunae)

**Classification:** Pramāṇa school (Dignāga/Dharmakīrti tradition); likely Dharmakīrti *Pramāṇaviniścaya* II
or a sub-commentary (Dharmottara / ṭīkā on PVA); bhāṣya prose, pūrvapakṣa–uttarapakṣa dialectic.

**Folio condition:** Palm-leaf damaged on right margin; each line loses approximately 50+ akṣaras at the
end. Reconstructions propose only the 8–15 akṣaras at the very start of each gap; the remainder is
marked `[…]`.

## LACUNA NOTES

---

### LACUNA 1
**Position:** Line 1, after `darśanayogyatā`

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~55

**Syntactic slot:** The term `darśanayogyatā` has just been given as the answer to the question `keyaṃ dṛśyatā?`. The gap follows immediately. A parallel definition in the Mīmāṃsā context (Śāstradīpikā SA_GSP28_sastrdiu:3143) continues: *tasyāmavasthāyāṃ nāstīti tena rūpeṇābhāvāt dṛśyaṃ nāstīty ucyate*. The text must immediately elaborate on what the absence of *darśanayogyatā* entails. Grammatically the gap begins with a locative-predication phrase.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `tasyāmavasthāyāṃ nāstīti`

**Confidence:** ◆ Attested

**Rationale:** The phrase is verbatim in SA_GSP28_sastrdiu:3143 as the direct continuation of the definition `dṛśyatā darśanayogyatā`. Prajñākaragupta SA_T11_prpva:1688 and the Tibetan parallel BO_T11_D4225:153a-20 both deploy the same definitional formula in the identical dialectical context (answering the pūrvapakṣin's question about what *dṛśyatā* amounts to). The Jñānaśrīmitra passage SA_T11_jnaniva:5566 (`na darśanayogyatā vigalati`) confirms the term is alive in this exact pramāṇa discourse. The continuation beyond the proposed opening (`tena rūpeṇābhāvāt dṛśyaṃ nāstīty ucyate`) is also confirmed by SA_GSP28_sastrdiu:3143 but is relegated to `[…]` because the folio's wording cannot be assumed verbatim.

### LACUNA 2
**Position:** Line 2, after `kapālakāle ghaṭasya kenacid anupalabdhatvād`

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~53

**Syntactic slot:** The sentence is a conditional: *atha kapālakāle ghaṭasya kenacid anupalabdhatvāt …*. The grammatical expectation is an ablative-governed result clause. The dialectical move is the pūrvapakṣin trying to ground *asattva* of the pot at the sherd-moment in someone's non-apprehension of it. The next surviving line begins `d eva`, showing the debate continues.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `asattvam eva sidhyati,`

**Confidence:** ◇ Probable

**Rationale:** The phrase `asattvam eva sidhyati` (or close variant `asattvam eva sādhyam`) is the natural conclusion of the ablative chain `anupalabdhatvāt` in this dialectical context. The Nyāyamañjarī SA_GSP29_nyamanu:10673 (`kapālakāle 'pi sarveṣām iti kā pramā`) shows the opponent is pressing the same *asattva*-from-non-perception inference. Dharmakīrti's own *Nyāyabindu* tradition (SA_T11_dhkpvsvu:3631, SA_T11_dhknyayu:121–130) deploys `asatsv api tulyatvāt` to rebut exactly this slide from anupalabdhi to asattva. The Dharmottarapradīpa passage (SA_GSP28_durdhpru:9158: `dṛśyatvam abhyupagamya dṛśyānupalabdher eva niṣedhaḥ`) shows the Buddhist response that the opponent is confusing anupalabdhi with asattva. The proposed opening reflects what the folio would present before the uttarapakṣa response.

### LACUNA 3
**Position:** Line 3, after `tathātrāpi tulya[jātīya]`

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~50

**Syntactic slot:** The argument is that just as a general type (*tajjātīya*) is inferred from smoke, not the specific prior fire, so too in the parallel case the argument fails to establish the same-stream identity. The surviving text has `tathātrāpi tulya[jātīya]` — the nominative *s* ending is expected. The gap must complete the sentence and then continue with the inference's application to the cross-stream (*santānāntara*) problem.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `s tādṛk santānāntare 'numīyate,`

**Confidence:** ◇ Probable

**Rationale:** The Ratnakīrti *Santānāntarasiddhi* passage SA_T11_bsa075_u:5531 (`yadi hi santānāntaraṃ saṃbhavet tadā tato bhedena svasantānasyāvaśyaṃ bhavitavyam`) shows that `santānāntara` is the immediately expected target of the *tathātrāpi* comparison. The text just argued that *aparadhūmāt* one infers *tajjātīya*, not the prior individual fire — the parallel application is that from another stream's states one infers a same-kind entity, not identity across streams. The form `santānāntare 'numīyate` matches both the ablative structure and Dharmakīrti's standard vocabulary for this move (cf. SA_T11_samtasi:21). Confidence is ◇ rather than ◆ because no verbatim match for the whole phrase is attested.

### LACUNA 4
**Position:** Line 4, after `pūrvadṛṣṭasya ghaṭa`

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~55

**Syntactic slot:** Genitive `pūrvadṛṣṭasya ghaṭa[sya]` (the `sya` ending may be at the lacuna's start). The argument being made is that the *punarupalambha* chain cannot establish *tādātmya* by perception, as was already refuted; inference is equally blocked. What follows should be an ablative or instrumental predication stating this epistemic failure.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `syānumānāt tattvaṃ na gṛhyate|`

**Confidence:** ◇ Probable

**Rationale:** The preceding clause (line 3) establishes that *pratyakṣa* cannot establish cross-stream identity (`parasantānānubhūtadarśanasya parokṣatvāt`); the parallel move for *anumāna* was just given (`nāpy anumānāt svaparadṛṣṭasya tattvaṃ sidhyati, tasya sāmānyaviṣayatvāt`). Here the same conclusion is applied to the *punarupalambha* case: the identity (*tattvaṃ*) of the previously-seen pot is not grasped even via inference. SA_T11_jnaniva:11084 (`pratibhāsabheda ityādi deśakālābhyāṃ bhedollekhaḥ`) shows Jñānaśrīmitra rehearsing exactly this temporal-difference argument against *tattva*-cognition in the *punarupalambha* context. The phrase `tattvaṃ na gṛhyate` is a standard closure formula in the *pramāṇa* literature for this kind of epistemic block.

### LACUNA 5
**Position:** Line 5, after `tatkālādīnāṃ [ma/sa]`

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~55

**Syntactic slot:** The word `tatkālādīnāṃ` is genitive plural, with the next akṣara uncertain (`ma` or `sa`). The argument is that *pūrvatā* (temporal anteriority) cannot be the content of the present cognition because the prior time and its features are accessible only to memory (mānasa), not to present perceptual content. `mānasasyaiva viṣayatayā sthiteḥ` would complete a genitive-absolute construction. Alternatively `sattva-` prefix suggests a compound noun.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `nasy aiva viṣayatayā sthiteḥ|`

**Confidence:** ○ Speculative

**Rationale:** No verbatim parallel found. The reconstruction is driven entirely by context: the surviving text argues that `pūrvakālāditā` cannot be content of the current cognition of the present object (`svagrāhyasya na gantum īśā`); the expected conclusion grounds this on the restriction that past-time features (`tatkālādīnām`) fall within the scope of memory alone (`mānasa`). The form `mānasyaiva viṣayatayā sthiteḥ` (genitive of reason, `sthiteḥ` = "because [they] abide") is a common bhāṣya construction in Dharmakīrti's school. The uncertain akṣara `[ma/sa]` is resolved here as `ma` (= start of `mānasa`). Marked ○ because the exact wording cannot be verified.

### LACUNA 6
**Position:** Line 6, after `athā` (the sentence's `athā` is the last surviving syllable)

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~51

**Syntactic slot:** The surviving text just argued: `na hi sphuṭam ābhāti punastambhādau pūrvatā` — temporal anteriority does not appear vividly at re-perception of the pillar. The `athā` introduces the pūrvapakṣin's next hypothetical. The expected move is: *even if* the prior-time appearance is faint, the present act still picks it up somehow — introducing a qualification the *uttarapakṣin* will then rebut.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `pi śakto bhaved ity etad api na,`

**Confidence:** ○ Speculative

**Rationale:** No direct parallel located. The reconstruction follows the standard pūrvapakṣa–counter-pūrvapakṣa rhythmics of Dharmakīrti's dialectic. After denying that `pūrvatā` appears vividly (`na sphuṭam`), the pūrvapakṣin naturally retreats to a weak form: *even if faintly capable* (`api śaktaḥ`). The `ity etad api na` opener of the *uttarapakṣa* denial is formulaic in this register (cf. numerous instances in SA_T11_jnaniva). Marked ○.

### LACUNA 7
**Position:** Line 7, after `tatkarmmatā` (preceded by `jñānāntarāvedyatvenāpratibhāsane`)

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~51

**Syntactic slot:** `tatkarmmatā` is nominative feminine. The sentence so far: `pūrvadṛśaś cyutāyā jñānāntarāvedyatvenāpratibhāsane tatkarmmatā` — "the quality of being-a-content-of-that [prior cognition], given its non-appearance as not cognizable by another cognition, …". The gap must complete a logical predication. The expected move is that this property (*tatkarmmatā*) must be established by inference, not perception.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `'numānāt sādhyā, na pratyakṣāt|`

**Confidence:** ◇ Probable

**Rationale:** The Santānāntarasiddhi-tradition argument (SA_T11_bsa075_u:5531, SA_T11_samtasi:21) establishes that qualities of a prior cognition in a past moment are necessarily *parokṣa* and therefore inferential objects, not perceptual. The phrase `anumānāt sādhyā, na pratyakṣāt` is the canonical formulation for inferential-vs-perceptual availability in Dharmakīrti's school (compare SA_T11_dhknyayu:02025 `tādātmya-tadupattī svabhāva-kāryayor eva iti tābhyām eva vastu-siddhiḥ`). Marked ◇ because the immediate textual context (lines about *atijaḍa* and *sthāyitā*) points unambiguously to this conclusion, but the exact wording is not attested verbatim.

### LACUNA 8
**Position:** Line 8, after `na pratyakṣa`

**Estimated total akṣaras in gap:** ~53

**Syntactic slot:** `na pratyakṣa[…]` — the negative prefix and partial word are the last surviving akṣaras. The dialectical context: the interlocutor asked *nanu kiṃ bhinnatvāt* — "what, because of difference?" The text has been arguing that *pūrvadṛṣṭatā* is not grasped in re-perception of the pot. The expected completion of `na pratyakṣa` is either instrumental `pratyakṣeṇa gṛhyate pūrvatā` or a compound `pratyakṣagṛhīta°`.

**Proposed opening (8–15 akṣaras):** `eṇa gṛhyate pūrvatā,`

**Confidence:** ◇ Probable

**Rationale:** The syntactic requirement is clear: `na pratyakṣa-` is the start of either the instrumental construction `pratyakṣeṇa gṛhyate` or a bahuvrīhi. Given that (a) the line immediately before establishes *bhinnatvāt* as the reason given by the interlocutor, and (b) the *uttarapakṣa* would now close by restating that *pūrvatā* is precisely what is *not* accessible to *pratyakṣa* — as argued throughout lines 5–7 — the instrumental `pratyakṣeṇa gṛhyate pūrvatā` is the expected completion. Cf. SA_GSP29_nyabhu:450 (`na pratyakṣeṇa bhedaḥ … gṛhyate`) and SA_T11_kgpramv:13083 (`na ca pratyakṣam evaikaṃ sadasadviṣayam`) for the formula type. Marked ◇ not ◆ because no verbatim match for the precise combination `na pratyakṣeṇa gṛhyate pūrvatā` was found.
