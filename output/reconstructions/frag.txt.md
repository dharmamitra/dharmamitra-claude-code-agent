# Folio Reconstruction: frag.txt

## 1. Classification

**School:** Pramāṇa (Dignāga/Dharmakīrti)

**Opponents:** Mīmāṃsā (primary; *tīrthya* reference, *apūrva* implicit); Sāṃkhya (named explicitly, line 2: *sāṃkhyair abhyupagamāt*); Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika (implicit in *sāmānya/padārtha* arguments)

**Topic:** Epistemological (three interlocking problems):
1. Whether *anupalabdhi* (non-apprehension) of the pot at the sherd-moment (*kapālakāla*) establishes its non-existence, and what *dṛśyatā* (perceptibility) consists in
2. Whether re-identification (*punarupalambha*) of a previously perceived object across temporal moments is possible via *pratyakṣa* or *anumāna*
3. The privacy of perceptual streams across mental continua (*santānāntara*)

**Register:** Bhāṣya prose; sustained pūrvapakṣa–uttarapakṣa dialectic

**Possible identity:** Dharmakīrti, *Pramāṇaviniścaya* II (Pratyakṣa chapter) or a ṭīkā thereon; possibly Dharmottara or a commentary on the *Pramāṇavārttikālaṃkāra*. The *dṛśyatā = darśanayogyatā* definition and the *punarupalambha* / *santānāntara* cluster are characteristic of post-Dharmakīrti scholasticism.

**Technical terms attested:** anupalabdhi, dṛśyānupalabdhi, darśanayogyatā, abhāva, pramāṇa, pratyakṣa, anumāna, sāmānyaviṣayatva, pratibhāsa, pratibhāsabheda, grāhyākāra, parasantāna, svaparasantāna, parokṣatva, punarupalambha, sphuṭarūpa, varttamānatva, pūrvatā, tīrthya, kapālakāla, asattva, vyavahāra

## 4. Translation

The destruction of the pot at the time of the sherd (*kapālakāla*) is established by non-apprehension (*anupalabdhi*), for it is based on the determination of that; whereas in the case of apprehension, it is established by the convention of existence (*sadvyavahāra*). However, since this non-apprehension exists even before the pot and the like — prior to the hammer and so on — how is there not destruction [continuously]? If one argues that [non-existence] is the mere non-apprehension of what is perceptible (*dṛśyānupalabdhi*, non-apprehension of the perceptible), then at that moment it is mere non-apprehension. But what is this perceptibility (*dṛśyatā*)? It is the capacity for being seen (*darśanayogyatā*), [which is absent in that state].

[If one argues that] there is only non-existence of the pot and so on from the beginning, then if non-apprehension exists from the start, let there be just non-apprehension of the pot even at the time of the sherd — not non-existence. To say that it is not accepted by anyone is also improper, for it is accepted by the Sāṃkhya. And non-acceptance is not a proof (*pramāṇa*). If one argues that because the pot is not apprehended by anyone at the time of the sherd, [its non-existence is established, the argument fails].

[One cannot argue] that "the very object I have apprehended is apprehended by another" via direct perception (*pratyakṣa*), because the experience of another's mental continuum (*santānāntara*) is indirect (*parokṣa*). Nor is the nature of what is seen by oneself and others established by inference (*anumāna*), because that has universals (*sāmānya*) as its object. Indeed, it is not the prior fire itself that is established here from another's smoke, but only something of that same class. Thus, even here, something of the same class [is inferred in another mental continuum].

[There is no] stable state. If one argues that there is a difference in happiness and the like due to a difference in appearance (*pratibhāsa*), then that difference in appearance — which can occur in oneself and others — should be a differentiator even in the apprehended form (*grāhyākāra*). "This is what I am re-perceiving" is not [valid] in the first vision, because it is stated that there is no manifestation of the pervasion of re-apprehension (*punarupalambha*) and so on. Therefore, the nature of a previously seen pot [is not grasped through inference].

[The reality of the object] is known from re-seeing, through the determination that "this very thing is still present today." This is also not valid, because the operation of re-seeing in the prior instance is not established. Indeed, one who cognizes the object as presently before them does so because of the experience of a vivid form (*sphuṭarūpa*). But the anteriority of what is to be apprehended cannot extend to the prior time, since the temporal factors [do not exist within its object-domain].

[An object] not appearing as having a blue form (*nīlarūpa*) while concealed by a yellow form is not understood as having a blue form. If, however, it appears as associated with a previous time or the like, then since the previous state of place, time, and condition (*pūrvadeśakāladaśā*) appears vividly to the eye, it is just presentness (*varttamānatva*) — not anteriority (*pūrvatā*). Indeed, anteriority does not appear vividly in the case of a repeated pillar and so on. [Even if it were capable of being so, this too is not valid].

[The object] that is not manifesting in that way does not have the reality of that which is manifest. It is not proper to posit unity of the apprehended object (*grāhyaikya*) when there is a difference in appearance, as this would lead to the cessation of all distinctions. Therefore, even the stability inferred from subsequent vision — which the dull-witted claim is "the grasping of previous visibility (*pūrvadṛṣṭatā*)" — is most pernicious; since the previous vision has ceased and is not cognizable by another cognition, its status as object (*karmmatā*) [must be established through inference, not through direct perception].

The argument of the Tīrthikas is a meditation [on such matters]. It is not established by the shapes that are to be reduced to ash by fires. And if one were to say that the blue object is apprehended by direct perception as the object of a previous vision, then even a different previously-seen object should appear there. If one argues that it does not appear precisely because of the difference, and therefore that the previously seen identical object appears — is it not [the case that] because it is different, the anteriority of the pot and the like [is not grasped by direct perception]?

## 5. Lacuna Notes

### Lacuna 1
**Position:** Line 1, after `darśanayogyatā`
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~55
**Syntactic slot:** Elaboration of the definition of *dṛśyatā* as *darśanayogyatā*; locative-predication formula extending the definition
**Proposed opening:** ⟨tasyāmavasthāyāṃ nāstīti⟩
**Confidence: ◆ Attested**
**Rationale:** Verbatim in Śāstradīpikā SA_GSP28_sastrdiu:3143 as the immediate continuation of `dṛśyatā darśanayogyatā`. Same formula in Prajñākaragupta SA_T11_prpva:1688; Tibetan parallel BO_T11_D4225:153a-20 confirms the passage is within the PV commentary tradition.

---

### Lacuna 2
**Position:** Line 2, after `kapālakāle ghaṭasya kenacid anupalabdhatvād`
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~53
**Syntactic slot:** Ablative-governed result clause: the consequence the pūrvapakṣin draws from universal non-apprehension at the sherd-moment
**Proposed opening:** ⟨asattvam eva sidhyati,⟩
**Confidence: ◇ Probable**
**Rationale:** Nyāyamañjarī SA_GSP29_nyamanu:10673 shows the *asattva*-from-*anupalabdhi* argument at *kapālakāle*. Dharmottarapradīpa SA_GSP28_durdhpru:9158 frames the Buddhist counter-position. The ablative-governed conclusion `asattvam eva sidhyati` is the natural grammatical continuation.

---

### Lacuna 3
**Position:** Line 3, after `tathātrāpi tulya[jātīya]`
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~50
**Syntactic slot:** Completion of the parallel inference argument applying *sāmānyaviṣayatva* to the *santānāntara* case
**Proposed opening:** ⟨s tādṛk santānāntare 'numīyate,⟩
**Confidence: ◇ Probable**
**Rationale:** Ratnakīrti SA_T11_bsa075_u:5531 and Santānāntarasiddhi SA_T11_samtasi:21 deploy *santānāntara* in precisely this anti-*pratyabhijñā* argument. The *tathātrāpi* mirrors the smoke–fire analogy: just as only *tajjātīyas* is inferred from smoke, so only a same-kind entity in another continuum is inferred.

---

### Lacuna 4
**Position:** Line 4, after `pūrvadṛṣṭasya ghaṭa`
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~55
**Syntactic slot:** Genitive `ghaṭasya` (completing the compound) + predication denying inferential access to the pot's identity
**Proposed opening:** ⟨syānumānāt tattvaṃ na gṛhyate|⟩
**Confidence: ◇ Probable**
**Rationale:** Lines 3–4 deploy a dual-block: *pratyakṣa* cannot cross the stream boundary (*parasantānānubhūtadarśanasya parokṣatvāt*) and *anumāna* cannot recover singular *tattva* (*sāmānyaviṣayatvāt*). This restates that dual-block for the *punarupalambha* case. Jñānaśrīmitra SA_T11_jnaniva:11084 names the temporal-difference argument the text is executing.

---

### Lacuna 5
**Position:** Line 5, after `tatkālādīnāṃ [ma/sa]`
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~55
**Syntactic slot:** Genitive-absolute or reason clause: why past-time features are not within the scope of present perception
**Proposed opening:** ⟨nasy aiva viṣayatayā sthiteḥ|⟩
**Confidence: ○ Speculative**
**Rationale:** No verbatim parallel found. The reconstruction follows the argument: *pūrvakālāditā* cannot be the content of the present cognition because past-time properties fall within the scope of memory (*mānasa*) only. The uncertain `[ma/sa]` is read as `ma` (= `mānasya`).

---

### Lacuna 6
**Position:** Line 6, after `athā` (line-final)
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~51
**Syntactic slot:** New *pūrvapakṣa* conditional after the denial that *pūrvatā* appears vividly
**Proposed opening:** ⟨pi śakto bhaved ity etad api na,⟩
**Confidence: ○ Speculative**
**Rationale:** No parallel found. After denying the vivid appearance of *pūrvatā*, the *pūrvapakṣin* standardly retreats to a weaker position (even a faint or potential capacity suffices). The *uttarapakṣin* denies even this. `ity etad api na` is a formulaic rejection in Dharmakīrtian prose.

---

### Lacuna 7
**Position:** Line 7, after `tatkarmmatā`
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~51
**Syntactic slot:** Predicate of `tatkarmmatā` (the status of being-a-content-of-the-prior-cognition); must be established by inference not perception
**Proposed opening:** ⟨'numānāt sādhyā, na pratyakṣāt|⟩
**Confidence: ◇ Probable**
**Rationale:** Santānāntarasiddhi tradition (SA_T11_samtasi:21; SA_T11_bsa075_u:5531) establishes that properties of a past moment are inferential objects. The contrast `anumānāt … na pratyakṣāt` is the expected Dharmakīrtian formulation for this class of argument.

---

### Lacuna 8
**Position:** Line 8, after `na pratyakṣa`
**Estimated total akṣaras:** ~53
**Syntactic slot:** Instrumental construction completing the denial that *pratyakṣa* grasps *pūrvatā*
**Proposed opening:** ⟨eṇa gṛhyate pūrvatā,⟩
**Confidence: ◇ Probable**
**Rationale:** Lines 5–7 converge on showing *pūrvatā* is inaccessible to *pratyakṣa*; this closing line restates the thesis. SA_GSP29_nyabhu:450 (`na pratyakṣeṇa bhedaḥ … gṛhyate`) and SA_T11_kgpramv:13083 supply the formulaic type. The interlocutor's `nanu kiṃ bhinnatvāt` makes *pūrvatā* the natural subject of denial.
