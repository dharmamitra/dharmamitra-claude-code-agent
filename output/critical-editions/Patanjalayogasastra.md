Vyāsa-*bhāṣya*). Companion to the translation in
`output/translations/Patanjalayogasastra.md`.

## I. Variant readings

**4.23 (sūtra), draṣṭṛ°** — Σ `draṣṭṛdṛśyoparaktaṃ`; T `dṛṣṭṛdṛśya-uparaktam`. T's
*dṛṣṭṛ°* is a simple slip for *draṣṭṛ°* (agent noun of √dṛś), confirmed by Vā, who
discusses *draṣṭṛdṛśyau* (Vā ad 4.23, *tadaneneti*). Read **draṣṭṛ°** with Σ.

**4.25 (sūtra), °bhāvanānivṛttiḥ** — Σ `ātmabhāvabhāvanānivṛttiḥ`; T records a variant
`(-vinivṛttiḥ?)`, i.e. *°bhāvanā-vinivṛttiḥ*. Sense is unaffected (*ni-* vs intensive
*vi-ni-* "cessation / complete cessation"); the *bhāṣya* reads plain *nivartate*
("ceases"), which favours Σ's **°nivṛttiḥ**. Note as a minor v.l.

**4.28 (bhāṣya), pratyayaprasūḥ** — Σ `na pratyayaprasūr bhavati` (root noun *prasū-*,
"is no producer of cognition"); T `na pratyayaprasūtir bhavati` (*prasūti-*, "there is no
production of cognition"). Both yield the same sense; Σ's *prasūḥ* (nom. of the agent/
root noun *prasū*) is the *lectio difficilior* and is the received reading. Flag.

**4.31 (embedded verse, pāda d), tam ajihvo** — Σ `tam ajihvo 'bhyapūjayat`; T
`cittam ajihvo 'bhyapūjayat`. **Σ is correct.** (i) The verse is *anuṣṭubh*; pāda d must
scan as 8 syllables — *tám ajihvó 'bhyapūjayat* (8) is regular, whereas *cittam ajihvo…*
(9) is hypermetrical. (ii) The verse turns on a fourfold anaphoric *tam* ("…pierced **it**,
strung **it**, put **it** on, praised **it**"); Σ preserves all four, T's *cittam*
destroys the figure. (iii) Vā glosses simply *abhyapūjayat stutavān* with no *cittam*,
and the proverb-form he cites has *tam* throughout. **T's *cittam* is a corruption**
(probably eye-skip to the surrounding *citta*-heavy context). → **Read tam.**

**4.33 (bhāṣya), saṃsārakramasamāptiḥ … vyākaraṇīya** — no inter-witness variation, but
a *logical* crux worth flagging (and where the DharmaMitra `cat-translate` output had to
be corrected by hand): Vyāsa first files the question "is saṃsāra finite or infinite?"
under *avacanīya* ("not to be answered"), then immediately overturns that —
*kuśalasyāsti saṃsārakramasamāptir netarasya … tasmād vyākaraṇīya evāyaṃ praśnaḥ* — so
his settled verdict is that the question **is** answerable, *by distinction*
(*vyākaraṇīya* = *vibhajya-vacanīya*): the sequence ends for the adept (*kuśala*), not
for others. Asserting either horn unconditionally (*anyatarāvadhāraṇa*) is the fault.
The translation must not stop at *avacanīya*.

## II. The three embedded quotations

### 1. 4.22 verse — *na pātālaṃ na ca vivaraṃ girīṇāṃ…*

A 12-syllable *jagatī*-class verse (upajāti cadence), introduced by *tathā coktam*
("and so it has been said") — i.e. Vyāsa cites it as a known authority. **Source
unidentified.** In the DharmaMitra corpus the half-verse `na pātālaṃ na ca vivaraṃ
girīṇāṃ` is attested *only* inside the PYŚ witnesses themselves
(`SA_GSP34_patanjalayou:1711`, `SA_GSP34_yogasubha:1160`, `SA_GSP34_patyogbu:1475`):
no independent source surfaced. The content — *brahman* hidden in a "cave" that turns
out to be nothing but the undifferentiated operation of the intellect (*buddhivṛtti*) —
reads like a Vedāntically-coloured gnomic verse repurposed for the Yoga point that
puruṣa's "self-cognition" is really cognition *of* the buddhi-operation.
- Citations: `https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_GSP34_patanjalayou/text?active_segment=SA_GSP34_patanjalayou:1711`

### 2. 4.31 verse — *andho maṇim avidhyat…* (the riddle of the jewel)

*Anuṣṭubh* (8×4). A **floating *nyāya* / subhāṣita**, not original to Vyāsa — it
circulated as a stock illustration of cognition functioning without its ordinary
instruments. Independently attested in the corpus:
- **Śaṅkara, *Bhagavadgītābhāṣya*** (ad 13.14, *sarvendriyaguṇābhāsam*),
  `SA_GSP33_bhgsbh:5831` — quotes *andho maṇi[m]…* to the same effect (the Self
  "perceiving" without sense-organs).
  `https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_GSP33_bhgsbh/text?active_segment=SA_GSP33_bhgsbh:5831`
- **Ruyyaka, *Vyaktivivekavyākhyā*** (on Mahimabhaṭṭa), `SA_GK16_ruvyavivya_pu:1394`:
  *tad ayam andho maṇim **avindat** tam anaṅgulir āvayad iti nyāya āyāt* — cited
  explicitly *as a nyāya* ("the maxim of…"), with the notable variant **avindat**
  ("found") for PYŚ **avidhyat** ("pierced").
  `https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_GK16_ruvyavivya_pu/text?active_segment=SA_GK16_ruvyavivya_pu:1394`
- Variant register: **avidhyat** (√vyadh, "pierced/bored," PYŚ + Śaṅkara) vs **avindat**
  (√vid, "found," Ruyyaka). *avidhyat* is the *lectio difficilior* and fits the
  jewel-stringing imagery (boring the bead); *avindat* is a trivializing simplification.
- Vā ad 4.31 confirms the prose paraphrase: *avidhyad andho maṇim … āvayad grathitavān,
  pratyamuñcat pinaddhavān, abhyapūjayat stutavān*.

### 3. 4.33 — the *oṃ bhoḥ* dialogue

Not a verse but staged dialogue. The exclamatory affirmation *oṃ bhoḥ* ("Yes indeed!")
answering "will everyone born die?" exemplifies the *ekānta-vacanīya* (categorical)
answer-type. See §III.3.

## III. Doctrinal parallels & the Buddhist polemic

### 1. 4.23 — refutation of *cittamātra* / Vijñānavāda

Vyāsa's targets (*bhrāntāḥ kecit … apare cittamātram eva …*) are (a) those who make the
mind itself conscious and (b) the Yogācāra "mind-only" denial of external objects.
- **Doxographic cousin in the corpus**: a Vedānta *Brahmasūtra* commentary
  (`SA_GSP33_vesa`, "Vedāntasāra") at BS **2.2.27–29** expressly "refute[s] the views of
  the Yogācāra-s … that establish the negation of objects other than cognition," using
  the very *ghaṭa* ("I know the pot") test-case (`SA_GSP33_vesa:388, :6026, :6029`) —
  structurally the same move as Vyāsa's *gavādir ghaṭādiś ca … loka*.
- **Vā's escalation (pramāṇa interest)**: Vācaspati ad 4.23 reads the opponent as the
  *vijñānavādin* and meets him with the **Dharmakīrtian *sahopalambha-niyama***
  (co-apprehension argument from the *Pramāṇaviniścaya*): *yad vedyate yena vedanena
  tat tato na bhidyate … niyata-sahopalambhaś ca artho jñānena* (Vā, `tattvavaisaradi`
  ll. 1221, 1255–1283). This is the most direct point of contact with the
  Dharmakīrti-tradition pramāṇa material — Vā answers the Buddhist on the Buddhist's own
  *sahopalambha* ground, arguing the reflected object (*pratibimba*) in *samādhiprajñā*
  proves a real *puruṣa* distinct from *citta*.

### 2. 4.24 — *saṃhatyakāritva* and Sāṃkhyakārikā 17

The argument "the aggregate acts for another, like a house" is the Yoga form of the
classic Sāṃkhya proof of *puruṣa*:
- **Sāṃkhyakārikā 17**: *saṃghāta-parārthatvāt … puruṣo 'sti* ("because every aggregate
  is for-another's-sake, … the puruṣa exists"). Vā ad 4.24 makes the link explicit with
  the SK's own *śayanāsana* simile: *parārthā buddhiḥ saṃhatyakāritvāc
  **chayanāsanābhyaṅgavat*** (Vā `tattvavaisaradi` l. 445; cf. Sāṃkhyatattvakaumudī
  ad SK 17, DM `SA_GSP31_stk:5108, :5135` — "a cook … for another's sake").
- Polemical edge: Vyāsa insists the "other" is no *sāmānyamātra* ("mere generality") —
  a jab at the Buddhist *vaināśika*, who can supply only an aggregate (hence itself
  *parārtha*), never the *viśeṣa* that is *puruṣa*.
- **NOT the Dharmakīrti pair (tested against the Vivaraṇa).** It is tempting to read the
  *viśeṣa / sāmānyamātra* hierarchy as Dharmakīrti's PV 3.3 pair (*viśeṣa* = *svalakṣaṇa* =
  *paramārthasat* vs *sāmānya* = *saṃvṛtisat*) "inverted on permanence" — but that is a
  **loose lexical coincidence**. The **Vivaraṇa** (ascribed to Śaṅkara; preferred here over
  Vācaspati, who retrojects Dharmakīrtian doctrine — *sahopalambhaniyama* ad 4.23) glosses
  *para-sāmānyamātram* concretely as **the body/senses/objects** (*śarīram indriyāṇi viṣayā
  vā*) and the Buddhist's **saṃghāta / santāna**, dismissed as ***avastu*** (*śaśaviṣāṇa-
  kalpa*, "rabbit's-horn") and as merely *kṣaṇika*; the *viśeṣa* is the puruṣa as
  ***bhinnajātīya***, a non-composite **permanent** individual. Register = the **Abhidharma
  pudgala/saṃghāta debate** (wholes/continua = *prajñapti*), **not** the Dignāgan/
  Dharmakīrtian *svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa* pair — no *svalakṣaṇa*, no *arthakriyā*, no
  *paramārtha/saṃvṛti*, and the "real" pole is the **permanent** puruṣa (inverse axis from
  the momentary svalakṣaṇa). The Vivaraṇa routes the refutation back to 4.19
  (*vyatiriktadṛśyatvāt*, rejecting *dharmamātra* + *kṣaṇikatva*) — a Dignāgan-level tool.
  By contrast, the genuinely Dignāgan *viśeṣa/sāmānya* epistemics *do* surface elsewhere —
  YBh 1.25 (*anumānaṃ na viśeṣapratipattau samartham*) and the *svasaṃvedana* refutation at
  4.19–22. See the consolidated report `output/reports/pys-pramanavada-points-of-contact.md`
  (§1, §5 inversion note) and `output/reports/dharmakirti-svalaksana-paramarthasat.md`.
- The companion *bhāṣya* line *na jñānaṃ jñānārtham … parārtham* ("cognition is not for
  its own sake") is already the **anti-*svasaṃvedana*** thesis (see §III.2-ter).

### 2-bis. 4.25 — the self-questions and *nairātmya* inverted to *true-ātman*

Vyāsa's catalogue of the self-questioning that ceases for the *viśeṣadarśin* —
*ko 'ham āsaṃ katham aham āsaṃ kiṃsvid idaṃ kathaṃsvid idaṃ ke bhaviṣyāmaḥ kathaṃ vā
bhaviṣyāmaḥ* ("Who was I? How was I? … What shall we become? How?") — is the **Buddhist
three-times self-doubt formula**, and unlike the Pāli loci of §III.3 it **is** in the
corpus in Sanskrit Buddhist dress: the **Śrāvakabhūmi** (`SA_T06_srabhu_u:2013`):
*ko nv aham abhūvaṃ, kathaṃ nv aham abhūvam atīte 'dhvani / ko nv ahaṃ bhaviṣyāmy
anāgate 'dhvani … ke santaḥ ke bhaviṣyāmaḥ* — i.e. the *ṣoḍaśa-vicikitsā* of the
Sabbāsava-sutta (MN 2), the doubts the noble disciple abandons on seeing the truth.
- **Structural inversion.** Both traditions: *a darśana dissolves the self-questioning and
  liberates.* The target Vyāsa reverses is **canonical Buddhist soteriology** — the
  noble disciple's abandonment of the self-doubts *on seeing the truth*
  (Sabbāsava-sutta MN 2 / Śrāvakabhūmi, **pre-Dignāga**); there the liberating *darśana* is
  **nairātmya-darśana**. (Dharmakīrti *versifies* this same inherited doctrine at PV
  2.218–219, *ātmani sati parasaṃjñā…*; the verse is an illustrative parallel, **not** the
  source — 4.25 carries no Dharmakīrtian marking.) For Yoga the *darśana* is instead
  **viśeṣa-darśana** — seeing the *true* self (puruṣa) as distinct from the citta
  (*cittasyaivaiṣa vicitraḥ pariṇāmaḥ, puruṣas tu … śuddhaḥ*). Identical therapeutic
  structure, content reversed: **anātman vs. true-ātman**. NB (inversion principle):
  the reversal proves acquaintance with nairātmya only at its *canonical* specificity —
  hence "knew canonical Buddhism," not "knew Dharmakīrti." Cf. report §5 methodological note.
- **The *sva/para* axis is shared, valenced oppositely.** 4.24 is built on *svārtha/parārtha*
  where the *para* (the beneficiary) **is** the self (puruṣa), the goal of the path;
  Dharmakīrti's *ātmani sati parasaṃjñā* makes the very arising of a *para* the disease.

### 2-ter. 4.22 / 4.33 within the larger pramāṇavāda engagement

Two in-scope loci belong to the broader dossier assembled in
`output/reports/pys-pramanavada-points-of-contact.md`:
- **4.22** *svabuddhisaṃvedanam* — the unchanging *citi* "cognizing its own buddhi" is a
  deliberately near-homonymous Yoga coinage *against* Buddhist *svasaṃvedana*: it is not a
  cognition cognizing *itself*. The frontal refutation of self-luminous cognition is at
  **4.19–4.21** (out of this apparatus's 4.22–34 range): *na tat svābhāsaṃ dṛśyatvāt*
  (`SA_GSP34_patyogbu:1450`), whose *pūrvapakṣa* states Dignāga's *dvirūpa* doctrine
  verbatim — *cittam eva svābhāsaṃ viṣayābhāsaṃ ca … agnivat* — and is met with the
  classic fire-analogy rebuttal. **Strongest Dignāgan fingerprint in the *bhāṣya*.**
- **4.33** *kṣaṇapratiyogī … kramaḥ* — Vyāsa accepts the *kṣaṇa* as the unit of succession
  but embeds it in *kūṭasthanityatā* (puruṣa) + *pariṇāminityatā* (guṇas): *kṣaṇa* without
  *kṣaṇa-bhaṅga*. The anti-*kṣaṇikatva* stance, with the supporting *satkāryavāda* at
  **4.12–13** (*nāsty asataḥ saṃbhavaḥ … atītānāgataṃ svarūpato 'sti*) and the
  dharma/dharmin gold-analogy at **3.13** — but in the **generic Sāṃkhya** idiom, with no
  specifically Dharmakīrtian instrument (no *arthakriyā*-based *sattvānumāna*).

### 3. 4.33 — the four-fold *pañha-vyākaraṇa* (the deepest Buddhist parallel)

Vyāsa's typology of questions —
*ekānta-vacanīya* (answer categorically) / *vibhajya-vacanīya* (answer with a
distinction) / *avacanīya* (set aside) — is a near-verbatim Brahmanical reflex of the
**Buddhist four-fold classification of questions** (*pañha-vyākaraṇa*):
1. *ekaṃsa-vyākaraṇīya* — to be answered categorically
2. *vibhajja-vyākaraṇīya* — to be answered analytically/with a distinction
3. *paṭipucchā-vyākaraṇīya* — to be answered by counter-question
4. *ṭhapanīya* — to be set aside
- Loci **[not in corpus]**: Aṅguttara-nikāya II.46 (Catukka-nipāta, *Pañha-sutta*);
  Dīgha-nikāya 33 (*Saṅgīti-sutta*); Abhidharmakośa & *-bhāṣya* (Skt.
  *ekāṃśa- / vibhajya- / paripṛcchā- / sthāpanīya-vyākaraṇa*).
- **Vyāsa keeps three of the four** (he has no *paṭipucchā*/counter-question type) and
  renames *sthāpanīya/ṭhapanīya* as *avacanīya*. His exemplar of the set-aside question —
  ***saṃsāro 'yam antavān athānantaḥ*** ("is this saṃsāra finite or infinite?") — is
  precisely one of the canonical Buddhist *avyākata* / *avyākṛta* questions
  (*antavā loko / anantavā loko*: Cūḷamāluṅkya-sutta, MN 63; SN 44, etc. **[not in
  corpus]**).
- Irony worth flagging: in the very chapter where Vyāsa hammers the *vaināśika*, his
  logical apparatus for handling metaphysical questions is borrowed from Buddhist
  scholastic method — then turned against the Buddhist conclusion (he *resolves* the
  finite/infinite question as *vyākaraṇīya*, against the Buddha's setting it aside: for
  the *kuśala* saṃsāra ends, for others not).

### 4. 4.29–4.30 — *dharmamegha* and *jīvanmukti*

- *dharmamegha-samādhi*: the term is shared with Mahāyāna (the tenth *bhūmi* is
  *dharmameghā* in the *Daśabhūmika*); a genuine cross-tradition resonance, though Yoga
  gives it its own *vivekakhyāti*-based sense. (Not retrievable as a textual parallel in
  this corpus; noted for the record.)
- *jīvann eva … vimukto bhavati* (4.30): living liberation. Cf. Mādhava/Vidyāraṇya,
  **Jīvanmuktiviveka** (`SA_GSP33_jimuvi`), which builds its whole treatment of
  *jīvanmukti* on Yoga material — e.g. on the liberated one who "does not see his body,
  whether he remains in one place or moves about" (`SA_GSP33_jimuvi:8586`),
  paraphrasing the *kṛtārtha*/*kaivalya* end-state of 4.32–4.34.
  `https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_GSP33_jimuvi/text?active_segment=SA_GSP33_jimuvi:8586`
