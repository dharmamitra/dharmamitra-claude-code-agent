# Intertextual Report: The *vajravrata* Verse

**Query verse** (as transmitted in Ānandagarbha, *Vajrajvālodayā*):

> siddhivajravrataṃ cānena gṛhnīyāt |
> idaṃ tat sarvabuddhatvaṃ vajraṃ vajradharasya tu |
> tvayāpi hi sadā dhāryaṃ mahāvajradharavratam || iti |

**Date of report:** 2026-10-21

---

## 1. Identification

The verse is the ***vajravrata*-stanza** — a liturgical verse recited at the moment of bestowing the vajra upon the initiate (*vajravratadāna*) during tantric consecration (*abhiṣeka*). It originates in the *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha* (STTS), the foundational Yoga Tantra, and is among the most widely quoted single stanzas in Indian Buddhist ritual literature.

The prose frame in the Vajrajvālodayā — `siddhivajravrataṃ cānena gṛhnīyāt` ("and by means of this [mantra] one should take up the siddhi-vajra-vow") — introduces the verse as a quotation. The gloss that follows it (`tad idaṃ tat sarvatathāgataparamamahābodhicittam asya | parityāgād asya ca sadā dhāraṇām atirahasyaṃ`) identifies the vajra with the supreme mahābodhicitta of all tathāgatas and calls its perpetual holding the supreme secret.

---

## 2. Two Recensions

The verse circulates in two distinct textual forms. Pādas a and c are invariant; the variation falls in pādas b and d.

### Recension A — "Vajradhara"

> idaṃ tat sarvabuddhatvaṃ **vajraṃ vajradharasya tu** |
> tvayāpi hi sadā dhāryaṃ **mahāvajradharavratam** ||

- "This is the state of all buddhas — the vajra belonging to Vajradhara. You too must always maintain the great vow of Vajradhara."
- This is the **Paramādya recension**. It is attested in the Sanskrit *Paramādya* fragments (SA_K12_paramadyatau:571–579: `tvayāpi hi sadā dhāryaṁ mahāvajradharaṁ vratam iti`), in the Tibetan *Paramādyamantrakalpakhaṇḍa* (D 488:228b-25), in Ānandagarbha's *Paramādiṭīkā* (D 2512, quoted several times), and in Ānandagarbha's *Vajrajvālodayā*. See §2a.
- The DharmaMitra edition of the Vajrajvālodayā marks `(source?)` here. The source is the Paramādya.

### Recension B — "Vajrasattva / Vajrapāṇi"

> idaṃ tat sarvabuddhatvaṃ **vajrasattvakare sthitam** |
> tvayāpi hi sadā dhāryaṃ **vajrapāṇidṛḍhavratam** ||

- "This is the state of all buddhas, abiding in the hand of Vajrasattva. You too must always maintain the firm vow of Vajrapāṇi."
- The canonical standard: attested in 11+ Sanskrit texts and all surveyed Tibetan translations.

### Variant: Abhayākaragupta's Heruka adaptation

> idaṃ tat sarvabuddhatvaṃ **śrīherukapade sthitam** |
> tvayāpi hi sadā dhārya[ṃ …]

- In the *Abhayapaddhati* (Buddhakapāla cycle), pāda b is adapted to **śrīherukapade sthitam**, "abiding at the feet of Śrī Heruka." This shows the verse functioned as a template whose pāda b could be adjusted to the presiding deity of the specific maṇḍala.

## 2a. The Paramādya source and the *rahasya* gloss *(added 2026-10-21)*

In the Vajrajvālodayā the verse is followed by a prose gloss and a mantra:

> tad idaṃ sarvatathāgataparamamahābodhicittam asyāparityāgāt | asya ca sadā dhāraṇam iti rahasyam | he vajragarvāpate dhīḥ śrīḥ strīḥ iti ||

"This [vajra] is the supreme great bodhicitta of all tathāgatas. Through not abandoning it, and through always holding it: that is the secret [meaning]."

The whole unit (verse, gloss, mantra) comes from the **Paramādya**:

**Textual points:**

1. **The text should read *asyāparityāgāt*, not *asya | parityāgāt*.** The DharmaMitra edition splits it as *asya | parityāgād*, which gives the opposite sense ("by abandoning it"). The Sanskrit Paramādya reads *asyāparityāgād*, and every Tibetan witness has ཡོངས་སུ་མི་སྤང / མ་སྤངས་པར (= *aparityāga*). So the *a-* is the negative prefix fused by sandhi.
2. ***iti rahasyam* vs. *atirahasyam*.** *iti rahasyam* is supported by the Sanskrit Paramādya (*iti rahasyaṁ*) and by D 2512's ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི … གསང་བ (= *iti … rahasyam*). It also matches the STTS habit of labelling hidden meanings *iti rahasyam*. D 488's ཤིན་ཏུ་གསང་ is **not** good evidence for *atirahasyam*. D 488 turns the gloss into seven-syllable verse, so ཤིན་ཏུ may be filler to fill out the line, and the line itself (ཤིན་ཏུ་གསང་བདེ་འདི་ཡིན་ཏེ) looks corrupt. The DharmaMitra Vajrajvālodayā reading *atirahasyaṃ* is best treated as an *iti/ati* misreading.
3. **The vocative *he vajragarvāpate*.** The Sanskrit evidence supports it: the Paramādya has *he vajragarvādhipati*, and *vajragarvāpati* closes the *anādinidhanaḥ sattvo* verse in the Sanskrit of Nāgabuddhi, Dīpaṃkarabhadra and the Paramādya. The Tibetan transcription in D 2512, ཧི་བཛྲ་གརྦྷ་བ་པ་ཏེ (*hi vajra garbha ba pa te*), **cannot decide between *garva* and *garbha***, because Tibetan mantra transcriptions blur *v/b/bh*. Tibetan translators in fact seem to have read *garbha*: in the *anādinidhana* verse, *vajragarvāpatiḥ patiḥ* is rendered རྡོ་རྗེ་སྙིང་པོ་བདག་པོའི་གཙོ (D 1261:318a-36; སྙིང་པོ = *garbha*). So *vajragarbha* has to be kept as a possible variant.
4. **The *dhīḥ śrīḥ strīḥ* ending is unparalleled.** In the Paramādya witnesses the vocative leads into the name-consecration formula (*śrī-vajra-nāma siddhisamayas tvaṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ*), whereas the Vajrajvālodayā closes with three seed syllables. The strings just before the vocative (*vajrakarmavādhidhi*, Sa.; ཀརྨྨ་བཱ་དྷི་དྷི, D 488) are corrupt in both witnesses, perhaps for *vajrakarmādhipati*, so they cannot explain the seed syllables. Re-examine the manuscript here.

*Method note:* the Tibetan witnesses are reliable where they **translate** (e.g. ཡོངས་སུ་མི་སྤང = *aparityāga*, which supports point 1). They are unreliable where they **transcribe** Sanskrit mantras, and in passages turned into verse where filler words may be added.

## 4. Tibetan Witnesses

All surveyed Tibetan translations follow **Recension B**. The standard Tibetan rendering is:

> འདི་དེ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི། །
> རྡོ་རྗེ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཕྱག་ན་གནས། །
> (**= vajrasattvakare sthitam**)
>
> ཁྱོད་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་ནི་རྟག་ཏུ་བཅང་། །
> ཕྱག་ན་རྡོ་རྗེའི་བརྟུལ་ཞུགས་བརྟན། །
> (**= vajrapāṇidṛḍhavratam**)

Minor variation exists in pāda b: some witnesses read རྡོ་རྗེ་སེམས་དཔའི་**ལག་ན** གནས (D 2510, D 2519: "abiding in the **hand** of Vajrasattva") vs. རྡོ་རྗེ་སེམས་དཔའ་**ཕྱག་ན** གནས (D 480: honorific ཕྱག "hand"), both translating *kare sthitam*.

## 5. Ritual Context

Across all witnesses, the verse occupies the same ritual moment: the **vajravratadāna** — the conferral of the vajra vow during abhiṣeka. The standard liturgical sequence, stable from Nāgabuddhi (8th c.) through Jagaddarpaṇa (12th c.), is:

1. The ācārya visualizes the disciple as Vajrasattva.
2. The verse is recited while placing the vajra in the disciple's right hand.
3. The mantra follows immediately: `oṃ sarvatathāgatasiddhivajrasamaya tiṣṭha eṣa tvāṃ dhārayāmi vajrasattva hīḥ hi hi hi hi hūṃ`.
4. The disciple takes hold of the vajra (*vajravrataṃ grāhayet*).

Several texts (Nāgabuddhi, Dīpaṃkarabhadra) place the verse at verse number 310–313 in numbered sequences, suggesting a fixed position within a standardized ritual text.

## 6. Companion verses

In Nāgabuddhi's *Viṃśatividhi* and the *Sarvadurgatipariśodhanatantra*, the *vajravrata*-stanza is preceded by a closely related verse:

> idaṃ tat sarvabuddhatvaṃ **gṛhṇa vajraṃ susiddhaye** |
> sarvabuddhais tvayi dattaṃ siddhivajravrataṃ bhavet ||

"This is the state of all buddhas — take the vajra for the sake of perfect accomplishment. Given to you by all the buddhas, may the siddhi-vajra-vow come to be."

This preparatory verse shares the same pāda a and is clearly part of the same liturgical unit. The Vajrajvālodayā's prose introduction (`siddhivajravrataṃ cānena gṛhnīyāt`) echoes its language (*siddhivajravrata*, *gṛhṇa*), suggesting Ānandagarbha had both verses in his source.

## 7. Observations

1. **Recension A is the Paramādya recension, not an isolated reading.** *(Revised 2026-10-21.)* The Vajradhara / mahāvajradhara wording, together with its *rahasya* gloss, is attested in the Sanskrit and Tibetan Paramādya and in Ānandagarbha's Paramādiṭīkā. The Vajrajvālodayā follows the Paramādya ritual tradition here, not the STTS.

2. **Recension B is the normative transmission.** It is attested in two root tantras (STTS, Sarvadurgatipariśodhana), at least nine sādhana/vidhi texts, and all Tibetan translations surveyed.

3. **The verse functioned as a modular template.** Abhayākaragupta's substitution of *śrīherukapade sthitam* in the Heruka cycle demonstrates that pāda b was understood as adjustable to the presiding deity — which may also explain the Vajrajvālodayā's Vajradhara reading.

4. **Chronological span of attestation:** The verse is traceable from Nāgabuddhi (~8th c.) and Ānandagarbha (~8th–9th c.) through Abhayākaragupta and Jagaddarpaṇa (~11th–12th c.) — a minimum circulation of four centuries in Indian Buddhist ritual literature.

5. **The two recensions belong to two scriptural lineages.** Recension B is the STTS (Vajradhātu) form, and it is what the later maṇḍalavidhi literature adopted. Recension A is the Paramādya form, and its anointing mantra ends in *mahāvajradhara* (*…adhipati tvām abhiṣiñcāmi mahāvajradhara*). The Vajradhara wording is therefore part of the Paramādya rite as a whole, not Ānandagarbha's own change. The corpus alone cannot settle which form is older.
