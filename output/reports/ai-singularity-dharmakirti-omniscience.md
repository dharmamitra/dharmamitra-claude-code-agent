# Recursive Self-Improvement, the AI Singularity, and Dharmakīrti's Proof of the Buddha's Omniscience

*With a comparison of the two omniscience arguments of the Pātañjalayogaśāstra
(kāṣṭhāprāpti vs. śāśvatika utkarṣa)*

Report — 2026-06-30

---

## Thesis in brief

The modern argument for an "intelligence explosion" or **technological
singularity** and Dharmakīrti's classical argument for the *possibility* of a
Buddha's omniscience share a single logical skeleton. Neither is an argument
*from degrees* ("knowledge comes in more and less, therefore a maximum
exists"). Both are arguments *from a mechanism*: each identifies a concrete
**self-amplifying process running on a stable, persisting substrate**, and then
observes that such a process has **no intrinsic ceiling**. The work is done not
by the observation that excellence varies, but by the claim that the variation
is *cumulative and self-feeding*.

This is exactly why, of the **two** distinct proofs of Īśvara's omniscience that
the *Pātañjalayogaśāstra* (PYŚ) offers, the second — the **śāśvatika utkarṣa**
argument (bhāṣya ad *Yogasūtra* 1.24) — is structurally closer to Dharmakīrti
than the first, the famous **kāṣṭhāprāpti** argument (ad YS 1.25). The
kāṣṭhāprāpti argument is the *static* argument-from-degrees that Dharmakīrti's
whole strategy is designed to *replace*; the śāśvatika utkarṣa argument supplies
a beginningless, mutually-reinforcing loop between knowledge and the refinement
of its substrate — i.e., recursive self-improvement.

## Part I. Dharmakīrti's argument (Pramāṇavārttika 2)

### I.1 What is to be proven: soteriological, not extensive, omniscience

Before the mechanism, Dharmakīrti narrows the target. He is uninterested in
proving that the Buddha knows *every fact*; he wants to prove that the Buddha is
**pramāṇa** (an authority) regarding what matters for liberation. The locus is
*Pramāṇavārttika* (PV) 2.31–33 (Miyasaka numbering; ed. *pramanavarttika_miyasaka.txt*
ll. 64–69):

> tasmād **anuṣṭheyagataṃ** jñānam asya vicāryatām /
> **kīṭasaṃkhyāparijñānaṃ** tasya naḥ kva upayujyate // (2.31)
> **heyopādeyatattvasya** hānyupāyasya vedakaḥ /
> yaḥ pramāṇam asāv iṣṭo **na tu sarvasya vedakaḥ** // (2.32)
> dūraṃ paśyatu vā mā vā tattvam iṣṭaṃ tu paśyatu /
> pramāṇaṃ **dūradarśī** ced eta **gṛdhrān** upāsmahe // (2.33)

"Let his knowledge *of what is to be practiced* be examined; of what use to us
is his knowing *the number of insects*? He who knows the reality of what is to
be abandoned and adopted (*heya-upādeya*) and the means of abandonment is the
one accepted as an authority — **not a knower of everything**. Whether he sees
far or not, let him see the *desired truth*; if seeing-far were the criterion,
**we may as well worship vultures**."

This restriction is itself a precise anticipation of how "superintelligence" is
actually defined in the singularity literature: not as a literal omniscience
over all facts, but as a *functional* mastery of the domain that counts
(achieving goals, outperforming humans across cognitively relevant tasks). The
"vultures see far" jibe is the ancient form of *more raw sensory range / more
compute ≠ more wisdom*.

### I.2 The mechanism: cultivation on a stable substrate (PV 2.34–35, 120–131)

Having fixed the target, Dharmakīrti must show that the relevant excellence —
above all **karuṇā** (compassion) and the liberating insight that rides on it —
can in fact be brought to perfection. The seed is PV 2.34 (ll. 70–71):

> **sādhanaṃ karuṇābhyāsāt** sa buddher **dehasaṃśrayāt** /
> asiddho 'bhyāsa iti cen na **āśrayapratiṣedhataḥ** //

"The proof [of perfectibility] is from *the repeated cultivation of compassion*,
which rests on the **mind** (buddhi), not on the body. If you object that the
cultivation is not established [to be limitless] — no, because of the *denial of
[the body as] its substrate*."

The full demonstration is PV 2.120–131. Dharmakīrti stages an opponent who
presses the obvious objection (2.120):

> **abhyāsena viśeṣe 'pi laṅghana-udaka-tāpa-vat** /
> svabhāva-atikramo mā bhūt iti cet ... //

"Granted that repeated practice produces enhancement — but as with **leaping**,
or **heating water**, [there must be a limit], lest the thing's *very nature be
transgressed* (a man cannot learn to jump arbitrarily far; water cannot be made
arbitrarily hot)."

Dharmakīrti's reply is the heart of the matter, and it turns entirely on the
**stability of the substrate** (2.121–128):

- Leaping is bounded because **its substrate is unstable** (*asthira-āśraya*):
  the body. The strength and effort that cause a jump have a *fixed capacity*
  (*sthita-śaktitva*), so each jump starts again from roughly the same baseline
  — "leaping does not arise from leaping" (*na ... laṅghanād eva laṅghanaṃ*,
  2.127). The gain does not *persist in the cause*, so it cannot compound.
- Mental qualities are different: once a quality is *instilled* (*āhita*) it
  **does not require renewed effort** (*na apekṣeta punar yatnam*, 2.123),
  because the increment has *become the nature itself* (*svabhāvo jāyate guṇaḥ*,
  2.125). Practice-born states arise "of their own accord" (*svarasena*, 2.124).
- Therefore each later effort builds on a permanently raised floor:
  *taduttarottaro yatno viśeṣasya vidhāyakaḥ* (2.125) — "each subsequent effort
  is a producer of [further] distinction." And because compassion-cognitions
  "grow from prior seeds of the same kind" (*tulyajātīya-pūrva-bīja-pravṛddhayaḥ*,
  2.126), the rhetorical question follows: **saty abhyāse kutaḥ sthitiḥ** —
  *"when there is practice, whence any stopping?"* There is **no upper bound**.

The conclusion (2.131, l. 263–264) even names the limit with the very word the
PYŚ will use:

> kṛpātmakatvam abhyāsād ghṛṇā-vairāgya-rāga-vat /
> **niṣpanna-karuṇā-utkarṣa** ... //

"...the **perfected culmination (utkarṣa) of compassion**." Dharmakīrti's
explosion terminates in *karuṇotkarṣa*; the Yoga author's Īśvara is defined by
*śāśvatika utkarṣa*. The lexical echo is not accidental — both are reaching for
the idea of an excellence pushed to its terminus.

### I.3 The argument in one line

> An excellence whose substrate persists, and each of whose increments
> *survives in the cause as a raised baseline for the next increment*, has no
> intrinsic limit; the path therefore makes the perfection of compassion-and-
> insight realizable; hence a perfected knower (of *heyopādeya*) is possible.

## Part II. The AI singularity: anatomy of the argument

The technological-singularity thesis (I. J. Good's 1965 *intelligence
explosion*; Vinge 1993; Kurzweil 2005; Bostrom 2014) has the identical
structure:

1. **A capacity that operates on itself.** An artificial agent intelligent
   enough to design intelligent agents can apply that capacity to *its own
   design*. (Good: "an ultraintelligent machine could design even better
   machines.")
2. **A stable, persisting substrate.** Improvements are *retained* — written
   back into weights, architecture, or code — and the improved system is the
   platform for the next round. Nothing has to be re-earned from a baseline.
3. **Compounding / positive feedback.** Each gain raises the rate of further
   gain: *more capable → better at self-improvement → more capable*. This is
   recursive self-improvement, and Kurzweil's "law of accelerating returns" is
   its quantitative face.
4. **No intrinsic ceiling within the mechanism.** The limit, if any, is
   external (physics, compute, data), not internal to the feedback loop. Absent
   such a wall, the trajectory is an *explosion* — "there would then
   unquestionably be an intelligence explosion, and the intelligence of man
   would be left far behind" (Good).

The decisive move — exactly as in Dharmakīrti — is **step 2–3**: the substrate
persists, so gains *accumulate in the cause*. The singularity theorist's
favourite contrast case is also Dharmakīrti's: domains that **plateau** because
their substrate does *not* carry improvement forward (a sprinter's body, a
fixed-architecture chip) versus domains that **compound** (software editing its
own source). "Leaping does not arise from leaping" is precisely the engineer's
"you cannot bootstrap a faster runner from running," set against "you *can*
bootstrap a better optimizer from optimizing."

## Part III. The parallelism, point by point

| Element | Dharmakīrti (PV 2) | AI singularity |
|---|---|---|
| Target | mastery of *heyopādeya* (functional, soteriological), **not** knowing the insect-count | "superintelligence" as functional dominance, not literal omniscience |
| Engine | *abhyāsa / bhāvanā* of compassion & insight | recursive self-improvement |
| Stable substrate | the mind-stream (*buddhi / citta*), not the body | persisted weights / code, not fixed hardware-of-the-moment |
| Why it compounds | the increment "becomes the nature," survives as a raised baseline (2.123–125) | each improvement is retained and platforms the next |
| Bounded contrast case | leaping, heating water — *asthira-āśraya* (2.120, 127) | body-bound athletics, fixed-architecture chips that plateau |
| No-ceiling claim | *saty abhyāse kutaḥ sthitiḥ* (2.126) | "intelligence explosion," accelerating returns |
| Terminus word | *karuṇā-**utkarṣa*** (2.131) | the "explosion" / ultraintelligence |

The shared logical core is a **realizability argument**: not "X admits of
degrees, so a maximal X exists," but "here is an actual process that carries X
upward without an internal stop."

## Part IV. The two omniscience arguments of the Pātañjalayogaśāstra

The PYŚ proves Īśvara's supremacy *twice*, with two logically different
arguments. Reading them side by side shows why one of them — and only one — is
the genuine analogue of Dharmakīrti and of the singularity.

### IV.1 The kāṣṭhāprāpti argument (bhāṣya ad YS 1.25)

YS 1.25: *tatra niratiśayaṃ sarvajñabījam* — "In him the seed of omniscience is
at its utmost." The bhāṣya (file *patanjali_yogasutra_w_bhasya_b.txt* l. 137):

> yad idam atīta-anāgata-pratyutpanna- ... -atīndriya-grahaṇam **alpaṃ bahv** iti
> sarvajñabījam etad vivardhamānaṃ **yatra niratiśayaṃ sa sarvajñaḥ**. asti
> **kāṣṭhāprāptiḥ** sarvajñabījasya **sātiśayatvāt parimāṇavat** iti. yatra
> kāṣṭhāprāptir jñānasya sa sarvajñaḥ.

"The grasping of supersensible things — past, future, present — is found in
**lesser and greater** degree; this 'seed of omniscience,' as it increases,
[reaches a point] where it admits of no further superiority — *that one is the
omniscient*. There **is** a culmination (*kāṣṭhāprāpti*) of the seed of
omniscience, **because it admits of degrees (*sātiśayatva*), like magnitude
(*parimāṇavat*)**. Where knowledge reaches its culmination, he is omniscient."

This is the classic **argument from degrees** (the structure later canonical in
the West as Aquinas's *quarta via*): *quality Q varies in intensity → Q has a
maximal instance → that maximum is realized in some being*. Its sole engine is
the *sātiśayatva–parimāṇavat* analogy: as magnitude runs up to a limit, so does
knowledge.

Crucially, **this is exactly the inference Dharmakīrti's strategy is built to
dethrone.** As a Buddhist he would deny the inference outright: that a quality
comes in degrees does not entail that an *existent* bearer of its maximum is
given (heat varies by degree without there being a hottest possible body; a
number can always be exceeded). The bare gradation, by itself, yields at most an
*asymptote* or *upper bound as a concept*, not a realized omniscient person.
Dharmakīrti's move is precisely to *supply the mechanism* the gradation argument
lacks — a cultivation process that actually transports the quality to its limit.
So the kāṣṭhāprāpti argument is not the parallel to the singularity; it is the
weaker, *pre-mechanistic* argument the singularity thesis (and Dharmakīrti)
improve upon.

### IV.2 The śāśvatika utkarṣa argument (bhāṣya ad YS 1.24)

The bhāṣya on YS 1.24 (*kleśakarmavipāka-āśayair aparāmṛṣṭaḥ puruṣaviśeṣa
īśvaraḥ*) first distinguishes Īśvara from ordinary liberated selves (*kevalin*):
they once had bondage and cut it; Īśvara *never* had it — *sadaiva muktaḥ
sadaiva īśvaraḥ*. Then the key passage (ll. 129–132):

> yo 'sau **prakṛṣṭasattva-upādānāt** īśvarasya **śāśvatika utkarṣaḥ** sa kiṃ
> sa-nimitta āhosvin nir-nimitta iti. **tasya śāstraṃ nimittam.** śāstraṃ punaḥ
> kiṃnimittam, **prakṛṣṭasattvanimittam.** etayoḥ **śāstra-utkarṣayoḥ** ...
> **anādiḥ saṃbandhaḥ**. ... tasmād **yatra kāṣṭhāprāptir aiśvaryasya sa īśvaraḥ**.

"That **perpetual pre-eminence (*śāśvatika utkarṣa*)** of Īśvara, arising **from
his appropriation of the most refined *sattva* (*prakṛṣṭa-sattva*)** — is it
caused or uncaused? **Its cause is scripture/knowledge (*śāstra*).** And what is
*śāstra*'s cause? **The most refined sattva.** Between these two — knowledge and
pre-eminence — both subsisting in Īśvara's being, the relation is
**beginningless (*anādi*)**..."

Note the structure. Here the maximum is *not* read off a bare gradation. It is
generated by a **feedback loop**:

> refined substrate (*prakṛṣṭasattva*) → knowledge (*śāstra*) → heightened
> excellence (*utkarṣa*) → still more refined substrate → ... (beginningless)

This is recursive self-improvement in the literal sense: *knowledge improves the
substrate, and the improved substrate yields more knowledge.* It is the same
loop as Dharmakīrti's "each increment becomes the nature and platforms the next"
(PV 2.125), and the same loop as the AI theorist's "more capable → better at
self-improvement → more capable." The PYŚ author even reuses the word
**utkarṣa**, the very term Dharmakīrti applies to perfected compassion (PV
2.131).

The bhāṣya then *appends* a kāṣṭhāprāpti clause ("therefore where lordship
reaches its culmination, he is Īśvara") — but here the culmination is the
*output* of the generative loop, not, as in IV.1, the conclusion of a
free-standing argument-from-degrees. The loop is doing the real work.

### IV.3 Why the śāśvatika utkarṣa argument is "the Dharmakīrtian one"

| | kāṣṭhāprāpti (ad 1.25) | śāśvatika utkarṣa (ad 1.24) | Dharmakīrti / singularity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Argument type | static, *from degrees* | dynamic, *from a generative loop* | dynamic, *from a mechanism* |
| Engine | *sātiśayatva*, analogy to magnitude | *sattva ↔ śāstra* mutual causation | cultivation / self-improvement on a stable substrate |
| What grounds the max | the bare existence of degrees | a self-reinforcing process | a self-reinforcing process |
| Vulnerable to "degrees ≠ realized maximum"? | **yes** | no | no |

The śāśvatika utkarṣa argument and Dharmakīrti's argument belong to the same
*family of proof* — the realizability/mechanism family — while the kāṣṭhāprāpti
argument belongs to the older, weaker, "gradation" family. That is the precise
sense in which "the latter is more similar to Dharmakīrti."

## Part V. Where the parallels break — and what the breaks teach

Honest comparison needs the disanalogies, and three of them are illuminating.

1. **Beginningless loop vs. temporal explosion.** The PYŚ defuses its own
   recursive engine by making the loop *anādi* (beginningless) and Īśvara
   *eternally already* perfect (*sadaiva īśvaraḥ*). The feedback never "runs";
   it is timelessly complete. This is the opposite of an *explosion* — there is
   no take-off event because there was no "before." **Dharmakīrti's is the
   genuinely temporal version**: his Buddha *becomes* perfect along a path, over
   a beginningless series of lives, with the qualities actually accruing. In
   that respect Dharmakīrti is closer to the singularity (a future *event*) than
   the PYŚ is — the PYŚ borrows the recursive *structure* but freezes it into
   eternity to secure a god who never needed to improve.

2. **No orthogonality: the engine is itself a value.** In the modern discussion
   (Bostrom's *orthogonality thesis*) the amplifying capacity is *value-neutral*
   — intelligence can be yoked to any goal, whence the alignment problem. In
   Dharmakīrti the quantity that compounds **is karuṇā** — compassion, a value,
   not bare cognitive horsepower — and the cognitive perfection *rides on* the
   affective cultivation (2.34: *sādhanaṃ karuṇābhyāsāt*). Dharmakīrti's
   intelligence explosion is *inseparably* a compassion explosion; there is no
   orthogonality and therefore no alignment problem internal to the model. The
   contrast sharpens what the AI debate is actually anxious about: a recursive
   process whose *direction* is not fixed by its *engine*. (The PYŚ sits in
   between: the engine is *prakṛṣṭasattva* + *śāstra*, cognitive-cum-luminous,
   and Īśvara's benevolence — *bhūtānugraha*, l. 138 — is asserted as a separate
   datum, not derived from the loop.)

3. **Substrate finitude.** The singularity's open question is whether the
   physical substrate (compute, energy, thermodynamics) imposes the external
   wall the *mechanism* lacks. Dharmakīrti faces the mirror-image question and
   answers it by metaphysics: the mind-stream is *beginningless and not
   body-dependent* (PV 2.34–36, *āśrayapratiṣedha*), so no material ceiling
   applies. Strip that metaphysics — make cognition substrate-dependent after
   all — and Dharmakīrti's argument collapses back toward the bounded "leaping"
   case. The whole proof rests on the immateriality and continuity of citta,
   exactly where a physicalist reading of minds (and of machines) would press.

## Part VI. Śāntarakṣita's refinement: the *Tattvasaṃgraha* sarvajña-chapter

The clearest classical statement of the "singularity-shaped" argument is not
Dharmakīrti's own compressed verses but Śāntarakṣita's *Atīndriyārthadarśiparīkṣā*
(the final chapter of the *Tattvasaṃgraha*, TS 3123–3646, 8th c.), with
Kamalaśīla's pañjikā. Here the dialectic is staged explicitly as
**bounded-domain skepticism vs. recursive cultivation** — exactly the shape of
the modern AI debate.

### VI.1 The opponent's case: capabilities are narrow and plateau (TS 3161–3169)

The Mīmāṃsaka pūrvapakṣa (Kumārila) argues against omniscience precisely the way
an AI-capability skeptic argues against the singularity: excellence in one
faculty does **not generalize** to others, and practice has **hard ceilings**.
(file: `santaraksita_tattvasangraha_atindriyartha.txt`)

> jñātvā vyākaraṇaṃ dūraṃ buddhiḥ śabda-apaśabdayoḥ /
> prakṛṣyate na **nakṣatra-tithi-grahaṇa-nirṇaye** // (TS 3165)
> ... daśahasta-antaraṃ vyomno yo nāma utplutya gacchati /
> na **yojanam asau gantuṃ śakto 'bhyāsa-śatair api** // (TS 3168)

"Having mastered grammar, the intellect is sharpened about correct and incorrect
words — *but not about determining the lunar mansions and dates*. ... One who can
leap ten cubits into the air cannot leap **a league, not even with a hundred
practices**" — therefore (TS 3169) extraordinary cognition extends a *little*
farther, but never reaches the supersensible (*atīndriya*).

Note that here **the leaping argument is in the skeptic's mouth.** This is the
ancestor of "narrow AI does not scale to general intelligence; benchmarks
saturate; returns diminish."

### VI.2 Śāntarakṣita's reply: stable substrate + compounding (TS 3411–3435)

Śāntarakṣita answers with the realizability/mechanism argument, sharpened well
beyond Dharmakīrti. The skeleton:

1. **Realizability from observed increase** (TS 3411): *prajñā-kṛpā-ādīnām
   abhyāsād vṛddhi-darśanāt* — because wisdom and compassion are *seen* to
   increase with practice, a further increase is inferred from the increasing
   quality. (Not "degrees exist" but "an actual increase is observed and
   extrapolated.")
2. **Mental qualities reach the uttermost limit** (TS 3412): *manoguṇatayāpy
   eṣāṃ **kāṣṭhā-paryanta-sambhavaḥ*** — *because* they are mental qualities,
   their reaching the **terminus** (kāṣṭhā-paryanta) is possible; great practice
   yields *niṣṭhā-aśeṣārtha-bodhana*, comprehension of all objects without
   remainder. The example chosen is jarring and deliberate: *nairghṛṇyavat* —
   "as with [the perfecting of] ruthlessness." Even cruelty can be cultivated
   without limit; the *mechanism is value-neutral* (see VI.3).
3. **Same-kind seeds ⇒ unlimited increase** (TS 3414): *samāna-jātīya-pūrva-bīja-
   pravṛttayaḥ ... atyanta-vṛddhi-dharmāṇaḥ **saṃskāra-utkarṣa-bhedataḥ*** — what
   grows from prior seeds of its own kind has the property of unlimited increase,
   through gradations of the *excellence of formative impressions*. (Again
   **utkarṣa** — the lexical thread back to PYŚ's *śāśvatika utkarṣa* and PV
   2.131's *karuṇotkarṣa*.) "Like rice from rice-seed" (*vrīhyādivat*, TS 3415),
   compassion and wisdom grow to *sarvadarśitā*, omniscience.
4. **The dual movement** (TS 3416–3420): qualities of *increase* (compassion,
   insight) rise to a maximum, while qualities of *decrease* (defilements, false
   cognition) are driven to **absolute elimination** in the presence of their
   antidote — *kaladhauta-mala-vat*, "like dross [removed] from gold." When the
   inner darkness is wholly removed, *jñānam avyāhataṃ mahat svātantryeṇa
   pravarteta sarvatra jñeya-maṇḍale* (TS 3420): great unobstructed knowledge
   operates **spontaneously over the entire field of the knowable**.
5. **The stable substrate, named** (TS 3421–3423, 3433): qualities resting on a
   *sthira-āśraya*, once instilled, need *no renewed effort* (*apunar-yatna*) and
   reach their *kāṣṭhā-paryanta* "like the purification of gold"
   (*śātakumbha-viśuddhivat*). And explicitly:

   > mānasānāṃ guṇānāṃ tu **cittasantatir āśrayaḥ** /
   > sādhāra-yogato vṛttān na kathaṃcin nivartate // (TS 3433)

   "The substrate of mental qualities is the **mind-continuum** (*citta-santati*),
   which, operating through a continuous retention [of impressions], **never
   ceases**." This is the persisting-substrate premise of the singularity, stated
   outright.

6. **Explicit positive feedback** (TS 3434): the recursive loop, where the
   *substrate itself* improves as qualities accumulate:

   > yāvad yāvad guṇa-augho 'syām ābhimukhyena vartate /
   > prabhāsvaratarā tāvat sutarām eva vartate // (TS 3434)

   "**The more** the flood of qualities operates toward the mind, **the more
   radiant** [and so the more capable] it becomes." More accumulated excellence →
   a better substrate → more excellence: this is recursive self-improvement in so
   many words, and it is *stronger* than Dharmakīrti, who emphasized the
   persistence of the gain but not the upgrading of the substrate by the gain.

### VI.3 The two contrast cases — plateau vs. take-off

Śāntarakṣita keeps Dharmakīrti's bounded case and adds a vivid take-off case,
giving the exact pair the singularity debate trades in:

- **Plateau (substrate does not retain gains)** — TS 3432:
  > **uṣṇatāṃ nīyamānasya kṣayo bhavati cāmbhasaḥ** /
  > **asthairyād āśrayasya** ataḥ kasya kasmin prakṛṣṭatā //
  "When water is heated its heat **decays**, because **its substrate is
  unstable**; so whose excellence could persist in what?" (A domain that resets to
  baseline — the hardware that cannot carry its own improvement forward.)
- **Take-off (substrate compounds practice)** — TS 3428–3430, the **royal-swan**:
  > **rājahaṃsa-śiśuḥ** śakto nirgantuṃ na gṛhād api /
  > yadi ca **abhyāsa-bhedena pāram ambhaḥpater api** // (TS 3428)
  > **āśrayopādhika-abhyāsa-bhedād** asya gatir yathā /
  > tādṛśī tādṛśād eva kiṃ na sambhāvyate 'dhikā // (TS 3429)
  "A cygnet of the royal goose cannot even leave the house, yet through gradations
  of practice [it flies] to the *far shore of the ocean*. As its range [grows]
  through practice conditioned on its substrate, why not a similar — indeed
  superior — [growth of cognition]?" (A domain that *does* take off; the
  bodhisattva who could not, after the distinguished samādhi, can — TS 3430.)
  Śāntarakṣita even concedes that *leaping itself* could become unbounded for a
  yogin endowed with *manojavā* ("mind-swift" power), TS 3425–3426 — i.e., the
  ceiling was never in the mechanism, only in the ordinary substrate.

### VI.4 Śāntarakṣita answers the orthogonality worry

Part V noted that the modern anxiety is *orthogonality*: amplification is
value-neutral, so the explosion's direction is unfixed. Remarkably,
Śāntarakṣita **concedes the value-neutrality of the mechanism** — *nairghṛṇyavat*
(TS 3412): ruthlessness, too, can be cultivated to its limit on the same
machinery — and then breaks the symmetry **metaphysically**, by the nature of the
substrate itself:

> **prabhāsvaram idaṃ cittaṃ tattvadarśana-sātmakam** /
> prakṛtyaiva sthitaṃ yasmān **malās tv āgantavo matāḥ** // (TS 3435)

"This mind is **naturally luminous, of the nature of seeing reality**; it is
established so by its very nature, whereas the defilements are **adventitious
(āgantuka)**." Because the ground state is already truth-aligned and the
distortions are removable add-ons, the *dual movement* of VI.2(4) is asymmetric:
the increase-qualities (which agree with the substrate's nature) have no ceiling,
while the decrease-qualities (which oppose it) have a floor of zero. This is, in
effect, a claim that **alignment is intrinsic to the substrate** — the precise
denial of orthogonality. Whether one accepts it turns entirely on the
*prakṛti-prabhāsvara* doctrine, exactly the contestable metaphysics flagged in
Part V.3.

### VI.5 Net effect

The *Tattvasaṃgraha* makes the comparison tighter than Dharmakīrti alone allows:
it stages the skeptic's bounded-domain argument (TS 3161–3169), rebuts it with an
explicit persisting-substrate-plus-positive-feedback model (TS 3433–3434),
supplies both a plateau case (heated water) and a take-off case (the swan), and
even confronts and answers a recognizable orthogonality objection (TS 3412 +
3435). It is the closest premodern analogue to the intelligence-explosion
argument that this corpus contains.

## One-paragraph summary

Dharmakīrti proves the *possibility* of a perfected knower not by noting that
knowledge comes in degrees, but by exhibiting a mechanism — compassion-cultivation
on the stable, continuous substrate of the mind, where each increment is retained
as the platform for the next, so that "with practice, whence any stopping?" This
is, structurally, the intelligence-explosion argument: recursive self-improvement
on a persisting substrate, with no internal ceiling, contrasted against bounded
"leaping" domains whose substrate cannot carry gains forward. The
*Pātañjalayogaśāstra* offers two proofs of Īśvara's omniscience, and only one of
them shares this skeleton: the *śāśvatika utkarṣa* argument (ad YS 1.24), with its
beginningless *prakṛṣṭasattva ↔ śāstra* feedback loop, is the Dharmakīrtian,
"singularity-shaped" one; the *kāṣṭhāprāpti* argument (ad YS 1.25) is the older
argument-from-degrees that the mechanism arguments are precisely designed to
surpass. Śāntarakṣita's *Tattvasaṃgraha* (ch. 26) sharpens the whole picture to
its closest premodern form: it stages the skeptic's bounded-domain objection,
rebuts it with an explicit *cittasantati*-as-persisting-substrate plus
positive-feedback model ("the more qualities accrue, the more radiant the mind"),
pairs a plateau case (heated water) with a take-off case (the royal swan), and
even meets a recognizable orthogonality objection by grounding alignment in the
mind's natural luminosity. The instructive disanalogies — the PYŚ freezing its
loop into eternity, the absence of any orthogonality between intelligence and
value in the Buddhist version (the engine *is* compassion, and the substrate is
truth-aligned by nature), and the dependence of the whole proof on an immaterial,
body-independent substrate — are where the comparison earns its keep.
