# The Structure of Dharmottara's Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi (D4253)

**Report date:** 2026-06-11
**Basis:** complete reading of the Tibetan translation, Derge Tengyur, tshad ma,
vol. ZHE, fols. 249b1–259a7 (local witness: `TD4253E.TXT`, 173 e-text lines).
Sanskrit lost; the Tibetan is the sole complete witness.
**Commentary:** Muktākalaśa, *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhivivaraṇa* (D4254, same volume),
useful as a structural control.
**Classic study:** E. Frauwallner, "Dharmottaras Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhiḥ: Text und
Übersetzung," WZKM 42 (1935) — cite for the German translation; the analysis
below is from the Tibetan text directly.

---

## 0. Identification

| | |
|---|---|
| Sanskrit title (transliterated in the Tibetan) | *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi* |
| Tibetan title | *sKad cig ma 'jig pa grub pa* |
| Author (colophon, 259a) | *slob dpon Chos mchog* = Ācārya **Dharmottara**, praised as "a second [Dharmakīrti] among the deeds of the Victorious One on earth," "whose mind was brought to full development by the supreme explanations of the glorious Ācārya Dharmakīrti (*dpal slob dpon Chos kyi grags pa*)" |
| Translators (colophon) | the Indian upādhyāya **Skal ldan rgyal po** (*Bhāgyarāja) and the lotsāba dge slong **Blo ldan shes rab** (rNgog lo tsā ba, 1059–1109) |
| Extent | ~9.5 Derge folios; a short, self-contained *prakaraṇa* (the opening of Muktākalaśa's Vivaraṇa says it was composed "with brief wording so that it may be grasped easily") |

The work is a monograph defence of **the inference of momentariness from
existence** (*sattvānumāna*: whatever exists is momentary). Its design is a
single large *pūrvapakṣa–uttarapakṣa* arc: Dharmottara first lets the opponent
state a connected battery of objections, then answers them in the same order,
and only **at the very end** states the formal proof whose viability the whole
preceding discussion has secured. The hammer-and-sherds analysis and the
refutation of recognition (*pratyabhijñā*) — the two showpieces of the work —
are both embedded in the reply section.

---

## 1. Structural outline

### I. Thesis (249b1)

> *'dus byas thams cad ni skad cig ma'o zhes kha cig gis rtogs so*
> "Some [the Buddhists] maintain: all conditioned things are momentary."

One sentence; the rest of the work defends it.

### II. Pūrvapakṣa — the opponent's connected indictment (249b–251a)

Eight objections, escalating from semantics to logic to perception to common
sense:

| § | Folio | Objection | Key Tibetan |
|---|-------|-----------|-------------|
| P1 | 249b | The very word *kṣaṇa/kṣaṇika* is indefinable on Buddhist premises: if "momentary" = "connected with a moment," the referent of *kṣaṇa* cannot be fixed; if it = "perishing immediately upon arising," the thing must still *last* through the causal assembly | *skad cig gi sgras brjod par bya ba'i don… rnam par gzhag par mi nus* |
| P2 | 249b–250a | **No example** (*dṛṣṭānta*) is available for the proof: the stock examples (cognition, flame) are themselves doubtful as to momentariness, so they cannot exhibit the pervasion | *blo dang me lce la sogs pa dpe'i dngos por nye bar blangs pa dag ni… the tsom za bas* |
| P3 | 250a | The hetu "being existent qua causally efficacious" (*don bya ba byed pa'i rang bzhin yod pa* = arthakriyā-sattva) is **inconclusive** (*anaikāntika*): a non-momentary thing could produce effects gradually, relying on cooperating causes — just as the hammer (*tho ba*, mudgara), without producing any new property in the pot, nevertheless destroys it | *tho ba la sogs pa nye bas de zlog par byed… ma nges pa'i gtan tsigs ltar snang* |
| P4 | 250a–250b | The hetu is also **unestablished** (*asiddha*): the "existence" actually established by perception is existence *as non-momentary*; pure existence is established by no pramāṇa | *yod pa dag pa nye bar 'god na ni… ma grub pas ma grub po* |
| P5 | 250b | **Perception contradicts** momentariness (*pratyakṣabādhā*): the recognitional perception "this is a cow," which grasps the convergence (*'du ba*) of the vivid moments as non-distinct, follows the senses and cannot be denied perceptual status, nor be objectless | *mngon sum gyis gnod do… ba lang zhes bya ba de lta bu'i rnam pa can* |
| P6 | 250b–251a | **Inference contradicts** it: where utter difference obtains, memory and recognition are impossible — Devadatta's seeing does not give Yajñadatta recognition; since recognition does occur, what was seen before and what is seen now must be one object (*vyāpakaviruddhopalabdhi* excluding difference: *khyab par byed pa 'gal ba dmigs pas tha dad pa sel*) | *lha sbyin mthong ba dang ldan pa ni mchod sbyin la… gzhan gyi dran pa ma yin no* |
| P7 | 251a | Social-act corollary: entrusting, dedicating, promising (*bcol ba dang bsngos pa dang smras pa*) presuppose the same continuing person — the momentary "many" is contradicted | |
| P8 | 251a | **Common consensus** (*grags pa*, prasiddhi) contradicts it: the whole world knows things endure for some time | *'gro ba mtha' dag gi grags pas kyang… gnod do* |

The pūrvapakṣa closes: *des na skad cig ma nyid ni sgrub par mi nus so zhe na* —
"therefore momentariness cannot be established — if this is argued…"

### III. Uttarapakṣa — replies in order (251a–258a)

Opens with the marker *'di la lan nyon cig* ("hear the reply to this," 251a).

**R1. The word *kṣaṇa* (251a–251b)** — answers P1.
Worldly and astronomical usage (*'jig rten dang skar ma shes pa'i gtzug lag* =
jyotiḥśāstra) shows *kṣaṇa* denotes a minute stretch of time; technically, a
time that admits no earlier/later division (*snga phyi'i cha gnyis dang bral
ba*) is the *kṣaṇa*; the word is therefore perfectly intelligible.

**R2. Example and pervasion (251b–252b)** — answers P2 and P4; doctrinally the
heart of the work.
- An example works through mere similarity and can never by itself transfer the
  probandum; what establishes the conclusion is the **pramāṇa that establishes
  the pervasion** (*khyab pa sgrub pa'i tsad ma*, vyāpti-sādhaka-pramāṇa).
- Once that pramāṇa is displayed, even the example is proven momentary; and the
  pervasion may be established **on the very subject of the proof**
  (*bsgrub par bya ba'i chos can nyid la*) — the celebrated Dharmottaran move.
- The pervasion-prover is the **annulling cognition** (*gnod par byed pa'i
  tsad ma*, bādhakapramāṇa): "if it were permanent, it could not engage in
  causal efficacy either successively or simultaneously."
- Scope: all entities, seen and unseen, fall under it (252a); pervasion is
  established "even without sound [the subject]," like pervasion being known
  without the rabbit's horn; the subject's existence is supplied by another
  pramāṇa, so *pakṣadharmatā* still has its role (252a–b).

**R3. Destruction, the hammer, and cooperating causes (252b–255b)** — answers
P3; the *vināśitvānumāna* complex.
- *252b–253a:* the hammer cannot act on the entity itself; if "destruction"
  were a further entity produced, the destroyed pot should still be there
  performing its function. Non-existence (*dngos po med pa*) is nothing
  producible — analysis of abhāva, with the excursus (253a–b) that anything
  conceptually determinable would appear to an omniscient cognition
  (*thams cad mkhyen pa'i ye shes*), and whatever is causally idle is no entity.
- *253b–254a:* trilemma on the destructible — born with a self-ceasing nature,
  or with a staying nature? If it stays "a while," the same question recurs at
  the end of that while: **infinite regress; the thing would never perish and
  is in effect admitted permanent** (*dngos po nam yang mi 'jig par 'gyur bas
  tsul 'di dngos po rtag par khas blang bar 'gyur ro*).
- *254a–254b:* the seed/sprout counter-analogy dismantled; then the positive
  Sautrāntika analysis of the hammer: hammer etc. are concomitant
  (*rjes su 'gro ba dang ldog pa*, anvaya-vyatireka) **only with the arising of
  the sherd-state**: "that the sherd-state arises from the pot, whose
  substratum-continuity (*nye bar len pa*, upādāna) depends on the hammer etc.
  as cooperating [conditions] — that is our position" (*kho bo cag gi phyogs
  yin no*, 254b). Destruction itself needs no cause.
- *254b–255b:* do momentary saṃskāras then admit cooperating causes at all?
  Two kinds of effects distinguished (those arising through a chain of
  perfected distinctions vs. those not so dependent); seed–field–sprout
  analysis; "awakening of the potency" (*nus pa sad pa*) has two causes
  (sahakārin and *nye bar 'du ba'i rkyen*, upasarpaṇapratyaya), the latter of
  two kinds (newly arisen, arrived from elsewhere). Conclusion: among momentary
  things nothing is "helped" (*phan 'dogs par byed pa ma yin*); cooperation is
  reanalysed without enduring substrata.

**R4. The hetu neither unestablished nor contradictory (255b–256b)** — answers
P4's residue and the charge (raised 255b) that "existent" entails association
with the permanent.
- Theory of pramāṇa as **obtainer** (*thob par byed pa* = prāpaka): its whole
  work terminates in directing a person to an object; perception shows, the
  determination (*zhen pa*, adhyavasāya) engages, inference ascertains
  (256a).
- Determination operates on **one exclusion at a time** (*ldog pa gcig*,
  ekavyāvṛtti): "permanent" = excluded-from-impermanent; "existent" =
  excluded-from-causally-idle. These are objects of **different**
  pramāṇa-operations, hence no single cognition certifies a "permanent
  existent": the hetu is taken as bare *sattva* established by perception
  (256a–b).

**R5. Recognition (*ngo shes pa* = pratyabhijñā) refuted (256b–258a)** —
answers P5–P7; the section closest to the polemics of frag.txt.
- *256b–257a:* what is "grasped by perception" is only what the determination
  born of perception's force says was grasped. The traveller example: a man
  from another continent (*gling gzhan nas 'ongs pa*) is taught "this is an ox,
  do not kick it"; seeing *another* ox elsewhere, he recognises "this is that
  very ox I was forbidden to kick." Recognition thus arises even toward
  **distinct lumps** (*gong bu tha dad pa dag la yang don gcig tu 'dzin pa'i
  ngo shes pa*) — so it is objectless, like the cognition of a man with
  timira, and unfit to be a perceptual pramāṇa.
- *257a–257b:* nor does a universal rescue it: recognition determines a vivid
  particular qualified by colour, shape, birth, decay — never a separate
  *sāmānya*; a cognition with continuity-form would have to grasp what its own
  vivid content excludes.
- *257b:* the decisive analysis of the form "**that very one is this**"
  (*de nyid 'di yin no*, tad evedam): the *tad* grasps something **hidden**
  (*lkog tu gyur pa*, parokṣa), the *idam* something **perceived** (*mngon
  sum*); recognition merely **conjoins** the two (*mtsams sbyor ba*,
  pratisandhāna), as Devadatta and Yajñadatta are "one" with respect to some
  joint effect: "recognition fuses the distinct into one; it does not perceive
  a non-distinct nature" (*ngo shes pas tha dad pa dag gcig tu sre bar byed pa
  yin gyi tha dad pa med pa'i rang bzhin dmigs pa ni ma yin*).
- *258a:* hence recognition is superimposition (*sgro 'dogs*) driven by
  practical purpose, objectless, and no perception. The inference of P6
  collapses (difference is no object of cognition, so the pervasion fails);
  and memory within one continuum (*rgyud gcig gi khongs su gtogs pa*) is
  fully compatible with momentariness — answering P6–P7. P8 (prasiddhi) is
  left to fall with them.

### IV. The formal proof (258a–259a)

Announced: *bsal zin pa 'di dag la skad cig 'jig par sgrub pa'i tsad ma nyon
cig* — "the refutations done, hear the pramāṇa that establishes momentary
destruction."

- **Syllogism (258a):** "Whatever exists is impermanent, like a pot; and the
  disputed sound etc. do exist" (*gang yod pa de thams cad ni mi rtag ste,
  dper na bum pa la sogs pa bzhin no, rtzod pa'i gnas su gyur pa'i sgra la
  sogs pa yang yod pa*).
- **Pervasion (258a–259a):** existence = causal efficacy, exhaustively divided
  into **successive or simultaneous** operation (*rim dang cig car*); each mode
  is perceptually attested as it is, no third mode exists; a non-momentary
  entity is incompatible with both (whatever it could do in the second moment
  it must already do in the first, so all its effects would arise at once —
  *skad cig dang por yang skye bar 'gyur*); cooperating conditions cannot
  relieve it (back-reference to R3).
- **Conclusion (259a):** since nothing pervades the vipakṣa (the
  non-momentary), by its exclusion *sattva* is pervaded by momentariness —
  "this is a *svabhāvahetu*" (*rang bzhin gyi gtan tsigs so*). End of treatise.

### V. Colophon (259a)

Author eulogy (Dharmottara as the great logician destroying the timira of bad
views, second only to Dharmakīrti, adorned with discipline and the armour of
holy composition); translation credit to *Bhāgyarāja (Skal ldan rgyal po) and
rNgog Blo ldan shes rab.

## 2. Architectural observations

1. **One arc, not chapters.** Unlike Ratnakīrti's *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi* (split
   into anvayātmikā/vyatirekātmikā) or the TS/TSP *sthirabhāvaparīkṣā* (verse
   by verse), Dharmottara's prakaraṇa is a single debate: an eight-point
   pūrvapakṣa mirrored by a five-block reply, capped by the proof. The reply
   order tracks the objection order almost exactly (P1→R1, P2+P4→R2, P3→R3,
   P4→R4, P5–P8→R5).

2. **The proof comes last.** The sattvānumāna is *stated* only after every
   instrument it needs (meaning of *kṣaṇa*, dispensability of the example,
   vyāpti-on-the-sādhyadharmin via bādhakapramāṇa, causeless destruction,
   exclusion-semantics of the hetu, disarmed pratyabhijñā) has been secured.
   The treatise is in effect a defence of the proof's *possibility*, with the
   proof itself as a one-folio coda.

3. **Two signature doctrines** organise the middle: (a) the
   **bādhakapramāṇa establishing vyāpti on the very subject** (R2) — the move
   for which Dharmottara's kṣaṇabhaṅga argument is known; (b) the
   ***tad evedam* analysis of recognition** as conjunction of a parokṣa and a
   pratyakṣa element (R5).

4. **Sautrāntika vināśa doctrine** (R3) is argued in the same trilemma style as
   the frag.txt folio (hammer → sherd-state; destruction needs no cause;
   "stays-a-while" regress), which is why D4253 254a–b and 256b–258a are the
   closest known parallels to that fragment (see
   `output/reconstructions/frag.txt.md`, parallels T1–T3).

5. **Relation to the Vivaraṇa.** Muktākalaśa (D4254) confirms the divisions:
   his opening flags the work's purpose as "refuting [the view] that the
   pramāṇa establishing momentary destruction has a [defective] basis" and
   immediately addresses *grangs can la sogs pa* (Sāṃkhya etc.) as the
   opponents — indicating that the tradition read the pūrvapakṣa as a composite
   Sāṃkhya/Naiyāyika position.

## 4. Citations and authorities

The body of the treatise **cites no named authority** — no scripture, no
author, no verse quotation (checked against all citation markers: *ji skad
du*, *zhes gsungs*, *lung*, *zhal snga nas*, *slob dpon*). The *slob dpon* of
the ox example (256b) is a generic instructor, not a cited master. The only
appeal to outside texts is generic and lexical: ordinary usage and "the
treatises of the astronomers" (*'jig rten dang skar ma shes pa'i gtzug lag*,
loka + jyotiḥśāstra) for the meaning of *kṣaṇa* (R1). **Dharmakīrti is named
only in the author colophon** (259a), as eulogy ("whose mind was brought to
full development by the supreme explanations of the glorious Ācārya
Dharmakīrti"), not as citation. Implicitly, however, the whole work reworks
Dharmakīrti's sattvānumāna (Hetubindu / Vādanyāya / PVin formulations: the
final syllogism and the krama-yaugapadya bādhakapramāṇa) without
acknowledgment — the normal manner of an independent *prakaraṇa*, which argues
by *yukti* alone; the proof-texting habit appears instead in the commentary
(Muktākalaśa's Vivaraṇa quotes verses).
