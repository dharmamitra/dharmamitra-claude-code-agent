# Is the svalakṣaṇa the *paramārthasat*, and was Dharmakīrti the first to say so?

**Two questions on the ultimate reality of the particular in the Dignāga–Dharmakīrti tradition**

*Compiled 2026-06-17. Sources: DharmaMitra `/primary/` canonical corpus (Pramāṇavārttika and its commentaries, Abhidharmakośa, and citing works); supplemented by the standard secondary literature on pramāṇavāda.*

---

## 0. The questions

The user's two claims, to be tested:

1. For Dharmakīrti, the **svalakṣaṇa** — the structural counterpart of what other schools call the *paramaviśeṣa* / *antya-viśeṣa* (the ultimate particular) — is the **paramārthasat**, and the realization of it is **mokṣa**. Is this correct?
2. Was **Dharmakīrti the first** to emphasize the *ultimate reality* of the svalakṣaṇa?

**Theses established below.**
(1a) svalakṣaṇa = paramārthasat: **correct and textually explicit** (PV 3.3). (1b) The *paramaviśeṣa* analogy holds at the level of "the irreducible particular" but **inverts on permanence**. (1c) "Realization of svalakṣaṇa = mokṣa" is **correct as a structural synthesis** but is not Dharmakīrti's explicit formula — his stated soteriological object is *nairātmya* / the removal of *satkāyadṛṣṭi* (PV 2).
(2) **No** — the centrality of svalakṣaṇa is **Dignāga's**, and the term plus the svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa pair is **Abhidharmic**; but the specific move of ontologizing svalakṣaṇa as *paramārthasat* via *arthakriyā* and *kṣaṇikatva* **is Dharmakīrti's signature contribution**.

---

## 1. svalakṣaṇa = paramārthasat: the locus classicus

The defining verse is **Pramāṇavārttika, *pratyakṣa-pariccheda* 3** (cited below as PV 3.3; see §5 on chapter numbering):

> *arthakriyāsamarthaṃ yat tad atra paramārthasat /*
> *anyat saṃvṛtisat proktaṃ te svasāmānyalakṣaṇe //*
> "What is capable of telic efficacy (*arthakriyā*) is here the ultimately real; the other is called conventionally real — these two being the particular (svalakṣaṇa) and the universal (sāmānyalakṣaṇa) [respectively]."

Witnesses retrieved (the verse survives chiefly through quotation; the running PV Sanskrit and the commentaries both transmit it):

- **Vajrayānadarśanamīmāṃsā** (`SA_T16_n2959u:755`) — quotes it verbatim and labels it *pratyakṣaparicchedaḥ 1 ślokaḥ 3*, and — crucially — places it immediately after the Abhidharmakośa two-truths verse (see §4): *...pramāṇavārtike cāpi — arthakriyāsamarthaṃ yat tad atra paramārthasat...*
  <https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_T16_n2959u/text?active_segment=SA_T16_n2959u:755>
- **Durveka Miśra, Dharmottarapradīpa** (`SA_GSP28_durdhpru:11897`) — quotes the verse and immediately follows it with the parallel formulation *sa pāramārthiko bhāvo ya evārthakriyākṣamaḥ* (see §1.1).
  <https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_GSP28_durdhpru/text?active_segment=SA_GSP28_durdhpru:11897>
- **Ratnakīrti, Vādarahasya** (`SA_T11_rvadrahu:1587`) — *...yat tad eva paramārthasat / anyat saṃvṛtisat proktaṃ te svasāmānyalakṣaṇe / ity arthaḥ*.
  <https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_T11_rvadrahu/text?active_segment=SA_T11_rvadrahu:1587>

The chain of identity is: **paramārthasat ≡ arthakriyāsamartha** (capable of causal/telic efficacy) ≡ the **svalakṣaṇa**, which Dharmakīrti further identifies with the **kṣaṇa** (the momentary). Its complement, the sāmānyalakṣaṇa, is a mental construct (the *apoha*, a conceptual exclusion) and is merely *saṃvṛtisat*. So:

> svalakṣaṇa = arthakriyāsamartha = kṣaṇika = **paramārthasat**
> sāmānyalakṣaṇa = vikalpa/apoha = **saṃvṛtisat**

### 1.1 The same equation restated in the pratyakṣa chapter

The criterion recurs in the chapter's analysis of causal cooperation and momentariness — **PV 3.166** (Karṇakagomin numbers it 168):

> *...ekāpāye phalābhāvād viśeṣebhyas tad udbhavaḥ /*
> *sa pāramārthiko bhāvo ya evārthakriyākṣamaḥ //*
> "...because, on the loss of [even] one [cooperating factor], the effect is absent, [the effect] arises from the particulars (viśeṣas). **That entity alone is ultimately real which is capable of telic efficacy.**"

- **Pramāṇavārttika** (`SA_T11_pramanvu:1991`), PV 3.164–166.
  <https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_T11_pramanvu/text?active_segment=SA_T11_pramanvu:1991>
- **Karṇakagomin, Pramāṇavārttikavṛttiṭīkā** (`SA_T11_kgpramv:9692`), glossing v. 168: *idam eva hi vastv-avastu-lakṣaṇam — arthakriyāyogyatā'yogyate... arthakriyāsamartho yogyo 'rthaḥ... tasmād vastu-viśeṣa eva* — "this very [capacity] is the mark of the real vs. the unreal: fitness/unfitness for telic efficacy... hence [the real] is just the particular (vastu-viśeṣa)."
  <https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_T11_kgpramv/text?active_segment=SA_T11_kgpramv:9692>

So the identification is not a one-off epigram: *arthakriyā* is Dharmakīrti's standing criterion of the real, and it selects the particular over the universal in both his ontology (PV 3.3) and his causal theory (PV 3.166).

**Verdict on (1a): correct, and as explicit as Dharmakīrti ever is.**

## 2. The *paramaviśeṣa* analogy

The user's framing — svalakṣaṇa as the counterpart of what others call the *paramaviśeṣa* — is apt as a structural comparison, with one decisive caveat.

- **The shared slot.** In Vaiśeṣika, the *antya-viśeṣa*s ("ultimate differentiators," the category *viśeṣa*) are what render the eternal substances (atoms, selves, ākāśa, manas) ultimately distinct from one another — they are, by definition, the floor of particularity below which no further differentiation is possible. The svalakṣaṇa occupies exactly this slot in Dharmakīrti's system: the *vyāvṛtti*-bearing, irreducibly unique particular. Dharmakīrti even uses the vocabulary of *viśeṣa* for the real (PV 3.166: *vastu-viśeṣa eva*).
- **The inversion.** The contents are opposite on the axis that matters most to Dharmakīrti. The Vaiśeṣika viśeṣa is **eternal** and inheres in **permanent** substrates; Dharmakīrti's svalakṣaṇa is real **precisely because it is momentary** (*kṣaṇika*) — for him, only the impermanent can be causally efficacious (*yat sat tat kṣaṇikam*). So the comparison illuminates the **role** (the ultimate particular) while reversing the **ontology** (eternal substance vs. momentary event).

**Verdict on (1b): a good comparandum for "the ultimate particular," provided the permanence inversion is flagged.**

## 3. "Realization of svalakṣaṇa = mokṣa": true in structure, but mind the explicit object

Dharmakīrti's actual soteriology is set out in **PV 2 (Pramāṇasiddhi)**, and it is framed not in terms of svalakṣaṇa but in terms of the **self**. The root of saṃsāra is *ātmasneha* / *satkāyadṛṣṭi*; the antidote is *nairātmyadarśana* matured, through *bhāvanā*, into vivid yogic perception (*yogipratyakṣa*):

> *...tena ātmābhiniveśo yāvat tāvat sa saṃsāre /*
> *ātmani sati parasaṃjñā svaparavibhāgāt parigrahadveṣau /*
> *anayoḥ sampratibaddhāḥ sarve doṣāḥ prajāyante //*
> "...as long as there is clinging to a self, so long is one in saṃsāra. **When there is a self, there is the notion of an 'other'; from the self/other division come grasping and aversion; bound up with these two, all the faults arise.**"

- **Pramāṇavārttika** (`SA_T11_pramanvu:443`), here labelled PV 1.220–221 (= the conventionally cited **PV 2.218–219**; see §5).
  <https://dharmamitra.org/nexus/db//sa/SA_T11_pramanvu/text?active_segment=SA_T11_pramanvu:443>
- Cited as a touchstone across traditions: **Bhaṭṭākalaṅkadeva, Laghīyastraya** (`SA_GR12_laghiyastrau:35692`) and **Akalaṅka, Siddhiviniścaya** (`SA_GR12_siddhivinisu:16789`) both quote the verse — a Jaina reception confirming its status as *the* Dharmakīrtian statement of the cause of bondage.

**Why the user's formulation is nonetheless defensible.** The self, permanence, and persisting wholes that *satkāyadṛṣṭi* reifies all belong to the **sāmānyalakṣaṇa / vikalpa** order — they are conceptual superimpositions, *saṃvṛti*. Reality as it actually is (momentary, causally streaming, ownerless) is the **svalakṣaṇa** order. Liberating gnosis therefore *takes the form of* a non-conceptual (*nirvikalpaka*) direct perception of the real — i.e., of the svalakṣaṇa — whose operative content is selflessness. In the higher, epistemic-idealist register of the system (John Dunne's "sliding scale of analysis"), the truly ultimate svalakṣaṇa is the self-luminous moment of awareness (*svasaṃvedana*), and the path culminates in non-dual *jñāna* free of the grāhya/grāhaka split.

So: **"realizing the svalakṣaṇa is liberation" is a sound synthesis of the system**, on the gloss "liberating insight is the non-conceptual perception of the real (= the selfless, momentary particular)." It is **not**, however, a slogan Dharmakīrti states in those words; his explicit liberating object is *nairātmya*, with svalakṣaṇa supplying the *epistemic form* (vivid, non-conceptual) that the insight must take.

**Verdict on (1c): correct as structural synthesis; not a verbatim Dharmakīrtian formula. State the object as *nairātmya*, with svalakṣaṇa-perception as its mode.**

## 4. Was Dharmakīrti the first? — Three claims, three answers

The question must be split, because "emphasize the ultimate reality of svalakṣaṇa" bundles three separable moves.

### (a) The term and the svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa pair — **Abhidharma, not Dharmakīrti**

The pair is Sarvāstivāda/Vaibhāṣika vocabulary: each *dharma* is characterized by its **svalakṣaṇa** (own-mark — e.g. the hardness, *kakkhaḷatta*, of the earth-element) as against the **sāmānyalakṣaṇa**s, the *shared* marks (anitya, duḥkha, śūnya, anātman) contemplated on the path. Equally, the **paramārthasat / saṃvṛtisat** two-truths predates Dharmakīrti. The Abhidharmakośa's definition is **AK 6.4**, retrieved beside PV 3.3 in the Vajrayānadarśanamīmāṃsā (`SA_T16_n2959u:755`):

> *yatra bhinne na tad-buddhir anyāpohe dhiyā ca tat /*
> *ghaṭāmbuvat saṃvṛtisat paramārthasad anyathā //*
> "That regarding which, when it is broken — or when [its parts] are mentally removed by the intellect — the cognition [of it] no longer arises, is conventionally real, like a pot or water; otherwise it is ultimately real."

Note that the *criterion* here is **analytical irreducibility**, not *arthakriyā*. So Dharmakīrti inherits both the svalakṣaṇa pair and the two-truths frame, but re-defines the latter.

### (b) svalakṣaṇa as the central object of perception — **Dignāga, not Dharmakīrti**

**Dignāga, Pramāṇasamuccaya 1.2** makes the svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa pair the *objects of the two pramāṇas*: there are exactly two means of knowledge because the knowable has just two characters — *pratyakṣa* grasps the **svalakṣaṇa** (the unique, inexpressible *anirdeśya* particular), *anumāna* the **sāmānyalakṣaṇa**; "there is no object other than these two" (PS 1.2, *svalakṣaṇa-sāmānya-lakṣaṇābhyām...*; the PS Sanskrit is largely lost and is cited per Steinkellner's reconstruction). The *architectural centrality* of svalakṣaṇa is thus Dignāga's achievement.

### (c) svalakṣaṇa as *paramārthasat*, via *arthakriyā* and momentariness — **this is Dharmakīrti's**

What Dignāga left as an *epistemic* category (the object of perception), Dharmakīrti **ontologized**: he made causal efficacy (*arthakriyā*) the criterion of the real (PV 3.3, 3.166), equated the efficacious with the momentary particular (*kṣaṇikatva*), and fused the inherited Abhidharma two-truths onto the Dignāgan svalakṣaṇa. The result — "the svalakṣaṇa *is* the paramārthasat, *because* it alone has arthakriyā" — is not found, in that form, before him.

**Verdict on (2):** No, Dharmakīrti did not make svalakṣaṇa central (Dignāga did, on Abhidharmic foundations). But if "emphasize the *ultimate reality* of svalakṣaṇa" means the *arthakriyā*-based ontologization specifically, **that is genuinely his signature contribution.**

## 5. Note on chapter numbering

The PV chapter order and numbering vary by edition, and the witnesses retrieved here reflect that:

- **arthakriyāsamarthaṃ yat...** — *pratyakṣa* chapter, verse 3 (Vajrayānadarśanamīmāṃsā: *pratyakṣaparicchedaḥ 1 ślokaḥ 3*). Conventionally **PV 3.3** in editions ordering the chapters Svārthānumāna (1) – Pramāṇasiddhi (2) – Pratyakṣa (3) – Parārthānumāna (4).
- **sa pāramārthiko bhāvo ya evārthakriyākṣamaḥ** — *pratyakṣa* chapter; the GRETIL running text (`SA_T11_pramanvu`) labels it **3.166**, Karṇakagomin **168**.
- **ātmani sati parasaṃjñā...** — *Pramāṇasiddhi* chapter; cited in the secondary literature as **PV 2.218–219** (Vetter/Franco), but the GRETIL file labels the run **1.220–221**, i.e. that digitization numbers Pramāṇasiddhi as chapter 1.

When citing, prefer the segment IDs and `src_link`s above as the audit trail, since the chapter labels are not stable across editions.

## 6. One-paragraph answer

Yes: for Dharmakīrti the svalakṣaṇa *is* the *paramārthasat*, by the explicit definition that only the telically efficacious (*arthakriyāsamartha*) — i.e. the momentary particular — is ultimately real, the universal being mere *saṃvṛtisat* (PV 3.3, restated PV 3.166). The *paramaviśeṣa* comparison is apt for "the ultimate particular" but inverts on permanence, since Dharmakīrti's particular is real because momentary. "Realization of svalakṣaṇa = mokṣa" is a sound synthesis — liberating gnosis is the non-conceptual perception of the real — but his *explicit* soteriological object is *nairātmya* and the uprooting of *satkāyadṛṣṭi* (PV 2.218–219), with svalakṣaṇa-perception supplying the form that insight takes. And no, he was not the first to foreground svalakṣaṇa: the term and the svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa pair are Abhidharmic, and their epistemological centrality is Dignāga's (PS 1.2); what is distinctively Dharmakīrti's is the *ontologization* of the svalakṣaṇa as paramārthasat through *arthakriyā* and *kṣaṇikatva*.

## 7. Transmission geography: the identification is recorded in Tibetan, absent in Chinese

A corpus probe of the Tibetan and Chinese canons confirms the priority claim of §6 from a second angle — *where* the identification survives. It survives precisely and only where Dharmakīrti was transmitted.

### 7.1 Tibetan — recorded in full, Dharmakīrti its origin

PV 3.3 in Tibetan (*rnam 'grel*) is ubiquitous:
> *don byed nus pa gang yin de / 'dir ni don dam yod pa yin / gzhan ni kun rdzob yod pa ste / de dag rang spyi'i mtshan nyid*

- **Tengyur commentary**: a canonical *Pramāṇavārttika* commentary on the *pratyakṣa* chapter (`BO_T11_D4225`) glosses it explicitly — *de nyid ni **don dam pa** ste ma bcos pa'i rang bzhin gyis dngos por yod pa'o / gang zhig **don byed mi nus pa** de ni gzhan te **kun rdzob** brtags nas yod pa zhes brjod do.*
- **Indigenous *tshad ma*** carries *rang mtshan = don dam bden pa / spyi mtshan = kun rdzob bden pa* as standard doctrine across schools: Sakya *Rigs gter* tradition (`BO_S04_TSEMA-142`: *don dam par don byed nus pa'i chos de rang mtshan gyi mtshan nyid … rang mtshan don dam bden pa*), dGe-lugs *yig cha / grub mtha'* (`BO_ACIP-SUNGBUM_S0210M_T:427`), *grub mtha'* surveys (`BO_S09_RONT055:2409`), and an Abhidharmasamuccaya commentary that juxtaposes the AKBh *paramārtha* (*gzugs lta bu*) with the PV criterion (`BO_S05_NGONPA-108:6847`).
- Within the Tibetan record **no pre-Dharmakīrti figure** makes it: Dignāga (transmitted as the *Tshad ma kun btus* = PS) supplies the *rang mtshan/spyi mtshan* pair as objects of the two pramāṇas but does not raise svalakṣaṇa to *don dam* via efficacy.

### 7.2 Chinese — absent, only the unfused precursors

**Dharmakīrti was never translated into classical Chinese**; the 因明 (hetuvidyā) lineage stops at **Dignāga** (陳那) and Śaṅkarasvāmin's *Nyāyapraveśa*. So the svalakṣaṇa = *arthakriyā* = paramārtha fusion is not in the Chinese canon. Present are only its components, never combined:

- **Abhidharma two-truths by analytical irreducibility** — Saṃghabhadra's *Nyāyānusāra* (阿毘達磨順正理論, `ZH_T29_1562_058:0666b05`): *若於多物施設為有名為世俗，但於一物施設為有名為勝義* — paramārtha = what survives analysis (= AKBh 6.4). Criterion = **irreducibility (破析), not efficacy**; bearer = *dharma/dravya*, not the Dignāgan svalakṣaṇa. (Saṃghabhadra's separate "the real is what generates a cognition" is the closest Chinese-recorded *near-miss* to *arthakriyā*, but it is a criterion of *dravyasat*, not yoked to svalakṣaṇa.)
- **Yogācāra 勝義有** = the perfected nature (圓成實) / suchness (成唯識論了義燈 `ZH_T43_1832`; 華嚴演義鈔 `ZH_T36_1736`) — paramārtha is true reality, not the perceptual particular; in Chinese Yogācāra the 自相 grasped by 現量 sits at the *paratantra* level, *below* 勝義.
- **Dignāga's 自相/共相** as objects of 現量/比量 (因明入正理論疏 `ZH_T44_1840`) — present, but never identified with 勝義有.

### 7.3 Upshot

**Tibetan: yes, abundantly** (Dharmakīrti PV 3.3 + Tengyur commentary `BO_T11_D4225` + the whole indigenous *tshad ma* lineage). **Chinese: no** — only the unfused precursors (Abhidharma dravya-as-paramārtha by irreducibility, Saṃghabhadra's efficacy-leaning criterion of *sat*, Dignāga's 自相), because Dharmakīrti did not reach China. The transmission-geography independently corroborates §6: the identification is **Dharmakīrti's signature**, surviving only where his corpus was transmitted.
