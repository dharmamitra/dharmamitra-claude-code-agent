# The Śāstradīpikā Parallel to frag.txt Line 1: Locus and Context

**Report date:** 2026-06-11
**Passage:** Pārthasārathi Miśra, *Śāstradīpikā*, tarkapāda (ad Mīmāṃsāsūtra
1.1.5), ātmavāda/mokṣavāda excursus.

(printed ed. with Rāmakṛṣṇa's *Siddhāntacandrikā*, pp. 127–129, page headers

## 1. The overlap

**frag.txt, line 1 (with the lacuna-1 reconstruction):**
> tatra keyaṃ dṛśyatā? darśanayogyatā ⟨… atha **tasyām avasthāyāṃ sā nāstīti
> tena rūpeṇābhāvo**⟩ vety abhāva eva pūrvaṃ ghaṭādeḥ…

**Śāstradīpikā (ed. p. 128 = SA_GSP28_sastrdiu:3143):**
> …na draṣṭur dvitīyam anyad darśanasādhanaṃ cakṣurvyāpārarūpaṃ dṛśyaṃ vā
> tasyām avasthāyām asti yato darśanaṃ syāt | yadyapi dṛśyaṃ rūpādikaṃ
> **svarūpatas** tasyām avasthāyām asti tathāpi **dṛśyatā darśanayogyatā
> tasyām avasthāyāṃ nāstīti tena rūpeṇābhāvād dṛśyaṃ nāstīty ucyate** |
> tasmāt **sādhanāntaravaikalyāt** tasyām avasthāyāṃ na paśyati **na
> śaktivaikalyāt**, śaktis tu na kadācid api lupyata iti |

The shared technical complex: *dṛśyatā* glossed as *darśanayogyatā*; the
concession that the thing exists *svarūpataḥ* while its *dṛśyatā* does not
exist "in that state" (*tasyām avasthāyām*); and the conclusion that the thing
"is said not to exist" only *tena rūpeṇa* — in that respect. This is the
Dharmakīrtian *dṛśyānupalabdhi* semantics (absence-talk licensed only for the
perceptibility-qualified object) in near-verbatim wording.

## 2. Where this falls in the Śāstradīpikā — NOT where one would expect

The natural guess — that Pārthasārathi deploys this in his **abhāvapramāṇa
chapter** against the Buddhist reduction of absence-knowledge to anupalabdhi —
is wrong. That chapter sits much earlier in the tarkapāda (DharmaMitra
segments ~1844–1894: *abhāvasyānupalabdhigamyatvam*, where he attacks the
Buddhist account by name: "yad atra kaiścid ucyate — dṛśyasya sattā
darśanavyāptā, tena vyāpakībhūtadarśananivṛttyā vyāpyasya dṛśyasya nivṛttir
avasīyata iti **tan nirastam**").

The literal overlap instead occurs in the **ātmavāda → mokṣavāda excursus**
near the end of the tarkapāda: an inner-Mīmāṃsaka (and anti-Vedāntic) debate
about **what survives in deep sleep and in liberation**. The Buddhist formula
appears here in service of Vedic exegesis — nothing to do with pots, sherds,
or momentariness.

## 3. The discussion before (ed. pp. 126–127)

The section opens as a dispute over the nature of mokṣa within the Bhāṭṭa
school:

1. **The ānanda-mokṣa-vādins:** in liberation the self abides experiencing
   supreme bliss by mental perception (*mānasapratyakṣeṇa paramānandam
   anubhavann ātmāvatiṣṭhate*); the external senses lapse but the manas
   persists, on the strength of the bliss-śrutis; cognition persists per "*na
   hi vijñātur vijñāter viparilopo vidyate*" (BṛhUp 4.3.30) and the
   *vijñānaghana* text. They demote the rival "absence" view to *paramata*,
   citing a vārttika ("tasmāt karmakṣayād eva hetvabhāvena mucyate…").

2. **"But others say" (apare tv āhuḥ)** — the view Pārthasārathi develops as
   his own: liberation is *abhāvātmaka*, mere cessation. The liberated self
   **cannot** experience bliss, for want of instruments (*karaṇābhāvāt*); not
   even manas remains, per the śruti "*amano 'vāgī*" (amanaska-śruti).

3. Editorial section header: **mokṣāvasthāyāṃ jñānābhāvopapādanam** —
   "demonstration that there is no cognition in the state of liberation." The
   problem: the viparilopa-śrutis seem to say the seer's seeing never ceases.
   Pārthasārathi's solution: those texts speak of the **capacity**
   (*jñānaśakti*), not occurrent cognition — otherwise cognition would persist
   even in deep sleep, against experience. He sets out the whole BṛhUp 4.3.23–30
   series ("*yad vai tan na paśyati paśyan vai tan na paśyati, na hi draṣṭur
   dṛṣṭer viparilopo vidyate, avināśitvāt; na tu tad dvitīyam asti tato 'nyad
   vibhaktaṃ yat paśyet*", with the parallel formulas for smelling, hearing,
   touching, knowing) and argues that *dṛṣṭi, ghrāti, śruti, spṛṣṭi* denote
   specific sense-born cognitions, impossible in sleep or liberation where
   neither senses nor objects are available. An opponent's analogy — the sun's
   illuminating power is permanent even with nothing to illuminate — is turned
   to Pārthasārathi's own use: exactly so, what is permanent is the *capacity*.

## 4. The immediate context of the overlap (ed. p. 128)

The overlapping sentence belongs to Pārthasārathi's formal construal
(*vacanavyakti*) of BṛhUp 4.3.23:

> "This is the articulation of the text: in deep sleep or in liberation he
> does not see — *while seeing*, i.e. while fully **capable** of seeing
> (*paśyann eva draṣṭuṃ śaknuvann eva na paśyati*). For the seer-self's power
> of seeing is never lost, since it is imperishable. [But] there is no second
> thing — no further instrument of seeing such as the eye-function, **nor a
> visible object** — in that state, from which seeing could arise. **Although
> the visible (colour etc.) exists in that state by its own nature, its
> visibility (dṛśyatā), i.e. fitness-to-be-seen (darśanayogyatā), does not
> exist in that state; hence, because of its absence in that form, it is said
> 'the visible does not exist.'** Therefore in that state he does not see
> because of the deficiency of the *other* requisites
> (*sādhanāntaravaikalyāt*), not from deficiency of the capacity — the
> capacity is never lost."

The Buddhist distinction does precise work here: it lets Pārthasārathi affirm
the śruti's "he does not see" *and* "the seer's sight never perishes" at once,
by relocating the failure from the seer's śakti to the object's *dṛśyatā* and
the missing instruments.

## 5. The discussion after (ed. pp. 128–129)

- The same śakti-construal is extended to the remaining formulas; *jñāti* in
  "na jñātur jñāter viparilopaḥ" is referred to inferential etc. cognition.
- "*Vijñānaṃ brahma*" and "*vijñānaghana*" are likewise explained as
  capacity-statements; the Maitreyī dialogue (BṛhUp 2.4.12: *vijñānaghana* vs.
  *na pretya saṃjñāsti*) is harmonized: the śruti itself glosses the
  apparent contradiction, since "even one who is capable may lack cognition."
- "*Yatra tv asya sarvam ātmaivābhūt tat kena kaṃ paśyet*" (BṛhUp 4.5.15):
  with neither instruments nor objects, no cognition arises in mukti; even
  self-knowledge is excluded ("*vijñātāram are kena vijānīyāt*") since the
  manas, the instrument of self-apprehension, is absent.
- Conclusion of the section: **mokṣa is niḥsaṃbandha and nirānanda** —
  relationless and devoid of bliss; only the bare cognitive capacity abides.
- Next section (editorial header **ātmano jñānānandasvarūpatvasya
  khaṇḍanam**, p. 129): the anti-Vedāntic corollary — the self is not
  essentially jñāna (in deep sleep nothing shines though the self is present)
  nor essentially ānanda (no bliss-form is recognized in the self); the
  coordinations "vijñānaṃ brahma," "ānandaṃ brahma" are explained away as
  dharma-statements.

## 6. What kind of parallel is this, then?

1. **Not a quotation of frag.txt's debate.** Pārthasārathi is not engaging
   the kṣaṇabhaṅga/anupalabdhi controversy here; he is doing Vedic exegesis
   of the suṣupti/mokṣa texts. Earlier reports' phrasing ("Pārthasārathi
   quotes Dharmottara's NBṬ directly," "verbatim match for L1") overstated
   the relationship and mis-assigned the context.

2. **A borrowed school formula.** The complex *dṛśyatā = darśanayogyatā* /
   exists *svarūpataḥ* but not *tena rūpeṇa* / "asad ity ucyate" is standard
   Dharmakīrtian dṛśyānupalabdhi semantics (cf. NB 2 with Dharmottara's ṭīkā
   and the Dharmottarapradīpa). Pārthasārathi — a well-read 11th-c. opponent
   of the Buddhists — lifts the machinery for his own purpose. The verbal
   closeness to frag.txt thus attests the **formula**, not the fragment's
   text: both draw on the same Buddhist definitional stock.

3. **The irony:** in his abhāva chapter Pārthasārathi *refutes* the Buddhist
   dṛśyānupalabdhi account of absence-knowledge (segments ~1844–1894,
   "tan nirastam"); here, a few adhikaraṇas later, he *uses* its central
   distinction to save the śruti. The borrowed concept evidently struck him as
   correct in itself — only its anti-abhāvapramāṇa employment was objectionable.

4. **Consequence for the frag.txt reconstruction (Lacuna 1).** The
   reconstruction "⟨…atha tasyām avasthāyāṃ sā nāstīti tena rūpeṇābhāvo⟩ (vā)"
   remains well supported: the ŚD passage shows the exact wording in which the
   Buddhist definition circulated ("dṛśyatā darśanayogyatā tasyām avasthāyāṃ
   nāstīti tena rūpeṇābhāvāt … nāstīty ucyate"), and frag.txt's question
   "tatra keyaṃ dṛśyatā?" calls precisely for it. But its evidential status is
   "school-standard formula attested in a hostile witness," not "the
   fragment's own text quoted by Pārthasārathi." Confidence label ◇ is
   confirmed; an upgrade to ◆ on the strength of this parallel would be
   unjustified.
