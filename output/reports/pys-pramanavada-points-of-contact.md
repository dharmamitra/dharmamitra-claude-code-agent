# The Pātañjalayogaśāstra and the pramāṇavāda

**Points of contact with the Dignāga–Dharmakīrti epistemology, and what they imply for the dating of the *bhāṣya***

*Compiled 2026-06-17. Sources: DharmaMitra `/primary/` over the PYŚ digitizations (`SA_GSP34_patanjalayou` base, with `SA_GSP34_patyogbu`, `SA_GSP34_yogasubha`, `SA_GSP34_yogsubhu`, `SA_GSP34_patyog_u`), Vācaspati's Tattvavaiśāradī, and citing works (Akalaṅka, Bhaṭṭākalaṅka, Śrāvakabhūmi). Companion to `dharmakirti-svalaksana-paramarthasat.md` and to the apparatus `output/critical-editions/Patanjalayogasastra.md`.*

## 0. The question and the thesis

Does the *Pātañjalayogaśāstra* (Yogasūtra + Vyāsa-*bhāṣya*, treated as one work) engage views characteristically associated with Dignāga and Dharmakīrti? And what does that imply for the still-open question of when the *bhāṣya* was written relative to the Buddhist epistemologists?

**Theses.**
1. The *bhāṣya* opposes, point for point, three pillars of the Buddhist epistemic framework — svalakṣaṇa-centrality, *svasaṃvedana*, *kṣaṇikatva* — and the *puruṣa* is constructed to be the single antidote to all three. The PYŚ is, structurally, the **photographic negative of the Buddhist framework as it stood by Dignāga's time** (Abhidharma momentariness + the Dignāgan svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa partition with its *svasaṃvedana*). It is **not** the negative of Dharmakīrti's distinctive *system* — see the methodological note in §5 on inversion: each pillar is reversed only at its **pre-Dharmakīrtian** specificity, and the one element that is uniquely Dharmakīrti's (the *arthakriyā*-welding of svalakṣaṇa to *paramārtha*) is the single thing never reversed (§4-bis).
2. The evidence is **graded**, not uniform. The *svasaṃvedana* refutation (4.19–22) carries a **Dignāgan verbal fingerprint** (*svābhāsa / viṣayābhāsa*, the fire analogy, the regress argument) and is the strongest single datum. The inference-restriction at 1.25 is the same Dignāgan epistemology in the omniscience proof. The *kṣaṇikatva* opposition (3.13, 4.12–13, 4.33) is real but uses the **generic Sāṃkhya/satkāryavāda** kit and shows no specifically Dharmakīrtian instrument.
3. **Chronological upshot.** A cumulative case that the *bhāṣya* is **post-Dignāga and writing inside pramāṇavāda** is well-supported — incompatible with the early end of Maas's 325–425 bracket. Awareness of **Dharmakīrti specifically** is a reasonable inference (he is the doctrines' great systematizer and chief target-maker) but is **not demonstrated** by the *bhāṣya*'s own words; the one unmistakably Dharmakīrtian instrument in the dossier — the *sahopalambhaniyama* — appears only in **Vācaspati**, read back into 4.23.

> **Dating caveat, stated up front.** Maas (2006, 2013) dates the unified PYŚ to c. 325–425 and argues single authorship; this is influential but **not consensus**, and the relative position of the *bhāṣya* to Dignāga (c. 480–540) and Dharmakīrti (c. 600–660) is exactly what is contested (cf. Bronkhorst for a later, Buddhist-aware *bhāṣya*). The argument below treats that relative chronology as open and reasons *from the textual fingerprints*, not from an assumed date.

## 1. svalakṣaṇa / viśeṣa vs. the puruṣa-viśeṣa — YBh 4.24

The Sāṃkhya proof of the self at 4.24 (*tad … parārthaṃ saṃhatyakāritvāt*, the SK 17 *saṃghātaparārthatva* inference; cf. Yuktidīpikā `SA_GSP31_yuktdipu:4119`, Gauḍapāda `SA_GSP31_iskgaudu:395` *…paryaṅkavat*) culminates in a polemic that uses the *viśeṣa / sāmānya* lexicon:

> *yat tu kiṃcit paraṃ **sāmānyamātraṃ** svarūpeṇodāhared **vaināśikas** tat sarvaṃ saṃhatyakāritvāt parārtham eva syāt. yas tv asau paro **viśeṣaḥ** sa na saṃhatyakārī puruṣa iti.* (`SA_GSP34_patanjalayou`, *ad* 4.24)

The "other" the Buddhist can supply is only a *sāmānyamātra* (itself an aggregate, hence itself *parārtha*, generating a regress); only a non-composite *viśeṣa* — the puruṣa — terminates the series.

**What *sāmānyamātra* / *viśeṣa* refer to here — tested against the Vivaraṇa (not Vācaspati).** It is tempting to read this as the *same pair Dharmakīrti weights* at PV 3.3 (*viśeṣa = svalakṣaṇa = paramārthasat* vs *sāmānya = saṃvṛtisat*), "inverted on permanence." That parallel is a **loose lexical coincidence and should not be pressed.** The Pātañjalayogaśāstra**vivaraṇa** (ascribed to Śaṅkara — the older, *un*-contaminated sub-commentary; Vācaspati is disqualified here because he retrojects Dharmakīrtian doctrine, e.g. the *sahopalambhaniyama* at 4.23) glosses *para-sāmānyamātram* concretely as **the body, senses, and objects** (*śarīram indriyāṇi viṣayā vā*) and the Buddhist's **saṃghāta / santāna**, which it dismisses as ***avastu*** ("a non-entity, like a rabbit's horn," *śaśaviṣāṇakalpa*) and as merely *kṣaṇika*; the *viśeṣa* is the puruṣa as ***bhinnajātīya*** (a non-composite, **permanent** individual). The register is therefore the **Abhidharma-era pudgala/saṃghāta debate** (wholes and continua are *prajñapti* over momentary dharmas — the Sāṃkhya turning the Buddhist's own admission against him), **not** the Dignāgan/Dharmakīrtian *svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa* pair: there is no *svalakṣaṇa*, no *arthakriyā*, no *paramārtha/saṃvṛti*, no apoha, and the "real" pole is the **permanent** puruṣa — the inverse axis from Dharmakīrti's *momentary* svalakṣaṇa. The Vivaraṇa even routes the refutation explicitly back to 4.19 (*vyatiriktadṛśyatvāt*, rejecting the Buddhist's *dharmamātra* + *kṣaṇikatva*). So 4.24 reverses the **Abhidharma/Sautrāntika** Buddhist via a Dignāgan-level (*dṛśyatva*) tool; it carries **no Dharmakīrti-specific marking even at the ontological level.**

A second thread in the same *bhāṣya* — *na jñānaṃ jñānārtham … parārtham* ("cognition is not for its own sake; it is for-another") — is already the **anti-*svasaṃvedana*** point developed at §3.

## 2. The omniscience proof and the restricted scope of inference — YBh 1.25

YBh 1.25 (`SA_GSP34_patanjalayou:238–246`) proves a maximal knower and then qualifies it epistemologically:

> *…sarvajñabījam etad vivardhamānaṃ yatra niratiśayaṃ sa sarvajñaḥ. **asti kāṣṭhāprāptiḥ sarvajñabījasya sātiśayatvāt parimāṇavad iti. yatra kāṣṭhāprāptir jñānasya sa sarvajñaḥ.** sa ca puruṣaviśeṣa iti. **sāmānyamātropasaṃhāre ca kṛtopakṣayam anumānaṃ na viśeṣapratipattau samartham iti. tasya saṃjñādiviśeṣapratipattir āgamataḥ paryanveṣyā.***

Two layers, only the second decisive:

- The **gradation inference** (*asti kāṣṭhāprāptiḥ … sātiśayatvāt parimāṇavat*, "admits of degrees → has a culmination, like magnitude") is **pan-śāstric** (Praśastapāda, Nyāya). It is *not* a Dharmakīrti-borrowing; Dharmakīrti's own signature on omniscience is the move *away* from "knows-everything" toward the *dharmajña / heyopādeya-tattvajña* (PV 2: "we don't care whether he counts the insects"). So this layer carries no Buddhist-epistemological fingerprint.
- The **qualification** — *sāmānyamātropasaṃhāre … anumānaṃ na viśeṣapratipattau samartham*, "inference, having spent itself on the bare *sāmānya*, cannot deliver the *viśeṣa*; his particulars must come from *āgama*" — **is** the Dignāgan/Dharmakīrtian doctrine: **anumāna's object is the *sāmānyalakṣaṇa*, never the *svalakṣaṇa/viśeṣa*** (PS 1.2; PV 3.3). The *bhāṣya* concedes inference reaches only "a maximal knower exists in general" and hands the *viśeṣa* to scripture. This is an author working *inside* the pratyakṣa/anumāna ÷ svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa partition.

The *viśeṣa/sāmānya* line links 1.25 to 4.24 (and to 4.19–22 below): **one epistemology doing consistent work.**

**Later reception confirms the register.** Post-Dharmakīrti pramāṇa authors quote 1.25 *as* a pramāṇa-śāstra argument: Akalaṅka's *Siddhiviniścaya* (`SA_GR12_siddhivinisu:20353`) and Bhaṭṭākalaṅka's *Laghīyastraya* (`SA_GR12_laghiyastrau:4307`) both cite *asti kāṣṭhāprāptiḥ … parimāṇavat* by name ("yogabhā 1.25") and answer it.

### 2.1 Inspiration of the omniscience argument — Vaiśeṣika *form*, śramaṇa/Buddhist *content*

The proof pulls apart into two strata with different provenances.

- **Form = Vaiśeṣika gradation-to-a-maximum.** *sātiśayatvāt parimāṇavat* ("admits of degrees, like magnitude") is the Vaiśeṣika *parimāṇa* gradation (dimension runs *aṇu → mahat/vibhu*, so a graded series has a terminus). The "degrees → a maximum" inferential shape is Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika. (Reception of *this* argument in the corpus is **Jaina**, for the *kevalin*: Akalaṅka `SA_GR12_siddhivinisu:20353`, *jñānasyātiśayāt sidhyed vibhutvaṃ parimāṇavat*; Bhaṭṭākalaṅka `SA_GR12_laghiyastrau:4309`.)
- **Content = the śramaṇa "attained omniscience" model, in its Buddhist *bhāvanā-prakarṣa* form.** The argument is *developmental*: the *sarvajñabīja* **increases** (*vivardhamāna*) to its culmination. That "a cultivated cognitive quality perfected = omniscience" is the Buddhist proof of the Buddha — grounded in the PV commentaries: `BO_T11_D4224` (Devendrabuddhi-tier), *snying rje (karuṇā) … **goms pa** (bhāvanā) las **rab kyi mthar phyin** (prakarṣaparyanta) par 'gyur*; and `BO_S10_LAMD037`, compassion is the *sgrub byed* (prover) of *thams cad mkhyen pa'i ye shes* (omniscient gnosis), *de'i **rtsa ba** las byung ba'i phyir* ("arising from it as its root"). This content is canonical Mahāyāna, **older than Dignāga**; Dharmakīrti only versifies it (PV 2).

**Not Dharmakīrti's apparatus.** The YBh deploys none of Dharmakīrti's *distinctive* machinery — no **stable-substrate** justification (firm mind-*āśraya* vs. water-heating), no **gold-purification** / great-fire analogy, no **karuṇā-as-root**; it uses *parimāṇavat*. So even this, the YBh's one *positive* doctrine with Buddhist-shaped content, carries **no Dharmakīrti fingerprint**.

**The tell that the content is borrowed.** A *developmental* gradation ("a cognition that **grows** to omniscience") fits **attained** omniscience (Buddha / *kevalin*), not the YBh's own **eternally-free** Īśvara — which is exactly why the **Jainas** adopt it. The frame confirms it: YBh 1.25–26 paints Īśvara as a **Buddha-like compassionate omniscient teacher** — *tasyātmānugrahābhāve 'pi **bhūtānugrahaḥ** prayojanam … ādividvān **nirmāṇacittam adhiṣṭhāya kāruṇyāt** bhagavān paramarṣir āsuraye … tantraṃ provāca* (the *nirmāṇacitta*, the *kāruṇya*, the *bhagavān paramarṣi* rescuing transmigrators across eons = the Buddha-model grafted onto Sāṃkhya's otherwise non-theistic frame).

**Adoption vs. inversion (per the §5 principle).** Everywhere else the *bhāṣya*'s Buddhism is the **inverted target** (svasaṃvedana, kṣaṇikatva, the saṃghāta/santāna of 4.24). The omniscience section is the **one place the YBh *positively adopts* a Buddhist-shaped model** — the compassionate omniscient teacher — and dresses it in a Vaiśeṣika proof. An *adoption* also implies knowledge of the original; here, of the Mahāyāna Buddha-as-omniscient-compassionate-teacher, again **pre-Dignāga** content.

## 3. svasaṃvedana — refuted head-on at YBh 4.18–4.22 (the strongest fingerprint)

The cluster 4.18–4.22 is built to deny self-luminous cognition and substitute the puruṣa-witness. The *pūrvapakṣa* at 4.19 (`SA_GSP34_patyogbu:1450`) states the Buddhist thesis almost verbatim:

> *syād āśaṅkā — **cittam eva svābhāsaṃ viṣayābhāsaṃ ca** bhaviṣyatīty **agnivat** — na tat svābhāsaṃ dṛśyatvāt. yathetarāṇīndriyāṇi śabdādayaś ca dṛśyatvān na svābhāsāni tathā mano 'pi pratyetavyam. **na cāgnir atra dṛṣṭāntaḥ, na hy agnir ātmasvarūpam aprakāśaṃ prakāśayati**…*

- The opponent's *svābhāsa / viṣayābhāsa* (cognition's two appearances, self- and object-) is **Dignāga's *dvirūpa / svasaṃvitti* doctrine** (PS 1.11), with the canonical **fire/lamp** analogy (*agnivat*).
- Vyāsa refutes by the standard anti-*svaprakāśa* moves: (i) the citta is *dṛśya*, hence not self-illuminating, like the senses and sound; (ii) **fire is no valid example** — it does not light "its own previously-dark self," since illumination is a relation to an *other*. This fire-analogy refutation is exactly what Dharmakīrti's school defends against.

The other exits are closed at 4.20–4.21:

- **4.20** *ekasamaye cobhayānavadhāraṇam* — at a single moment (*ekasamaye*, i.e. one *kṣaṇa*) the citta cannot determine both itself and its object, for that makes *saiva kriyā tad eva ca kārakam* ("the same thing both act and agent," `SA_GSP34_patyogbu:1462`) — the self-reflexivity / agent-object objection (a blade cannot cut itself).
- **4.21** *cittāntaradṛśye buddhibuddher atiprasaṅgaḥ smṛtisaṃkaraś ca* — the *pūrvapakṣa* switches to the **Sautrāntika** alternative: *svarasaniruddhaṃ cittaṃ cittāntareṇa **samanantareṇa** gṛhyate* ("a self-ceased cognition is grasped by the immediately-following one"). Refuted by **infinite regress** (*sāpy anyayā sāpy anyayā ity atiprasaṅgaḥ*) and confusion of memory.

**The dialectical point.** The regress argument that Dignāga uses *to establish* svasaṃvedana (a cognition known by another cognition ⇒ regress ⇒ it must know itself) is here **redeployed against the succession theory** — after svasaṃvedana itself has already been killed at 4.19. Both Buddhist options eliminated, 4.18 + 4.22 install the survivor: cognitions are *sadā jñātāḥ* — always known — by the *apariṇāmin*, *apratisaṃkrama* puruṣa, which escapes the regress precisely because it is **not a cognition** (not *dṛśya*, not reflexive). The puruṣa is the anti-svasaṃvedana raised to a positive thesis. (Note the deliberately near-homonymous Yoga coinage at 4.22: *sva-buddhi-saṃvedanam* — the unchanging *citi* "cognizing its own buddhi," which is *not* a cognition cognizing *itself*.)

This is the dossier's clearest evidence of engagement with developed pramāṇavāda: *svābhāsa/viṣayābhāsa* is a Dignāgan signature, and the fire-analogy is where the *svasaṃvitti* doctrine is contested.

## 4. kṣaṇikatva — opposed, but with a generic-Buddhist target

Momentariness is denied structurally, via *pariṇāma-nityatā* + *satkāryavāda*:

- **4.12** (`SA_GSP34_patyogbu:1391`): *nāsty asataḥ saṃbhavaḥ, na cāsti sato vināśaḥ* — the *satkāryavāda* axiom, direct negation of momentary origination-and-perishing. Hence *atītānāgataṃ svarūpato 'sty adhvabhedād dharmāṇām* — past and future **exist**; *bhaviṣyadvyaktikam anāgatam, anubhūtavyaktikam atītaṃ … trayaṃ caitad vastu jñānasya jñeyam* — all three times are real, only the *vyakti* (manifestation) changing, not the *dravya*. (Sarvāstivāda-shaped, harnessed to a persisting substrate.)
- **3.13** (`SA_GSP34_patyogbu:914`): the *dharma/dharmin* defense. The opponent (*apara āha*) voices the Buddhist no-substrate worry — *dharmānabhyadhiko dharmī … kauṭasthyenaiva parivarteta yady anvayī syāt* — answered by the gold-and-ornament distinction: *bhāvānyathātva* (change of state) without *dravyānyathātva* (change of substance).
- **4.33** (apparatus, this corpus): the verdict. Vyāsa **accepts the *kṣaṇa*** as the unit of succession but embeds it in *dvayī … nityatā — kūṭasthanityatā* (the puruṣa) and ***pariṇāminityatā*** (the guṇas): the substrate is permanent *through* its momentary transformations — *kṣaṇa* without *kṣaṇa-bhaṅga*.

**Caveat.** This apparatus — *satkāryavāda*, *dravya/bhāva*, persisting *guṇas* — is the **generic Sāṃkhya-vs-Abhidharma** anti-momentariness kit; it predates Dignāga and targets Buddhist momentariness as such. There is **no engagement here with the specifically Dharmakīrtian proofs** — the *sattvānumāna* (*yat sat tat kṣaṇikam*, momentariness from *arthakriyā*) or the *vināśitva* argument. So the *kṣaṇikatva* opposition, though real and important, is **weaker evidence for Dharmakīrti-awareness** than §3.

## 4-bis. Direct test for the Dharmakīrtian signature (arthakriyā / sattvānumāna): negative

A full scan of the *bhāṣya* (base `patanjali_yogasutra_w_bhasya_b.txt`) for the criterion-word and for its likely paraphrases returns nothing Dharmakīrtian:

- ***arthakriyā* / *kriyākārit* / *kriyākṣama*: zero occurrences.** The criterion-word is simply absent. Causal-efficacy talk enters only through two **pre-Dharmakīrti** channels:
  1. The **Sarvāstivāda *vyāpāra* (= *kāritra*) theory of the three times**, raised as the opponents' *kauṭasthya* objection at **3.13** (`…:914` region): *avasthāpariṇāme **kauṭasthyaprasaṅgadoṣaḥ** kaiścid uktaḥ … adhvano **vyāpāreṇa** vyavahitatvāt; yadā dharmaḥ **svavyāpāraṃ na karoti** tadānāgato, yadā karoti tadā vartamāno …* The present is defined as the moment of *operation*, and the persisting substrate is then charged with collapsing into the inefficacious immutable. This is the structural pressure behind the *sattvānumāna*, but framed as **Vasumitra's *avasthā* theory (AKBh 5.26)** — note the *rekhā*-in-decimal-places analogy beside it — not as *arthakriyā*. Answered by *vimarda-vaicitrya*: *nāsau doṣaḥ … guṇinityatve 'pi guṇānāṃ vimardavaicitryāt.*
  2. **Sāṃkhya *satkāryavāda*** causation, which *rejects* productive efficacy as the mark of the real — **4.12**: *sataś ca phalasya **nimittaṃ vartamānīkaraṇe samarthaṃ nāpūrvopajanane** … nāpūrvam utpādayati* ("the cause only *makes-present* a pre-existing fruit; it does not *produce a new one*"). The antithesis of *arthakriyā*-as-production, but the ancient *satkārya* position.

- **The *sattvānumāna* (*yat sat tat kṣaṇikam*) is absent as an inference.** What appears is only the *answer* to its *yaugapadya* horn, again in satkāryavāda idiom — **3.14**: *yogyatāvacchinnā dharmiṇaḥ **śaktir** eva dharmaḥ … **deśakālākāranimittāpabandhān na khalu samānakālam** ātmanām abhivyaktiḥ* (a persisting substrate's capacities do not all fire at once because manifestation is regulated by place/time/form/cause — the satkāryavāda counterpart to the *sahakārin* discussion). The *yaugapadya* dilemma-form does occur (**2.13**: *tad anekaṃ janma **yugapan na saṃbhavatīti krameṇaiva vācyam***) but applied to **karma and rebirth**, not to existence-entails-momentariness. 3.14 also runs the standard anti-*niranvaya* battery (*kṛtanāśa*, **memory**, **pratyabhijñā**: *vastupratyabhijñānāc ca sthito 'nvayī dharmī*) against the Sautrāntika/Yogācāra *santāna* — again pre-Dignāga.

- **Incidental gain (reinforces §3, adds no new angle).** The `kṣaṇika` hits show Vyāsa naming the holder of self-cognition as the **kṣaṇikavādin** at **4.20** (`…:845`): *na caikasmin **kṣaṇe** svapararūpāvadhāraṇaṃ yuktaṃ, **kṣaṇikavādino** yad bhavanaṃ saiva kriyā tad eva ca kārakam ity abhyupagamaḥ* — *svasaṃvedana* correctly identified as the momentariness-theorist's doctrine and refuted by the *kriyā/kāraka*-identity objection (its *epistemological* face, not the *arthakriyā* ontology). And **1.32** refutes the *kṣaṇika*-stream theory of mind (*nāsti pravāhacittaṃ kṣaṇikatvāt*).

**Result.** The hypothesis that the *bhāṣya* paraphrases *arthakriyā* or the *sattvānumāna* is **falsified**: where it touches efficacy and momentariness it speaks **AKBh-Sarvāstivāda** (*vyāpāra/kāritra*, the *kauṭasthya* objection) and **generic Sāṃkhya satkāryavāda** (manifestation-not-production; conditioned *abhivyakti*) — strata all **pre-Dignāga**. The momentariness/efficacy front therefore gives **no** push past Dignāga and **no** Dharmakīrti-specific fingerprint.

## 5. Synthesis: the puruṣa as triple antidote

| Dharmakīrti pillar | PYŚ negation | Locus | Fingerprint strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *svalakṣaṇa = paramārthasat* (real = momentary efficient particular) | the ultimate *viśeṣa* is the **non-composite, permanent** puruṣa | 4.24 | shared *viśeṣa/sāmānya* lexicon, inverted on permanence — **moderate** |
| inference restricted to *sāmānya* (PS 1.2) | conceded; *viśeṣa* of Īśvara from *āgama* | 1.25 | Dignāgan epistemology, explicit — **strong** |
| *svasaṃvedana* (self-luminous, *svābhāsa+viṣayābhāsa*, fire-like) | citta is *dṛśya*, **not** self-luminous; known by the puruṣa-witness | 4.18–22 | **Dignāgan verbal fingerprint** — **strongest** |
| *kṣaṇikatva* (all that is, is momentary; spontaneous perishing) | *pariṇāmanityatā*: persisting substrate; past/future real | 3.13, 4.12–13, 4.33 | generic Sāṃkhya/*satkāryavāda* — **weakest for D.-specificity** |

The single *viśeṣa/sāmānya* distinction runs through 1.25, 4.24, and 4.19–22; the *puruṣa* simultaneously answers all three Buddhist pillars — it is the permanent *viśeṣa* (vs *svalakṣaṇa*), the non-objectifiable knower (vs *svasaṃvedana*), and the *kūṭastha-nitya* substrate-correlate (vs *kṣaṇikatva*).

**Methodological note — inversion and what it licenses.** To reverse a doctrine one must know it, so each *negation* above is also a *proof of acquaintance*. But an inversion reveals the original **only at the specificity it reproduces**: reversing "no-self" into "self" proves knowledge of *nairātmya* in general, not of any author's version, unless the inversion carries author-specific markings. Calibrated this way, the bhāṣya's inversions license exactly:

| Inverted doctrine | Specificity of the *target reversed* | Knowledge licensed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *svasaṃvedana* (4.19) | Dignāga-specific (*svābhāsa/viṣayābhāsa*, PS 1.11) | **Dignāga** |
| *kṣaṇikatva* (3.13, 4.12) | AKBh-specific (*vyāpāra/kāritra* 3-times theory) | **Vasubandhu / Sarvāstivāda** |
| svalakṣaṇa-centrality (4.24) | *parārthatva* contrast: non-composite individual vs. *saṃghāta/santāna* as *avastu* (per the **Vivaraṇa**) — **not** the *svalakṣaṇa/sāmānyalakṣaṇa* pair, **not** *arthakriyā* | **Abhidharma/Sautrāntika** (pudgala-debate) |
| *nairātmya* (4.25) | canonical self-doubt formula (Śrāvakabhūmi/Sabbāsava) | **canonical Buddhism** |
| *arthakriyā* = criterion of *paramārtha* | **never reversed** (§4-bis) | **none** |

The inversions reconstruct Dignāga and Abhidharma because the bhāṣya reverses *those* at their own specificity. They do not reconstruct Dharmakīrti, because the doctrine uniquely his — the *arthakriyā*-welding of svalakṣaṇa to *paramārtha* — is the single one the bhāṣya never inverts. By the same principle, **the absence of that inversion is evidence of absence of knowledge** of that specific doctrine. (This is also why §5's "triple antidote" must be read against the *by-Dignāga* framework, not Dharmakīrti's system: see §0.1.)

## 6. Verdict on the dating question

- **Well-supported:** the *bhāṣya* is **post-Dignāga** and composes *inside* the pramāṇavāda — the 1.25 inference-restriction and the 4.19 *svābhāsa/viṣayābhāsa* + fire refutation are not generic anti-Buddhist polemic but engagement with the specific Dignāgan epistemology. This is incompatible with the early end of Maas's bracket.
- **Reasonable but not demonstrated:** awareness of **Dharmakīrti specifically**. The opposed doctrines are stated in their **Dignāgan** form; the one unmistakably Dharmakīrtian instrument (the *sahopalambhaniyama*) enters only with **Vācaspati** at 4.23. Dharmakīrti is the natural systematizer and chief target-maker behind these doctrines, so the inference is sound — but the *bhāṣya*'s own words license "post-Dignāga / pramāṇavāda-aware," not more.

**Where a Dharmakīrti-only signature would be found:** engagement with the *arthakriyā* criterion of the real, the *sattvānumāna* for momentariness, the *sahopalambhaniyama* in the *bhāṣya* (not just Vā), or the *bhāvanā-prakarṣa* argument. **These were searched for directly (§4-bis) and none is present.** The efficacy/momentariness front is satisfied entirely by **Vasubandhu's AKBh Sarvāstivāda + old Sāṃkhya *satkārya***, both pre-Dignāga; the *only* material that *requires* a post-Dignāga author is the *svasaṃvedana* epistemology (4.19–20) and the inference-restriction (1.25), both **Dignāgan**.

**Refined conclusion.** The cumulative evidence supports a *bhāṣya* that is **post-Dignāga, engaging Dignāga's epistemology and Vasubandhu's Abhidharma, with no demonstrable trace of Dharmakīrti's distinctive ontological arguments.** Since Dharmakīrti's signature is *absent precisely where it would be hardest to avoid* (the momentariness/efficacy front), this is a defeasible argument that the *bhāṣya* did **not** know Dharmakīrti — and that the right Buddhist interlocutor to look for is **Dignāga or a post-Dignāga Yogācāra epistemologist short of Dharmakīrti** (Sthiramati, Dharmapāla; the latter preserved mainly in Chinese). See §8.

## 8. Which post-Dignāga Buddhist interlocutor? — a Chinese/Tibetan probe

Given §4-bis (no Dharmakīrti signature), the right question is: *whose* form of the
*svasaṃvedana* doctrine does the *bhāṣya* oppose? A search of the Chinese and Tibetan
corpora narrows it sharply.

**The doctrine opposed = Dignāga's three-aspect (*trisvarūpa*) self-cognition.** The
*bhāṣya*'s 4.19 *pūrvapakṣa* (*cittam eva svābhāsaṃ viṣayābhāsaṃ ca … agnivat*) is the
*dvirūpa* + self-illumination that the Chinese tradition assigns to Dignāga. The
**Cheng weishi lun (成唯識論 = Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi, Dharmapāla via Xuanzang),
`ZH_T31_1585_002:0010b13`**, quotes the *Pramāṇasamuccaya* (集量論) verse for it:
*似境相所量，能取相自證，即能量及果，此三體無別* — "the object-aspect (相分) is the
*prameya*; the grasping-aspect-with-self-cognition (能取相自證) is the *pramāṇa* and its
result; these three are one in substance" (= PS 1.10, *trisvarūpa*). This is precisely the
self-cognising cognition the *bhāṣya* denies.

**Dharmapāla is the natural post-Dignāga developer — and the regress he answers is YBh
4.21 verbatim.** Cheng weishi lun continues: *又心心所若細分別應有四分 … 此若無者，
**誰證第三**？* — "the mind should have **four** aspects … without [the fourth], **who
verifies the third?**" The fourth aspect (證自證分, *svasaṃvitti-saṃvitti*) exists solely
to stop the regress, which is closed because the third and fourth mutually verify
(成唯識論自攷 `ZH_X51_0823_002:0176b10`: *第三第四既皆現量，故得互證，**無無窮過***
— "no infinite-regress fault"). Dharmapāla's *誰證第三* is the exact Buddhist counterpart
of the *bhāṣya*'s **4.21** *buddhibuddhiḥ kena gṛhyate, sāpy anyayā … ity atiprasaṅgaḥ*.
Further Cheng-weishi-lun-tradition attestations: `ZH_X63_1231_001:0091b22`,
`ZH_X51_0824_002:0326a22`, `ZH_T36_1736_033:0252a14`.

**But the evidence does *not* elevate Dharmapāla over Dignāga.** The *bhāṣya* treats the
regress as *fatal* and concludes one needs a **non-cognition** witness (the puruṣa); it
shows **no awareness of the four-aspect closure** that is Dharmapāla's whole point. Its
regress-objection therefore sits exactly at the stage Dharmapāla was built to answer —
i.e. it engages the **Dignāgan** formulation (where a single *svasaṃvitti* is meant to halt
the regress) and is innocent of, or ignores, the four-aspect refinement. So Dharmapāla is
the closest *thematic* match in the post-Dignāga Yogācāra (and the one richly preserved in
**Chinese**), but the *bhāṣya*'s actual target is the Dignāgan doctrine he was correcting.

**The refutation-tools are stock Madhyamaka, not a post-Dignāga fingerprint.** The 4.19
fire-rebuttal (*na cāgnir atra dṛṣṭāntaḥ, na hy agnir ātmasvarūpam aprakāśaṃ prakāśayati*)
is the **lamp topos** that goes back to **Nāgārjuna, MMK 7.9–12** (*ji ltar mar me rang gi
dngos / … mar me gsal bar bya min te*, attested in the Tibetan corpus at
`BO_TSD_BO_DC_LEK-PHI-048:315`) and recurs in **Bhāviveka's Prajñāpradīpa** (般若燈論,
`ZH_T30_1566_005:0076b16`), the **Buddhatā-śāstra** (佛性論, tr. Paramārtha,
`ZH_T31_1610_001:0791b05–12`: *若燈能自照…瓶等亦應自照*), and **Śāntideva's BCA 9** via
Prajñākaramati's Pañjikā (`BO_T04_D3877:181a-19`, `BO_T04_D3880:262a-22`). Because the
lamp-rebuttal is a shared topos, it adds **no** author-specific information beyond "the
target holds self-illumination."

**Reverse polemic (Buddhists vs. the Sāṃkhya/Yoga witness-puruṣa) exists but clusters off
the relevant date.** The Buddhist attack on the *buddhi-for-puruṣa* / permanent-knower is
real in the corpus — Āryadeva's **Catuḥśataka** refuting the permanent *jñātṛ*
(`BO_S02_UMA-021:959`, but the root text is 3rd c., too early) and the **Tattvasaṃgraha-
pañjikā** of Kamalaśīla (`BO_T11_D4267:209a-11`, *blo … rtag pa … blo ni ngo shes par
byed*, but c. 760, too late). The one datable mid-6th-c. Buddhist Sāṃkhya-refuter,
**Bhāviveka's Madhyamakahṛdaya/Tarkajvālā ch. 6** (*Sāṃkhyatattvāvatāra*), did **not**
surface cleanly in this corpus (the Tibetan hits were later dGe-lugs/Sa-skya scholastic
literature); cited here from standard scholarship and flagged **[not cleanly in corpus]**.

**§8 verdict.** No post-Dignāga figure is a *demonstrably better* fit than **Dignāga**
himself for the doctrine the *bhāṣya* opposes. **Dharmapāla** (護法; Chinese transmission)
is the most relevant post-Dignāga developer — his *誰證第三* regress-and-four-aspects is the
precise Buddhist mirror of YBh 4.21 — but the *bhāṣya*'s silence on the four-aspect
solution shows it engaging the **Dignāgan** three-aspect doctrine, with **stock Madhyamaka
lamp-refutation** as its tool. The cumulative dating estimate is therefore unchanged:
**post-Dignāga, pre- or independent-of the four-aspect Yogācāra and of Dharmakīrti.**
