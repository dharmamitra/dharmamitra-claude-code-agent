# Did any Sāṃkhya philosopher argue for the *singularity* of puruṣa?

**A doxographic investigation against the Sāṃkhyakārikā's *puruṣabahutva***

*Compiled 2026-06-16. Sources: local Sanskrit E-texts corpus (`~/Documents/E-texts/1_sanskr`), local Tibetan Derge Tengyur (`~/Documents/Tibetan`), and the DharmaMitra `/primary/` canonical corpus.*

---

## 1. The question

Sāṃkhyakārikā (SK) 18 establishes the **plurality of selves** (*puruṣabahutva*) as a defining tenet of classical Sāṃkhya:

> *janana-maraṇa-karaṇānāṃ pratiniyamād ayugapat pravṛtteś ca / puruṣa-bahutvaṃ siddhaṃ traiguṇya-viparyayāc caiva* (SK 18)
> "The plurality of puruṣas is established (a) from the distributed assignment of birth, death, and the faculties, (b) from non-simultaneous activity, and (c) from the differing arrangement of the three guṇas."

The commentaries report that this was a contested question — that *some* held puruṣa to be **one**. The investigation asks: **is there evidence for a named Sāṃkhya philosopher who defended the singularity of puruṣa against SK 18?** Or is the "one-puruṣa Sāṃkhya" a doxographic placeholder rather than a historical school?

**Thesis established by the evidence:** No named Sāṃkhya philosopher ever held the singularity of puruṣa. The one-self position is reported only anonymously or externalised to non-Sāṃkhya schools (Vedānta / Aupaniṣadas / Advaita). Where a single puruṣa genuinely lives is the **theistic / universal-self register**, and even there the systems that adopt a supreme puruṣa (Pātañjala Yoga, the Mokṣadharma, Caraka, Vijñānabhikṣu) house it *within* a plurality rather than reducing the many to one.

---

## 2. The Sāṃkhya commentaries: the one-self view reported, but never owned

### 2.1 The locus classicus and its *ācāryavipratipatti*

Every Sāṃkhya commentary on SK 18 affirms plurality, but several preface it by reporting a disagreement on whether the established puruṣa is one or many.

- **Māṭhara-vṛtti** (`isvarakrsna_samkhyakarika_w_mathara-vrtti.txt:444`) grounds the doubt explicitly in *teacher-disagreement*:
  > *sa kiṃ sarvaśarīreṣv ekaḥ puruṣa āhosvit pratiśarīraṃ bhinnaḥ puruṣa iti | kutaḥ saṃśaya iti cec codaka **ācāryavipratipatteḥ saṃśayaḥ** | iha kecid ācāryā … eko 'yaṃ puruṣaḥ sarvaśarīreṣūpalabhyate **maṇisūtravat** … āhosvij **jalacandravat** …*
  "Is there one puruṣa in all bodies, or a distinct one per body? — The doubt arises **from the disagreement of teachers**. Some hold one puruṣa is found in all bodies, **like a single thread through many gems**, or **like the moon in many waters** …"

- **Jayamaṅgalā** (ascribed to Śaṅkara-ārya; `samkara-arya_jayamangala.txt:197`) gives a three-way doxography ad SK 17:
  > *tatra siddhe puṃsi vivādāḥ / **eka evāyaṃ puruṣaḥ sarvaśarīreṣu sthita ity eke** / … pratiśarīram anekaḥ puruṣa ity apare / eka eva purāṇaḥ puruṣaḥ, tasmād agner iva visphuliṅgāḥ pratiśarīraṃ puruṣā āvirbhūtā iti **vedāntavādinaḥ**//17//*
  (i) *eke* — one puruṣa in all bodies; (ii) *apare* — many, one per body (the Kārikā's view); (iii) the **Vedāntins** — one primeval puruṣa from which individuals spark off like sparks from fire (*agni-visphuliṅga*).

- **Sāṃkhyasūtra** 1.149–150 (`kapila/kapila_samkhyasutra_b.txt:528, 531`) stages the one-self position as a formal *pūrvapakṣa*:
  > *janmādivyavasthātaḥ puruṣabahutvam* (1.149); *upādhibhede 'py ekasya nānāyogaḥ **ākāśasyeva ghaṭādibhiḥ*** (1.150)
  Sūtra 1.150 voices the eka-ātman objection — plurality is merely apparent through limiting adjuncts (*upādhi*), as space (*ākāśa*) seems divided by pots — precisely so the following sūtras can refute it. This is the standard *Vedānta* argument-form.

- The **Paramārthatīkā** (`unknown_samkhyakarika-paramarthatika.txt:958, 962`) likewise frames "one puruṣa pervades all bodies" before rejecting it.

**The four similes** for the one-puruṣa side recur across the witnesses: *maṇi-sūtra* (thread through gems), *jala-candra* (moon in many waters), *ākāśa-ghaṭa* (space in many pots), *agni-visphuliṅga* (sparks from fire — explicitly Vedāntic).

### 2.2 The Yuktidīpikā: the sharpest report — and it points away from Sāṃkhya

The most analytical commentary names the parties to the disagreement *by school*, and assigns the one-self view to the **Aupaniṣadas (Vedāntins)**, not to any Sāṃkhya teacher (`yuktidipika/unknown_yuktidipika_e.txt:3762–3766`):

> *ācāryavipratipatteḥ | **aupaniṣadāḥ khalu eka ātmeti pratipannāḥ** | kāṇādākṣapādārhata-prabhṛtayaḥ punar [an]eka iti |*
> "[The doubt arises] from teacher-disagreement: the **Aupaniṣadas hold the self is one**; the Kāṇādas (Vaiśeṣika), Akṣapādas (Nyāya), Ārhatas (Jaina) and the rest hold it is **many**."

*Text-critical note:* all local copies (`_e:3764`, `_c:3755`, `_b:1049`) read *punar **eka** iti*, but this is an archetype error — Vaiśeṣika/Nyāya/Jaina are the paradigmatic *many-selves* schools, and the b-copy confirms the framing *idānīm **aneko** 'tha eka iti vicāryam*. Read *punar **aneka** iti* (so Wezler–Motegi).

The four classic **named** Sāṃkhya teachers — **Vārṣagaṇya, Vindhyavāsin, Pañcādhikaraṇa, Patañjali** — do cluster in the YD, but in the *tattva-evolution* debate ad kārikā 22 (`…e.txt:4123–4146`), disagreeing over the sequence and nature of the evolutes (whether *mahat* arises from *pradhāna*, the graduation of the *tanmātras*, the elemental nature of the *indriyas*, the momentariness of *buddhi*). **None of these disagreements touches puruṣa-number.**

### 2.3 Verdict on the Sāṃkhya tradition

The one-puruṣa view is reported anonymously (*kecid ācāryāḥ / eke*) or externalised to Vedānta. The Yuktidīpikā's named teachers are unanimous on plurality. **No Sāṃkhya commentator credits any individual with the singularity of puruṣa.**

## 3. Vijñānabhikṣu: relocating the "one self" to a supreme puruṣa

In the *Sāṃkhyasāra*, Vijñānabhikṣu harmonises the "one self" *śrutis* without abandoning plurality, by reading them as referring to a **primal puruṣa** (`vijnanabhiksu_samkhyasara.txt:251`):

> *tata eva cādipuruṣāt vyaṣṭipuruṣāṇāṃ vibhāgād ante ca tatraiva layāt **sa eva caika ātmeti** śrutismṛtyor vyavahriyate | ato na vyavahāra-paratayā **nārāyaṇa eva sarvabhūtānām ātmeti** śrutismṛtivirodhaḥ |*
> "Because the individual puruṣas (*vyaṣṭipuruṣa*) divide off from the primal Puruṣa (*ādipuruṣa*) and dissolve back into it, scripture speaks figuratively of 'one and the same self' / 'Nārāyaṇa alone is the self of all beings'; so there is no contradiction with the plurality [taught in śruti-smṛti]."

The only *named* opponents he engages on this point are the **Advaitins** — *ādhunikā vedāntibruvāḥ*, "modern would-be-Vedāntins" (`:175`, refuted `:178`) — whom he rejects. He credits **no Sāṃkhya** with monism; the one self is the supreme *ādipuruṣa* (Nārāyaṇa), the many *vyaṣṭi*-puruṣas remaining real.

## 4. The Mahābhārata Mokṣadharma: the question posed, Sāṃkhya assigned plurality

The Śāntiparvan's Nārāyaṇīya (adhyāya 338) poses the very question verbatim (`vyasa_mahabharata_parvan-12-santiparvan.txt:33732`):

> *bahavaḥ puruṣā brahmann utāho **eka eva tu*** (12,338.001) — "Are puruṣas many, O brahman, or only one?"

And answers it along the school line (12,338.002):

> *bahavaḥ puruṣā loke **sāṃkhya-yoga-vicāriṇām** / **naitad icchanti puruṣam ekaṃ** kurukulodvaha* — "For those who reason by Sāṃkhya and Yoga, puruṣas are many; **they do not accept a single puruṣa**."

The one-puruṣa doctrine is then credited to **Vyāsa**, grounded on **Kapila's** adhyātma-corpus (12,338.005–007: *ṛṣibhiḥ **kapilādibhiḥ** … yad **vyāsaḥ puruṣaikatvam** uktavān*), and resolved theologically: the many puruṣas have a single *ādhāra* / *yoni* — the universal, guṇa-less supreme Puruṣa (*puruṣa virāṭ / puruṣottama*) into which the liberated re-enter (Brahmā–Rudra dialogue, 12,338.022–025). An older upaniṣadic one-self verse appears at 12,231.020: *vasaty **eko mahān ātmā** yena sarvam idaṃ tatam*.

So even in this archaic theistic stratum, **Sāṃkhya–Yoga = plurality**; the single Puruṣa is a *theological* supreme self, not a Sāṃkhya reading of SK 18.

## 5. Caraka: the closest thing to a one-self Sāṃkhya — and it is anonymous

Caraka's *Śārīrasthāna* 1 (*Katidhāpuruṣīya*; `agnivesa_carakasamhita_sthana-1-6.txt`) preserves a distinctive, pre-classical Sāṃkhya that runs a **two-level** account of puruṣa, the higher level being singular:

> *khādayaś cetanāṣaṣṭhā dhātavaḥ puruṣaḥ smṛtaḥ / **cetanā-dhātur apy ekaḥ** smṛtaḥ puruṣasaṃjñakaḥ / punaś ca dhātu-bhedena caturviṃśatikaḥ smṛtaḥ* (CS Śā 1.16, ll.5414–5416)
> "The conscious element, too, is held to be **one**, named puruṣa; and again, by division of constituents, it is reckoned twenty-four-fold."

The singular principle is described in universal *paramātman* terms:
- *prabhavo no hy anāditvād vidyate **paramātmanaḥ*** (1.53, l.5488);
- *anādiḥ puruṣo nityo* (1.59, l.5500);
- *avyaktam ātmā kṣetrajñaḥ **śāśvato vibhur avyayaḥ*** (1.61, l.5504);
- *nirvikāraḥ paras tv ātmā **sarvabhūtānāṃ nirviśeṣaḥ*** (l.198 / l.5943) — "the supreme Self, undifferentiated across all beings."

Crucially, Caraka flags the provenance — the *ṣaḍdhātuja rāśi* "was proclaimed by **the early Sāṃkhyas**" (*sāṃkhyair **ādyaiḥ** prakīrtitaḥ*, l.2725).

**Caveat:** Caraka is not a clean monist. The same chapter requires individuated rebirth (*dehāntara-gatiḥ smṛtiḥ*, 1.52; the *rāśi-puruṣa* is *mohecchā-dveṣa-karmaja*, 1.53), so it operates a two-tier scheme — a universal *paramātman / cetanā-dhātu* (singular) over individuated, karma-bound aggregate-puruṣas. It reaches the "one" via a Vedānta-flavoured universal self, and names **no individual teacher** (only "the early Sāṃkhyas" collectively). This is the pre-classical / heterodox medical Sāṃkhya noted in the secondary literature (Dasgupta, Larson, Comba).

## 6. Pātañjala Yoga: pluralist, with the systematized supreme puruṣa

The Pātañjalayogaśāstra (`patanjali_yogasutra_w_bhasya_b.txt`) never stages a one-vs-many debate; it operates with plurality throughout and defends the puruṣa against the Buddhists.

**Plurality, explicit and load-bearing:**
- *YS 2.22* (l.373): *kṛtārtham ekaṃ puruṣaṃ prati dṛśyaṃ naṣṭam api … anaṣṭaṃ **tad-anya-puruṣa-sādhāraṇatvāt*** — prakṛti persists for the *other* puruṣas (the Yoga counterpart of the SK 18 liberation argument).
- *YS 4.15–16* (ll.10, 828): *bahu-cittālambanī-bhūtam ekaṃ vastu sādhāraṇam* … *svatantro 'rthaḥ **sarva-puruṣa-sādhāraṇaḥ** svatantrāṇi ca cittāni **pratipuruṣaṃ** pravartante* — object common to all puruṣas, minds one-per-puruṣa.
- *YS 4.21* (l.850): *sāṃkhya-yogādayas tu pravādāḥ svaśabdena **puruṣam eva svāminaṃ** cittasya bhoktāram upayanti* — Sāṃkhya-Yoga affirm the puruṣa against the Buddhist no-self (*vaināśikāḥ*). (Cf. 4.5, l.780: *bahūnāṃ cittānām*.)

**The supreme puruṣa — but within plurality:**
- *YS 1.24* (ll.125, 132): *kleśa-karma-vipākāśayair aparāmṛṣṭaḥ **puruṣaviśeṣa** īśvaraḥ* — Īśvara is a *special* puruṣa, eternally free (*sadaiva muktaḥ*); the "two-equals" argument shows there can be only one such unsurpassed lord.

Yoga is thus the orthodox system that most fully develops a supreme self — yet keeps it a *puruṣa-viśeṣa*, a distinguished **member** of the plurality, not its dissolution into one. This is the structural key to the whole investigation (see §8).

**The "Patañjali" homonymy:** the Sāṃkhya teacher Patañjali named in the YD (§2.2) is, by scholarly consensus (Frauwallner and after), of unproven identity with the Yogasūtra's Patañjali; either way a pluralist.

## 7. The Buddhist doxographies: a clean negative, end-to-end

### 7.1 Bhāviveka, Madhyamakahṛdaya / Tarkajvālā (Sāṃkhya chapter), read in full

The major Buddhist critical doxography of Sāṃkhya is Bhāviveka's *Tarkajvālā*, chapter 6 (*grangs can pa'i de kho na nyid gtan la dbab pa*). The Sāṃkhya chapter was read **end-to-end** from the local Derge Tengyur (Wylie/ACIP `TD3856E.TXT`), **le'u 6, lines 3359–3629, folios dza 227b–242a** (matching the published extent dza 227a7–242a7; the stretch 242a–254b is already the Vaiśeṣika and Vedānta chapters, le'u 7–8).

The chapter reports and refutes **only the plurality** doctrine:
- *rang bzhin ni gcig cig yin la, **skyes bu ni mang po** yin* (l.3464) — "prakṛti is one, but the puruṣas are many."
- *grangs can pa'i grub pa'i mtha' 'di yin te … **so so'i lus re re la yang skyes bu gnas** shing … **thams cad la khyab pa** yin* (ll.3466–67) — one puruṣa per body, all pervading; refuted by the "if one is freed, all are freed / caste and kinship collapse" argument (ll.3470–75).
- The "*kha cig* / some Sāṃkhyas" variants (ll.3416–18) concern *bondage and liberation mechanics*, not puruṣa-number.
- The previously-unsearched second half (folios 234b–242a) is entirely the five-membered *pradhāna* inference (*gtzo bo yod de…*, l.3488) and its refutation, the sandalwood/pieces analogy, and the puruṣa-as-knower / non-pervasion refutation (*kun la khyab pa ma yin*, l.3611) — **no single-puruṣa report anywhere**.
- The only teacher named is **Kapila** (*thub pa ser skya*, l.3359), as the pluralist founder.

(Corroborated in the DharmaMitra corpus: `BO_T04_D3855:27a-31`, `BO_T04_D3856:247a-34` — the *saṃkhyā-yoga* number-association dilemma, which denies that puruṣa can coherently be called *one*, presupposing plurality as the baseline.)

### 7.2 Other Buddhist and Vedāntic doxography

- **Tattvasaṅgraha** *ātmaparīkṣā* (`SA_T11_tattvasangru:3814`): the one-and-many self it examines is the **Jaina (Digambara)** self, not Sāṃkhya.
- **Śaṅkara, Brahmasūtrabhāṣya** (`SA_GSP33_brssgo2u:79`): the only text that even poses "one or many puruṣas?" against Sāṃkhya — and it does so by **quoting the same Mokṣadharma verse** (*bahavaḥ puruṣā brahmann utāho eka eva tu …*), concluding that Kapila does **not** accept the single all-self, *ātma-bhedābhyupagamāt* ("because he assumes a plurality of selves").

## 8. Synthesis

Across six independent probes — the Kārikā commentaries, the Yuktidīpikā, Vijñānabhikṣu, the Mokṣadharma, Caraka, Pātañjala Yoga, and the Buddhist doxographies (with Bhāviveka's chapter read line-by-line) — the evidence converges:

1. **No named Sāṃkhya philosopher holds the singularity of puruṣa.** The view is reported only anonymously (*eke*, *kecid ācāryāḥ*, *kha cig*) or externalised to outsiders — Vedāntins, Aupaniṣadas, Advaitins, and "Vyāsa" in the theistic stratum.

2. **The Yuktidīpikā's named teachers** (Vārṣagaṇya, Vindhyavāsin, Pañcādhikaraṇa, Patañjali) disagree only about the evolutes, never puruṣa-number; the YD explicitly assigns the one-self to the *aupaniṣadāḥ*.

3. **Where a single puruṣa genuinely lives is the theistic / universal-self register** — the Mokṣadharma's *puruṣa virāṭ*, Caraka's *vibhu, nirviśeṣa paramātman*, Vijñānabhikṣu's *ādipuruṣa* Nārāyaṇa, and Pātañjala Yoga's *puruṣaviśeṣa* Īśvara.

4. **The decisive structural point (Yoga):** the supreme-puruṣa idea was fully available and was developed wherever the theistic impulse was strong — but every system that adopted it housed the supreme one *within* a plurality (a distinguished member), rather than reducing the many to one. That is precisely why a one-and-only-puruṣa Sāṃkhya never had a doctrinal home or a named proponent: the orthodox options were always "many puruṣas," or "many puruṣas with one supreme among them" — never "one puruṣa only."

**Conclusion.** The "Sāṃkhya philosophers who argued for the singularity of puruṣa" are a doxographic and theological category, not a documented set of historical individuals. The nearest historical reality is the pre-classical *universal-self* Sāṃkhya of Caraka — anonymous, collective ("ādya Sāṃkhya"), and framing its one puruṣa as a pervading *paramātman* rather than as a rival reading of SK 18.

## 9. Addendum: *pariṇāma* and the spheres of creation

A natural follow-up asks whether the *pariṇāma* — the universe arising from the *puruṣa–prakṛti saṃyoga* — was conceived such that one puruṣa corresponds to one universe. It was not. Three sub-investigations confirm that Sāṃkhya locates the individuation of the many puruṣas in the *interior* (subtle body, buddhi, experience), while keeping the cosmos *common*.

### 9.1 Tattvakaumudī: one prakṛti, one cosmos, per-puruṣa interiority

Vācaspati on SK 21 confirms the mechanism (*"from this union proceeds creation"*; `SA_GSP31_stk:3354–3395`), but the many-one relation is resolved without a per-puruṣa universe:

- **SK 62** (`:5294, 5305`): *"no Spirit is bound, none migrates, none released; it is **Nature alone, having many receptacles** (*anekāśrayā*), that is bound, migrates, released"* — one prakṛti individuated into many bodies, bondage figuratively ascribed to the puruṣas (king/soldiers simile).
- **SK 40** (`:4283, 4311`): the subtle body (*liṅgaśarīra*) evolves *"for each Spirit individually"*; Nārāyaṇa Tīrtha — *"an astral body is restricted to one particular Spirit."* The *liṅga* is per-puruṣa; the gross cosmos is shared.
- **SK 59 / 65–66** (`:5269, 5467`): liberation is individuated — the modest dancer (prakṛti) withdraws from the spectator who has seen, but performs on for the others (= Yogasūtra 2.22 *anya-sādhāraṇatvāt*).
- **SK 18** (`:3210, 3213`): explicitly refutes the unity of spirit (one spirit ⇒ one's birth/death affects all).

The *paṅgv-andha* (one lame + one blind) simile of SK 21 might suggest a one-to-one pairing, but Vācaspati does **not** draw it; he resolves the asymmetry via "one Nature with many receptacles" (SK 62) and the per-puruṣa *liṅga* (SK 40). "One universe per puruṣa" is a *third* option that classical Sāṃkhya declines — it neither reduces to one puruṣa nor pairs each with its own prakṛti.

### 9.2 Tattvavaiśāradī: ādhyātmika/ādhibhautika/ādhidaivika qualifies *suffering*, not *pariṇāma*

In the Yoga commentary the triad appears under **YS 2.15** (*pariṇāma-tāpa-saṃskāra-duḥkha*), classifying the three *tāpas* (`vacaspatimisra_tattvavaisaradi.txt:196`):
> *ādhyātmikaṃ śārīraṃ vyādhi-vaśān mānasaṃ ca kāmādi-vaśāt; **ādhibhautikaṃ** vyāghrādi-janitam; **ādhidaivikaṃ** graha-pīḍādi-janitam.*

and groups two as external (`:374`): *ādhibhautika-ādhidaivikayos tāpayor **bāhyatvenaikatvaṃ** vivakṣitam* — the *bāhya* (object + cosmic) vs *adhyātma* (self) split. There is **no** threefold *pariṇāma* (sphere/deity scheme) in the Tattvavaiśāradī or the Yogabhāṣya; the term *pariṇāma* in YS 2.15 names one *kind of suffering* (the suffering of change), and the *ādhi-x* triad classifies the *tāpa*.

### 9.3 Purāṇic Sāṃkhya: the genuine adhyātma/adhibhūta/adhidaiva *pariṇāma*

The sphere-scheme of the *transformation* — including the **presiding deities** — is a real doctrine, but it belongs to **Purāṇic Sāṃkhya's three-ahaṃkāra cosmogony**. Mārkaṇḍeya-purāṇa 45.47–50 (`3_purana/unknown_markandeyapurana_adhyaya-1-93_u.txt:6422`):
> *bhūta-tanmātra-sargo 'yam ahaṃkārāt tu **tāmasāt** … vaikārikād ahaṃkārāt … **sāttvikāt** … buddhīndriyāṇi … **taijasānīndriyāṇy āhur devā vaikārikā daśa** / ekādaśaṃ manas tatra **devā vaikārikāḥ smṛtāḥ***

| Sphere | ahaṃkāra | product |
|---|---|---|
| ādhibhautika | *tāmasa* (bhūtādi) | tanmātras → five elements |
| ādhyātmika | *taijasa* (rājasa) | the ten *indriyas* (+ manas) |
| ādhidaivika | *vaikārika* (sāttvika) | the ten presiding **deities** |

Deity list, Bhāgavata 2.5.30 (`3_purana/unknown_bhagavatapurana_skandha-02_u.txt:410`): *devā vaikārikā daśa: dig-vāta-arka-praceto-'śvi-vahni-indra-upendra-mitrakāḥ* (Directions, Wind, Sun, Varuṇa, Aśvins, Fire, Indra, Upendra, Mitra, Prajāpati). Parallels: Kūrma 1.4.22, Liṅga 1, Saura.

This differs from the **Kārikā Sāṃkhya** (Gauḍapāda/Māṭhara ad SK 25, `gaudapada_samkhya-bhasya_sharma-ed.txt:357–358`; `isvarakrsna_samkhyakarika_w_mathara-vrtti.txt:556`), where the *indriyas* come from the *sāttvika/vaikṛta* ahaṃkāra and there is **no separate deity-line**. The Purāṇa splits that sāttvika line off as the *deities* and derives the organs from *taijasa* — thereby generating the full triad.

**Bearing on the thesis.** The *ādhibhautika* (elements/objects) and *ādhidaivika* (presiding deities) spheres are **cosmic and common** — one shared world, one Sun, one Indra — for all puruṣas; only the *ādhyātmika* (organ/buddhi/liṅga) sphere is per-individual. This is the cosmological elaboration of Yogasūtra 4.16 (*object common to all puruṣas, minds one-per-puruṣa*), and the strongest reason the *pariṇāma* was never read as "one universe per puruṣa": the *daiva* and *bhauta* spheres are unitary and shared; multiplicity lives only on the *adhyātma* side.
